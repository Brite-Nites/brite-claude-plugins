## Research grounding (2026-04-21 — pre-Tier-A)

Deep-research agent findings informing every answer below.

**Actual competitive landscape (not S4 / MK Illumination as I had assumed):**
- **Tianyu Arts & Culture** — dominant incumbent. Chinese-lantern festival producers, turnkey design-build-staff + rev-share / seasonal lease. Clients: Tulsa Zoo, Potawatomi Zoo, Maryland Zoo, Memphis Zoo, Saint Louis Zoo (Animals Aglow), Nashville Zoo (Zoolumination), Dallas Zoo (Illuminature), Columbus Zoo, Woodland Park (WildLanterns), Franklin Park (Boston Lights). Production cost ~$400K–$1M+ per season (inferred from Oakland Glowfari $100K tariff hit).
- **Illuminight Lighting** (Chicago) — 22 years, Lincoln Park ZooLights vendor of record. Mid-market commercial LED strings + animated sculptures + arches/pole trees. No projection or narrative design.
- **Moment Factory / Limelight Art / Christie Digital** — projection-mapping + immersive studios. Singapore Rainforest Lumina, OdySea Aquarium 360° lobby. High six- to low seven-figure per installation. IALD-award territory.
- **LED-retrofit vendors** (Goodlight UK, Orphek, EcoMedia) — back-of-house habitat lighting. Different buyer, different budget cycle. Adjacent, not competitive.

**Brite Labs capability gap (honest):** Brite Nites gallery shows residential + commercial (Google, Adobe, City Creek) in UT/TX/CA. Zero indexed zoo/aquarium work. Going head-on against Tianyu for a full-venue contract is loss even if Brite wins — delivery risk too high. Entry must be a wedge.

**Real buyer motivations (from CEO statements, annual reports, economic-impact studies):**
- Winter-trough revenue (Jan–Feb attendance hole)
- Net-new tourism / hotel-room-nights (Denver Zoo: 85.1% net-new economic activity, 16,502 hotel-room-nights/year)
- Membership acquisition funnel (LA Zoo: 170K event attendees, 70% over goal)
- Corporate sponsorship inventory — named title sponsors are 5-7 figure commitments (CPS Energy, TXU Energy, Invesco QQQ, ComEd, Meijer, LES)
- F&B + merch per-cap uplift
- Mission/education/conservation framing (required for board-level sell)

**Typical program economics:**
- Outdoor zoo lights: $18–$31 adult, $15–$22 child, 30 nights, Nov–Jan (or Jul–Sep for Cleveland)
- Premium lantern (Tianyu): $22–$30 adult
- Aquarium 21+ after-hours: **$50–$60 adult, 3.5 hours, includes food + meet-and-greets** (Newport $59.99 verified)
- Attendance ceiling observed: 170K (LA Zoo month), 105K (Cleveland summer), 77K (Lincoln Children's winter)
- Program longevity: Columbus 37 years, Lincoln Park 30 years, Cleveland 1M+ cumulative

**Good vs bad program taxonomy (from TripAdvisor + trade press):**
- Static vs dynamic (uplit trees = bad; animation/kinetics/narrative = good)
- Crowd flow (one-way = good; two-way = disaster)
- Value ceiling ~$100 family-of-4
- Animal welfare integration communicated upfront (Dallas Illuminature, Columbus, Nashville Zoolumination all do this well)
- Year-over-year refresh > raw scale (longevity = reason)

**Aquariums are structurally different from zoos:**
- Indoor, climate-controlled, smaller footprint. Can't do million-LED outdoor trails.
- Analog: 21+ after-hours themed events, summer Glow Nights (bioluminescence-native), holiday overlays (Georgia Aquarium 40-ft tree + twinkle overlay).
- Thinner incumbent vendor set. Lanterns don't work in humid tank galleries.
- Brite's actual capability (architectural commercial LED, color-controllable) is a better match here than for outdoor zoos.

**Three wedge-offer candidates (research-derived):**
1. **"$25K Pilot Zone"** — specialist add-on for venues that already have an incumbent lighting vendor. Install-at-cost for case-study footage rights. Photo-share benchmark guarantee.
2. **"Permanent Backbone, Seasonal Dress"** — year-round architectural on gateway/plaza that doubles as seasonal entry. Facilities-VP buyer, subscription model.
3. **"Aquarium Glow-Native"** — bioluminescence-accurate LED/UV overlay for 21+ after-hours aquarium events. Revenue-share fronts install cost. Research agent's pick as the one Brite can actually win.

**Structural flag for planning:** Zoos and aquariums are different enough that a single combined preset may not fit. The manifest table currently groups them. Splitting into two separate verticals (`zoos` + `aquariums`) is worth considering — Offer C is aquarium-only, Offer A is zoo-biased.

### A1. Offer / motion — ANSWERED 2026-04-21
**Decision:** Offer E — Production Finance. Brite orchestrates multi-stream funding (title sponsor + city economic-dev + zoo-side sponsors + ad revenue on DMX-pixel displays), sources hardware from S4 / similar as supply chain, designs the composition + ad stack, runs the show, takes revenue-share on net. Venue puts up near-zero capital.

**Hormozi mapping:**
- Dream Outcome → seasonal program that makes the zoo money (not costs it); attendance + sponsor inventory + ad revenue + city partnership all built in
- Perceived Likelihood → proven by pipeline (sponsor pledges + city letter + ad pre-commits before install)
- Time Delay → 6-9 mo venue-commit to program-live; year-2 renewal default upside
- Effort + Sacrifice → venue near-zero capital, near-zero execution risk, operations limited to hosting

**Why this beats Offers A/B/C/D:**
- Capital from Brite → near-zero (removes balance-sheet question the earlier capability analysis surfaced)
- Venue gets net-positive-revenue partnership, not expense line
- Tianyu can't match: lanterns are static, can't carry ad inventory, vendor-owns-IP model means venue can't monetize
- Brite's edge isn't fabrication; it's production-finance orchestration + ad-monetized programmable-media infrastructure

**Tech stack anchor:** DMX-pixel light curtains + pixel trees (sourced from S4 Lights or equivalent component supplier), pixel-mapped content including ad inventory. Programmable = ad-compatible; static lanterns are not. That's the technical differentiator Brite owns.

**Funding channels (the three Brite raises from):**
1. Title / presenting sponsor — precedent: Invesco QQQ + ComEd at Lincoln Park, Meijer at Cleveland, CPS Energy at San Antonio. 5-7 figure per sponsor.
2. City / economic-dev / destination-marketing bureau — precedent: Denver Zoo economic-impact study showed 85.1% net-new economic activity, 16,502 hotel-room-nights/yr.
3. Zoo-side corporate-donor pipeline — zoos already have development teams chasing these; Brite co-pitches.
4. (Ongoing, not year-1 capital) Ad inventory on displays — branded light moments, sponsor logo animations, scrolling content, brand-sync shows.

**Open risks flagged (not dealbreakers, but real):**
- Sponsor-sales capability: Brite needs to demonstrate it can close sponsors, not just install lights
- City economic-dev funding cycles are slow + political (6-18 mo); year-1 may not have this unlock
- Ad revenue on pixel installations is unproven at scale at zoos (stadium jumbotron + mall digital signage as analog)
- First venue is hardest (trust earned through relationships, not cold outbound); outbound email has to carry this complexity without overwhelming
- Multi-revenue-stream rev-share accounting requires infrastructure (ticketing integration, sponsor accounting, ad reconciliation)

## Offer-by-offer review (Hormozi / Brunson / Abraham frameworks applied)

### Offer A — Pilot Zone — KEEP AS TACTICAL COMPLEMENT 2026-04-21

**Decision:** Keep but not as primary. Use when prospect research shows a long-tenured incumbent lighting vendor + decision-maker open to low-commitment test.

**Fixes required before composition:**
1. **Sharpen frontend deliverable.** "Pilot-zone concept" → "rendered concept board (24"×36") for [specific area at venue]". Tangible artifact, not visualization-required promise.
2. **Swap backend guarantee metric.** "Photo-share benchmark" is gimmicky + disconnected from venue success. Replace with venue-native metric: **dwell time in the pilot zone** (sensor / head-count), **attendance uplift in programmed hours** (gate-scan data), or **sponsor-visibility impressions** (if sponsor brand on pilot zone).
3. **Reframe commercial structure.** "Install-at-cost for case-study footage rights" sounds supplicant. Rephrase: "We'll underwrite the install cost. In exchange, we use the results — footage, metrics, venue name — in our sales material." Confident, not asking a favor.

**Why keep:** narrow but real use case — venue with entrenched incumbent + open-to-test decision-maker. Different sales motion from Offer E (specialist add-on vs whole-program partnership).

**Don't lead outbound with it.** E primary, A tactical.
