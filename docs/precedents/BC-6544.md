## Task 1 — Plan-gate scope-expansion when issue-enumerated sites are a strict subset of grep-discoverable matches

**Date:** 2026-05-01
**Category:** pattern-application
**Confidence:** 7/10
**Tags:** pattern-application, plan-gate, scope-expansion, internal-doc-consistency, grep-discoverable

### Decision

When the issue body enumerates N sites for a "same misleading-pattern" fix and a quick `grep` against the changed files reveals N+M sites carrying the identical misleading framing, surface the scope choice at plan-gate as `(a) approve the full N+M-site scope`, `(b) narrow to issue-enumerated N sites only`, or `(c) approve the full scope plus a strengthening tweak`. Recommend (a) when internal-doc consistency is the load-bearing rationale (sites M would drift if left and the same author would have to reopen the file later anyway). Don't silently expand scope past the issue body, and don't silently narrow scope to issue-enumerated sites when the M extra sites are the same one-line wording fix.

For BC-6544: issue body enumerated 2 sites (`launch-campaign.md` line 545 + `email-bison.md` row 2a). A `grep -n "idempotent"` revealed 4 sites with the same misleading framing — the issue-enumerated 2 plus `launch-campaign.md` line 548 (Phase 5 fail-mid-loop) and `email-bison.md` line 271 (Known gotchas plain_text bullet). Plan surfaced all 3 options; user picked (a) "Approve all 4 sites (Recommended)" with rationale "internal-doc consistency".

### Alternatives Considered

1. **Approve all 4 sites in one PR** *(chosen)* — keeps internal-doc consistency. Sites 2 and 4 would drift in a narrow ship and require a follow-up PR within the same chain; bundling is cheaper.
2. **Narrow to issue-enumerated 2 sites only** — rejected. Would leave sites 2 and 4 carrying the same misleading framing the rest of the chain just spent a PR fixing; future spec-readers would hit the foot-gun in either of the unfixed surfaces.
3. **Approve all 4 + cross-reference site 4 with stronger inline foot-gun call (preview option)** — rejected as polish-overhead. The site-4 cross-reference already names BC-6544; the row-2a anchor is the canonical-reference and operators reading the gotcha bullet routinely cross-read row 2a anyway. Marginal value didn't justify the extra wording.
