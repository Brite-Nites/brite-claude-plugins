## BC-5919 — 2026-04-22 — Terminal-anchor placement for cross-reference pointers: insert at end-of-subsection (after last content line, before next heading), not at top

**Category:** pattern-choice
**Confidence:** 6/10

**Inputs:**

- `plugins/marketing/skills/email-copywriting/SKILL.md` §§ 3-4 as they stood pre-edit — 3 subsections where a one-line pointer could land at the top (preamble) or the bottom (terminal anchor)
- BC-5918 precedent (docs-only execution collapse) — single-pass authoring favors whole-file prose coherence over mechanical placement rules

**Alternatives Considered:**
1. **CHOSEN.** Insert each pointer as a standalone paragraph at the *end* of the target subsection — after the last content line and blank line, immediately before the next `### ...` heading. For §4 Cross-skill boundaries (a bulleted subsection), append the pointer as the last bullet after "Hands off to:". Reason: terminal anchors hit the reader *after* they've absorbed the inline framework content, acting as a "for more, see X" exit ramp rather than a pre-read distraction. Preserves the reading flow authors already designed. Also makes future pointer additions mechanically obvious (always append).
2. Insert at the top of each subsection as a preamble. Rejected — forces every reader to route through the pointer before reaching the inline content the skill already teaches, defeating the self-contained skill-body principle (D3 lazy-load pattern: inline content is load-bearing, references are supplemental).
3. Inline the pointer into the existing prose (e.g. "…per D2. (See `offer-design-frameworks.md` for the source framework.)"). Rejected — prose insertion requires fiddling with sentence grammar and risks the pointer being missed on a skim. Standalone paragraph is more grep-friendly and visible.
4. Insert at the top of §3 Methodology as a single consolidated "See also" block covering all three references. Rejected — divorces each pointer from its thematic anchor subsection, making it harder for a reader deep in a specific subsection to find the right reference.
