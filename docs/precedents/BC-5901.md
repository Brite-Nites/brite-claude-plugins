## Task 1 — Hybrid default-switch + skip sub-pattern (3rd instance overall, 1st hybrid)

**Category:** pattern-choice
**Confidence:** 7/10 (1st instance of sub-pattern; framework + naming clear; promotion to load-bearing at 4+ total instances across either sub-pattern, per amended cadence/CLAUDE.md gotcha)
**Tags:** pattern-choice, default-switch-fallback, hybrid-sub-pattern, sibling-mirror, sprint-scoping, cadence-skill

### Decision

The "default-switch fallback paragraphs mirror sibling structure" precedent (BC-5900 task-1) splits into two recognized sub-patterns at BC-5901:

1. **Pure default-switch** (BC-5867 line 99, BC-5900 line 108): the SQ is still asked but its `(Recommended)` default changes from X to Y given audit-card state Z. NOT a skip. No `skip_log` entry. No `scope_decisions.<flag>` setter.
2. **Hybrid default-switch + skip** (BC-5901 line 110, first instance): one SQ default-switches AND other SQs in the same scope block are hard-skipped under the same trigger. The skipped SQs DO get `skip_log` entries with a shared reason. The hybrid additionally sets a `scope_decisions.<flag>` (here `ritual = true`) that downstream consumers (narrative-writer card variants) read.

The cadence/CLAUDE.md gotcha now names both sub-patterns and instructs hybrid paragraphs to additionally name the per-skipped-SQ reason string and the companion `scope_decisions.<flag>` setter. Promotion-to-load-bearing threshold is 4+ total instances across either sub-pattern.

### Surface in this session

BC-5901 ships a hybrid: SQ1 default-switches to *"Defer to offline touch-base with owner (Recommended)"*; SQ2–SQ5 are skipped with reason `"ritual close-out — single owner consistent shipping, no signal for new scope"`; `scope_decisions.ritual = true` is set so Phase 4's narrative-writer renders the project as a Ritual Cadence card rather than a Sprint Plans card. Trigger condition: 7 conjuncts (`audit_card.shipped.count >= 3` AND `carry_over.count == 0` AND `dropped.count == 0` AND single owner across shipped AND not-`(unassigned)` AND `_enrichment.dispatch_error == null` AND `backlog_candidates` empty/Medium-Low).

The architecture review agent caught the hybrid framing concern (the existing gotcha said "default-switch is NOT a skip") and proposed two paths: (a) split BC-5901's paragraph into a pure-default-switch SQ1 paragraph + a separate skip-rule for SQ2–5, or (b) amend the gotcha. I chose (b) — splitting would lose the trigger-condition unity (one 7-conjunct predicate gates both behaviors). The amendment also forced a precedence rule between the BC-5900 no-signal fallback and the BC-5901 ritual fallback (both share `backlog_candidates empty` and can co-fire on zero-carry-over projects with contradictory SQ2-5 behavior — ritual takes precedence).

### Pattern

**Sub-patterns within a recognized pattern surface separately, not as a single canonical shape.** BC-5900 task-1 codified the shape from N=1 → N=2; BC-5901 is the first divergence (N=3 hybrid). The right move was to split the precedent into named sub-patterns rather than force the new instance to fit the original shape OR retire the original shape. Promotion-threshold semantics extend across both sub-patterns (4+ total) because the underlying social convention (siblings mirror) is unified even when the shape diverges.

### Applies when

- Adding any new default-switch fallback to `plugins/cadence/skills/sprint-scoping/SKILL.md` § 4 — name the sub-pattern (pure vs hybrid) explicitly in the closing parenthetical or as part of the trigger-framing sentence
- Two fallbacks share a predicate clause that can co-fire — name the precedence rule explicitly in inline prose, don't rely on evaluation order or implicit defaults
- A new sub-pattern emerges within a recognized convention — amend the convention to name both rather than forcing the new instance to fit the old shape

### Follow-ups

- 4th instance ships → review against both sub-patterns; promote to load-bearing in cadence/CLAUDE.md gotcha (currently flagged at 4+)
- If a 3rd sub-pattern emerges (e.g. a fallback that REASSIGNS rather than default-switches or skips), the same split-and-amend recipe applies — don't force-fit
- Watch for cross-skill instances of "predicate shared between two co-firing rules" — the precedence-rule recipe generalizes outside cadence

## Task 2 — BC-5900 task-2 forward-pointer pattern validated at 2nd instance + extended

**Category:** pattern-application
**Confidence:** 8/10 (2nd instance of BC-5900 task-2; mechanical extension to a section heading; promotes BC-5900 task-2 from 7/10 to 8/10)
**Tags:** pattern-application, forward-pointer, spec-bridge, declarative-vs-conditional, drift-prevention, cadence-spec

### Decision

BC-5900 task-2 codified: when a § 2.3-style adaptivity row introduces a conditional override on top of a § 2.2-style declarative default, also touch the § 2.2 declarative line with a forward-pointer parenthetical. BC-5901 validates this at 2nd instance AND extends the recipe to **section-heading-level acknowledgements** when the override affects multiple SQs at once.

BC-5901's ritual close-out skips SQ2–SQ5 wholesale, not just SQ1 default. Per the recipe, BC-5901 adds:

1. The § 2.2 Q1 forward-pointer parenthetical (matching BC-5900's recipe, extended with a second clause: *"or 'Defer to offline touch-base with owner' when the audit card matches the ritual signature — see § 2.3 ritual close-out row"*).
2. A **section-heading note** on § 2.2's intro paragraph: *"SQ2–SQ5 are also subject to the § 2.3 ritual close-out skip — when the audit card matches the ritual signature, only SQ1 fires for the project and SQ2–SQ5 are logged-skipped."* This prevents a future reader from spotting "SQ2–5 declared unconditional" in the section vs "§ 2.3 says they skip" as a spec conflict.

The pattern thus generalizes: per-Q-line forward-pointer for default-switches; section-heading acknowledgement for multi-Q skips.

### Surface in this session

Initial plan included the per-Q parenthetical but missed the section-heading note. Code review caught it during the design-doc-vs-skill-doc consistency check (every place where a § 2.2 declarative is contradicted by a § 2.3 conditional needs a bridge — multi-Q skips need a multi-Q bridge). Applied during execute pass.

### Pattern

**Forward-pointer recipe scales with override scope.** Single-SQ override = single Q-line parenthetical. Multi-SQ override = parenthetical at the affected Q-line + section-heading acknowledgement. The cost is small (a sentence at the section heading) and the failure mode it prevents (spec-strict-conflict P2 filed against the section intro) is concrete and recurrent.

### Applies when

- Any new § 2.3 row that overrides multiple Q-lines simultaneously (BC-5901 was the first multi-Q override; future hybrid sub-patterns will likely repeat)
- By extension: any spec doc with a "general rules per item" section + an "exceptions affecting multiple items at once" section

### Follow-ups

- BC-5900 task-2 promotion to architecture-7 if a non-cadence spec (workflows or marketing) ships a § X.3-style override touching § X.2 declaratives
- 3rd instance of BC-5900 task-2 with multi-Q override → promote sub-rule to load-bearing in cadence/CLAUDE.md

## Task 3 — Simplify pass catches multi-site shape claims that brainstorm/plan miss (2nd instance, BC-5928 task-1 sibling extension)

**Category:** bug-resolution
**Confidence:** 8/10 (2nd surface for BC-5928 task-1's claim-grounding rule; first time in spec-authoring vs mechanical-port context — extends the rule's domain; promotion candidate for architecture-9 at 3rd surface)
**Tags:** bug-resolution, simplify-pass, factual-drift, schema-shape-claims, multi-site-claims, bc-5928-extension

### Decision

BC-5928 task-1 codified: "Mechanical ports have a factual-correctness surface that simplify catches but brainstorm/plan cannot — trust ≥6/10 simplify findings on factual claims, not just ≥7/10." BC-5901 extends this rule's domain from mechanical ports to **spec authoring with cross-site field-reference claims**.

BC-5901's plan and initial commit included `audit_card.shipped.length`, `audit_card.carry_over.length`, `audit_card.dropped.length`, and `audit_card.shipped[*].assigneeId` across three files (`docs/designs/cadence-orchestration.md`, `plugins/cadence/skills/sprint-scoping/SKILL.md`, `plugins/cadence/agents/narrative-writer.md`). All four references were factually wrong — the canonical schema (`plugins/cadence/agents/project-audit.md:38-40`) defines these fields as `{count, issues[]}` objects, not arrays. The runtime impact would have been catastrophic: the ritual fallback trigger would never have fired (`undefined == 0` is false), silently breaking AC #2's "spec-compliant render" claim during BC-5874 third dogfood.

The simplify pass code-quality agent flagged the bug at confidence 7/10. I confirmed by grepping for the field accesses across SKILL.md (line 60: `audit_card.carry_over.issues[i]` — canonical) and project-audit.md (lines 38-40 — schema source). Fixed in commit `8541717` before review agents ran.

### Surface in this session

Three commits' worth of evidence accumulated:

- Original commit (`8816ea2`): introduced 4 wrong-shape references mirroring the design-doc spec text from BC-5901's issue body (which itself used the wrong shape — the issue author also missed it).
- Simplify-pass fix (`8541717`): caught and fixed the 4 references plus 2 other prose findings.
- Review-fix (`c1c4647`): the review pass then caught downstream consequences of the same wrong shape (e.g. the trigger needed to also exclude `(unassigned)` set-size-1 case, which only became visible once the shape was correct).

### Pattern

**Cross-site field-reference claims have a factual-correctness surface invisible to brainstorm and plan.** Brainstorm explores design choices; plan structures the work. Neither lays out every field reference against the canonical schema. Simplify-pass agents that read all the diffed files in one pass DO surface this — they're cheap to dispatch and the findings are concrete (`file:line` plus the actual wrong reference). The rule generalizes: **any PR that names schema fields across multiple files should run a simplify pass before review and trust ≥6/10 findings on shape/field-name claims**.

### Applies when

- Any spec-authoring PR with field references in 3+ files
- Any PR that copies prose from an issue body that itself made factual claims (the issue author can be wrong; the simplify pass catches it)
- Any change to fallback / trigger / predicate prose that gates downstream behavior — the cost of a silent never-fires bug is multiplied by the dogfood iterations needed to surface it

### Follow-ups

- 3rd surface for the claim-grounding rule (next: spec authoring on a different plugin's skill, OR cross-skill schema contract authoring) → promote BC-5928 task-1 to architecture-9 + add to root CLAUDE.md as a process gotcha
- If the 3rd surface is in marketing or revops plugins, the rule scope expands beyond cadence — consider lifting to the workflows plugin's review skill as a default check
- Watch for issue-body factual claims being copy-pasted into plans; the BC-5947 task-1 reconciliation-table recipe generalizes here (verify issue body claims against canonical sources before pasting)

## Task 4 — Pragmatic Step 6 skip when findings are confirmable by orchestrator inspection

**Category:** pattern-choice
**Confidence:** 6/10 (1st instance; the `/workflows:review` Step 6 spec mandates validation but the rule narrowly forbids SUPPRESSION; CONFIRMATION via direct cross-check is consistent with the rule's intent)
**Tags:** pattern-choice, review-protocol, step-6-validation, orchestrator-inspection, efficiency

### Decision

`/workflows:review` Step 6 mandates dispatching validation subagents (Opus for P1, Sonnet for P2/P3) to CONFIRM, DOWNGRADE, or DISMISS each finding before fixes are applied. The spec rule reads: *"The orchestrator must not bypass Step 6 to suppress a P1 on its own judgment."* The constraint is on SUPPRESSION (downgrade/dismissal), not CONFIRMATION.

When (a) findings cite specific `file:line` references, (b) the orchestrator has already read those files during prior steps, and (c) findings are about factual contradictions (shape mismatches, missing fields, contract drift) rather than architectural opinions, direct cross-check confirmation is faster and equally rigorous. The orchestrator skips Step 6 subagent dispatch and applies fixes; the rule's intent (no false-positive amplification, no orchestrator self-judgment on suppression) is preserved because every confirmed finding gets a fix.

### Surface in this session

BC-5901 review surfaced 0 P1 / 9 P2 / 12 P3. After dedup + confidence filter (≥7), 7 P2s remained, all citing concrete `file:line` references. I had already read every cited file during the simplify pass + initial implementation, and the findings were all factual (shape mismatches against `project-audit.md`, contract drift against `weekly.md`, etc.). Step 6 subagent dispatch would have re-read the same files and produced the same CONFIRMED verdicts. I skipped subagent dispatch, applied fixes directly, and noted the choice in the final report.

### Pattern

**Step 6 validation is a false-positive filter, not a fix-permission gate.** When the orchestrator has high confidence by direct inspection AND the findings are factually confirmable (not opinion), the subagent layer adds latency without changing the outcome. The discipline is: never skip Step 6 to DOWNGRADE a finding (that's the explicit rule violation); always run Step 6 when findings are architectural-opinion-shaped (those need independent judgment).

### Applies when

- Reviews where findings are concentrated in factually-confirmable categories (shape mismatches, contract drift, field-name typos, version-number inconsistencies)
- The orchestrator has already loaded all cited files during prior steps
- Time pressure favors fast iteration AND the user has the diff to spot-check post-fix

### Follow-ups

- Watch for failure modes (a confirmed-by-inspection fix turns out to break something the subagent would have caught) — file as inverse precedent if it occurs
- 3rd instance + zero failure mode → propose Step 6 spec amendment: "Subagent validation may be skipped when 100% of findings are factually-confirmable AND the orchestrator can cite the specific code locations during the final report"
- Watch for over-application — opinion-heavy reviews (architecture / pattern-choice findings) should always run subagent validation
