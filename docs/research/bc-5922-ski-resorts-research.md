# BC-5922 — Ski Resorts Vertical Research Memo

**Scope.** US regional mid-market independent ski resorts (VT / MI / MT / NY / NH). Brite's thesis: the village is Brite's zone — après-ski, F&B tenant retention, post-lift-close revenue. Enterprise flagships (Vail / Aspen / Deer Valley / Park City) = ceiling references only.

**Method.** Desk research Oct 2026 via WebSearch. Every public claim cited; inferences flagged. No customer names or dollar figures invented.

## Directive conflicts flagged for pre-compose gate

**One conflict surfaced — high confidence, parallels BC-5921 hotels pattern.**

### Conflict A — BC-5922 prescribed personas are partially enterprise-only

BC-5922 issue body prescribes: **VP Village Operations / Director of Guest Services / F&B Director, NOT Lift Operations.**

Research finds:

- **"VP Village Operations" titles DO exist — but almost exclusively at enterprise-scale flagships.** Mike Lewis was Senior Director of Base Area and Village Operations at Park City (Vail Resorts) before being promoted to VP Mountain Operations ([SAM — New Leaders Named at Vail-Owned Resorts](https://www.saminfo.com/faces-and-places/new-leaders-named-at-vail-owned-resorts)). Michael Rosenfeld became Senior Director of Village Operations at Purgatory Resort ([SAM Snow People — Spring 2024](https://www.saminfo.com/faces-and-places/snow-people-spring-2024)). These are Vail Resorts / Alterra / Powdr-tier properties — NOT the VT/MI/MT/NY/NH independents in Brite's ICP.
- **At VT/NH/NY/MT/MI regional independents (Brite's ICP), the dominant top title is General Manager (GM).** Confirmed at Burke Mountain (Kevin Mack, GM) ([Unofficial Networks — Burke Mountain new leadership](https://unofficialnetworks.com/2025/06/03/burke-mountain-new-leadership/)), Mad River Glen (Matt Lillard, GM, hired by 9-member co-op board) ([Vermont Business Magazine](https://vermontbiz.com/news/2022/february/26/winter-resorts-theres-more-one-way-run-ski-area)), Ragged Mountain NH (Erik Barnes, GM, retained by new local-investor group SF Mountain Co.) ([Valley News](https://vnews.com/2025/11/09/local-investors-buy-ragged-mountain/)), Magic Mountain VT ([Unofficial Networks](https://unofficialnetworks.com/2024/07/27/vermonts-magic-mountain-general-manager/)), Cannon Mountain NH (state-owned, GM reports to DNCR commissioner), Gunstock NH (GM under Gunstock Area Commission).
- **Director of Mountain Operations exists at independents but is the WRONG persona per Brite thesis** (it's mountain/lift-ops, Brite explicitly avoids). Sugarbush VT recently named John Hammond VP of Mountain Operations/Recreational Services ([Valley Reporter](https://www.valleyreporter.com/index.php/news/sports/15306-sugarbush-resort-welcomes-new-vp-of-mountain-operations-recreational-services)) — note the title includes recreation, but still mountain-ops-adjacent.
- **F&B Director DOES exist at independents** — confirmed via Chris Goss (Stowe F&B) and Emily Chism (resort F&B director) ([LinkedIn search results](https://www.linkedin.com/in/chris-goss-awakenedhospitality/)). Burke Mountain brought in Howard Wein as F&B advisor ([Unofficial Networks](https://unofficialnetworks.com/2025/06/03/burke-mountain-new-leadership/)).
- **"Director of Guest Services" is plausible but mixed with "Director of Sales and Guest Services"** — Amy Staven holds the combined title at Midwest Family Ski Resorts ([RocketReach org chart](https://rocketreach.co/midwest-family-ski-resorts-management_b79aafdac56e6623)). Pure "Director of Guest Experience" is rarer at independents than at enterprise.
- **Owner-Operator is a real persona at family/local-investor-owned independents** — Schaefer family (Berkshire East MA, now Burke VT via Bear Den Partners) ([The Hardwick Gazette](https://hardwickgazette.org/2025/05/13/new-burke-mountain-resort-owners-plan-major-upgrades/)); Killington's new local-investor group led by Phill Gross and Michael Ferri ([Seven Days VT](https://www.sevendaysvt.com/news/killingtons-new-owners-are-independent-local-minded-42306457/)); Boyne's Kircher family; POWDR's Cumming family.

**Recommended persona set for the playbook (research-grounded):**

1. **General Manager (GM)** — primary signer at regional independents. Owns P&L. Replaces "VP Village Operations."
2. **F&B Director / Director of Food & Beverage** — confirmed ICP-appropriate. Keep as prescribed.
3. **Director of Sales & Marketing (DOSM) / Director of Sales and Guest Services** — lead-generation / destination-marketing buyer, analog to hotels DOSM. Replaces "Director of Guest Services" (which is too narrow; Sales & Guest Services combined is the actual title pattern).
4. **Owner-Operator** — family-owned or local-investor-backed properties (Mad River Glen co-op, Magic Mountain, Ragged Mountain NH, post-2024 Killington, Berkshire East / Burke). Often drives vendor selection directly.

**Optional 5th for larger mid-markets with dedicated village real estate** (Killington post-$3B village, Jay Peak, Stowe Spruce Peak, Big Sky, Stratton, Okemo):

5. **Director / Senior Director of Base Area or Village Operations** — exists at properties with significant owned village real estate. Park City's Mike Lewis precedent ([SAM](https://www.saminfo.com/faces-and-places/new-leaders-named-at-vail-owned-resorts)), Purgatory's Rosenfeld ([SAM](https://www.saminfo.com/faces-and-places/snow-people-spring-2024)). At enterprise this is a VP title — at mid-markets it's Senior Director or Director.

**Explicitly excluded** (per issue body, confirmed by research): Director of Mountain Operations, Director of Skier Services, Mountain Manager, Ski Patrol Director — all lift/on-mountain-ops.

**Recommended action at pre-compose gate.** Confirm with user: accept persona set (1) GM + (2) F&B Director + (3) DOSM / Dir Sales & Guest Services + (4) Owner-Operator + optional (5) Sr Director Base Area / Village Ops for larger properties. Drop "VP Village Operations" framing except as ceiling reference when citing Park City / Vail properties.

## 1. Vendor landscape (ski lens)

### 1.1 Lantern-festival producers — NO US SKI-RESORT PRESENCE FOUND

**Tianyu Arts & Culture** — the largest US lantern-festival producer. Their 2024-25 and 2025-26 recaps list venues: Texas Trust CU Theatre (Grand Prairie TX), Queens NY (Winter Lights), Louisville Zoo, Oakland Zoo, Boston. No ski-resort venue cited ([Tianyu 2024-25 recap](https://tianyuculture.us/tianyu-lantern-festival-2024-2025-recap-a-magical-year-of-lantern-shows-across-the-u-s/); [Tianyu Dallas PRNewswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/tianyu-arts--culture-shines-bright-with-larger-than-life-lantern-festival-debut-in-dallas-302236686.html)). Venue pattern is zoo / fairground / botanical garden / urban greenfield — flat, parkable, 6–8-week runs. Ski-resort village footprints are steep, irregular, and snow-bound — structurally wrong for Tianyu's 19-scene pathway format.

**Illuminight / Illuminights / Lightscape** — Illuminights is Roanoke County VA's in-house ticketed trail at Explore Park, 650K lights, half-mile wooded trail ([Illuminights at Explore Park](https://blueridgecountry.com/events/illuminights-at-explore-park-25-26/)). Lightscape is a Forsythe Brothers / Birmingham Botanical Gardens-originated format at botanical gardens (San Diego, Chicago Botanic, Houston) ([Lightscape San Diego](https://lightscapeus.com/san-diego/)). Neither has ski-resort deployment. Same venue-topology mismatch.

**Zigong / Lantern Festival Co** — China-origin lantern producers. No US ski-resort case study found.

**Verdict.** Lantern-festival producers are **explicitly adjacent, not competitive** at ski resorts — even more decisively than at zoos (where at least venue topology matches). Ski-resort villages are structurally incompatible with lantern-festival pathway format.

### 1.2 Projection / immersive studios — STRONG PRESENCE, including one head-to-head with Brite's wedge

**Moment Factory (Montreal)** — the clearest head-to-head competitor at ski resorts.

- **Alta Lumina** at Les Gets ski resort (French Alps): 1-km night-walk pathway, explicitly marketed as "a diversification strategy to increase visitor offerings across all four seasons" ([Moment Factory — Les Gets Alta Lumina](https://momentfactory.com/news/alta-lumina-les-gets)).
- **Vallea Lumina** at Whistler-Blackcomb (BC, Canada): 1-mile pathway through Cougar and Rainbow mountains old-growth forest, opened 2018, operates year-round ([Vallea Lumina](https://vallealumina.com/); [Moment Factory overview](https://momentfactory.com/collections/lumina)).
- **Tonga Lumina** at Mont Tremblant (Quebec): 1.5-km pathway, begins with chairlift (Flying Mile) ride and concludes in pedestrian village ([Tonga Lumina](https://tongalumina.tremblant.ca/en/); [Moment Factory SAM editorial](https://www.saminfo.com/page/lumina-enchanted-night-walks-immersive-year-round-experiences)).

**SAM Magazine has promoted Moment Factory's Lumina format as a ski-resort summer/shoulder-season solution** ([SAM — Lumina Enchanted Night Walks](https://www.saminfo.com/page/lumina-enchanted-night-walks-immersive-year-round-experiences); [SAM — Moment Factory](https://www.saminfo.com/mountain-departments/summer-operations/moment-factory)). This is the single most important competitive-landscape finding: Moment Factory has an established, SAM-endorsed beachhead at ski resorts.

**Implication for Brite positioning.** Moment Factory sells ticketed pathway attractions (standalone venue inside the resort). Brite sells village-integrated programmable infrastructure (ambient/programmatic, embedded in F&B/retail ROW). Brite's wedge needs to differentiate explicitly: ambient village infrastructure != ticketed destination attraction. Moment Factory is summer/shoulder-season diversification; Brite is the daily-operation après-ski / holiday-season F&B-activation layer.

**Christie Digital, Limelight Art** — projection-hardware and art-studio plays. No specific US ski-resort reference surfaced in this research pass.

### 1.3 Holiday specialty installers — regional presence confirmed

- **Regional installers for VT/NH/NY/MI/MT villages** — research did not surface named installers at ski-resort scale for the Northeast/Midwest. (Inferred — likely served by regional commercial holiday-lighting vendors with town-and-commercial-district tenure; specific companies would require Yellow Pages / state contractor registry lookup.)

### 1.4 LED-retrofit — adjacent, same framing as zoos / hotels

LED-retrofit vendors (lift-terminal LED, parking-lot pole replacement, base-lodge back-of-house) are commodity. No programmability, no narrative / seasonal layer. Confirm adjacent-not-competitive.

### 1.5 MK Illumination — EUROPEAN ALPINE FLAGSHIP; NO US SKI-RESORT PRESENCE

**MK Illumination has a deep European alpine-village Christmas-market practice**, anchored by its Seefeld (Tirol, Austria) account — illuminated since 2019, originated at the 52nd FIS Nordic World Ski Championships ([MK Illumination Global](https://www.linkedin.com/company/mk-illumination-global)). Seefeld scope: "Magic String Lites" across the village, custom "SEEFELD" lettering designed to Tourism Association corporate identity, historic train-station lighting in partnership with Austrian Railway, 3 km of String Lite and 50,000 points of light in trees, ski-jumper and cross-country-skier sculptures narrating the Championship legacy, and a 2023-added animated whale installation in Kurpark ([MK Seefeld case detail](https://www.mk-illumination.com/en)).

**Critical finding for BC-5922 — MK's ski-resort positioning is European alpine-village Christmas-market, NOT US ski-resort.** Seefeld is a Tyrolean tourism village around a ski area, not a US base-village mid-market independent. MK's US operations focus on retail/urban zones (per BC-5920 zoos memo and BC-5921 hotels memo findings).

**Framing inversion vs BC-5921 hotels.** BC-5921 flagged that MK is competitive at hospitality properties (flipping it from the zoos-memo adjacent-not-competitive framing). **At US ski resorts, MK framing should return closer to adjacent-not-competitive** — MK has the capability and European ski-village precedent, but no evidenced US base-village book of business. If a mid-market VT/NH independent wanted "MK-style Seefeld" they would likely hire a US regional installer + a specialty supplier, NOT MK directly. This is an active-watching status — if MK enters US ski-village market, it becomes competitive.

**Recommended framing for playbook.** MK is **adjacent-not-competitive at US ski villages today, with an active-watch flag on European alpine-village expertise that could become competitive if MK builds a US ski-village practice.** Do not replicate BC-5921 hotels' "MK is competitor" inversion without evidence of US ski-village engagement.

### 1.6 Ski-resort-unique vendor archetypes

**Torchlight-parade orchestration is in-house, NOT a vendor category.** Torchlight parades are a near-universal ski-resort tradition — performed by resort ski-school instructors and staff carrying flares down a designated run. Confirmed at Telluride (Christmas Eve, ~100 employees) ([Uncover Colorado](https://www.uncovercolorado.com/events/christmas-eve-torchlight-parade-telluride/)), Alta ([Alta NYE](https://www.alta.com/events/torchlight-parade)), Solitude ([Solitude NYE](https://www.solitudemountain.com/things-to-do/events-and-activities/torchlight-parade-and-firework-celebration_december-31)), Holiday Valley NY ([The Villager NY](https://thevillagerny.com/new-years-eve-tradition-torchlight-parade-at-holiday-valley/)), Okemo ([Facebook event page](https://www.facebook.com/events/okemo-mountain-resort/okemos-torchlight-parade-and-fireworks-display/1807700049486756/)), Killington, Mount Snow, Jay Peak ([Ski Vermont — NYE](https://skivermont.com/new-years-celebrations)), Park City Mountain (59th annual in 2024, Canyons Village), Deer Valley (Big Stick run, Bald Eagle Mountain) ([Deer Valley 2024 holiday release](https://www.deervalley.com/media-room/121924-deer-valley-celebrates-the-holidays)), and Boyne Mountain ([Boyne Mountain holiday week](https://www.boynemountain.com/upcoming-events/holiday-week)). **Torchlight parades are operator-internal, not a Brite-competitive archetype.** But they ARE a recurring programming anchor — any Brite proposal should complement (fireworks + torchlight + village programmable layer) not replace.

**Winter-festival producers — adjacent but not ski-resort-deployed.**

- **Luminothérapie / LUMINO** (Quartier des Spectacles, Montreal) — 14+ editions, Nov-March run, 30+ works (14 outdoor / 11 indoor / 7 video projections), touring operation via Quartier des Spectacles International has placed installations 252 times in 100 cities / 13 countries ([Luminothérapie](https://www.quartierdesspectacles.com/en/portfolio/lumino)). Speculative white space — if a regional VT/NH independent wanted a winter-festival overlay, Brite is positioned to ship it via DMX-pixel village infrastructure; the alternatives are one-off imports from Montreal or custom Moment Factory engagement.

**Ice / snow sculpture teams.** Not surfaced at mid-market scale in this research. Harbin Ice Festival (Manchuria) = ceiling reference. (Inferred — US mid-market ski resorts generally produce snow sculpture in-house or via regional art commissions; not a Brite-adjacent category.)

**Ski-resort-focused AV integrators.** Not surfaced as a distinct ski-vertical archetype. AV for ski-resort ballrooms / event spaces (weddings, corporate retreats) is typically served by regional AV integrators (same pool as hotels per BC-5921 finding — AVI-SPL, regional XTG-style practices). ### 2.2 Shoulder-season / mud-season / summer

Ski-Area Management surveys show 29% of resorts have mountain coasters, 34% zip lines, 21% ropes courses. Steamboat Springs reports summer is 35% of annual revenue; Aspen generates ~$180M in summer revenue; Spirit Mountain gets ~33% of gross from summer ([SAM — State of Summer Operations](https://www.saminfo.com/archives/2020-2029/2025/january-2025/the-state-of-summer-operations)). One Northeast resort: "The operating window for summer operations is profitable, however, when you layer in the shoulder seasons, summer becomes a break even." Mud-season (April-May) and shoulder-season programming is the documented weak link.

**Brite's shoulder-season angle.** Infrastructure installed for holiday + ski season is idle for ~6 months at Northeast independents. Multi-season programming (summer festivals, fall foliage, mud-season) is the operator headache Brite's permanent programmable layer can solve — re-programmed for summer music festivals, fall color anchors, spring events.

## 3. Buyer motivations — Brite's wedge thesis

### 3.1 Lift revenue / ancillary split — CONFIRMS VILLAGE = 48% of revenue opportunity

**Lift revenue = 52% of total resort revenue; ancillary (F&B, retail, lessons, rentals) = 48%** ([SAM — Take It All Into Account](https://www.saminfo.com/archives/2020-2029/2024/may-2024/take-it-all-into-account); [PeakRankings — Vail Financials FY24](https://www.peakrankings.com/content/vail-resorts-financials-unpacked-fiscal-year-2024)). Ancillary has been a majority-of-revenue category for 4 consecutive years. Per-skier revenue is **$60-$120 total** across NA and Europe (lift + lessons + rentals + F&B + retail).

**Brite thesis confirmed — village ancillary is near-half of resort revenue**, and is the category operators say they want to grow. Vail Resorts FY25 Q2: "increased ancillary spend per guest across our ski school and dining businesses."

### 3.2 Bad-snow-year sensitivity — amplifies Brite value

**Vail Resorts saw a 20% visitation drop during dismal Western snowfall.** During the 2024-25 early-season low-snow window (through Jan 4), **ski school revenue dropped 14.9% YoY, F&B dining dropped 15.9% YoY** ([Summit Daily](https://www.summitdaily.com/news/skier-visits-down-lift-revenue-up-at-vail-resorts-properties-for-2024-25-season/)). 2001-2016 window: 5 lowest-snow years = 5.5M lower-than-average skier visits and ~17,400 jobs lost in low-snow years ([Grist](https://grist.org/energy/ski-resorts-are-increasingly-reliant-on-snowmaking-but-at-what-cost/); [Bank of America Institute](https://institute.bankofamerica.com/sustainability/snow-trends-impact-on-ski-resorts.html)).

**Implication.** F&B / après-ski revenue is MORE sensitive to visitation than lift revenue. Village programming that extends dwell time and captures après-ski spend is a hedge against snow-dependent lift revenue. (Inferred — village-programmable-media is a snow-independent revenue lever; in low-snow years it's a relative-share gainer.)

### 3.3 Village dwell time / per-cap — not publicly benchmarked

**No public per-cap or dwell-time benchmarks surfaced** for mid-market villages. Vail Resorts tracks "ancillary spend per destination guest visit" internally ([Vail Resorts FY25 Q2](https://investors.vailresorts.com/news-releases/news-release-details/vail-resorts-reports-fiscal-2025-second-quarter-results-and)). NSAA does not publish village-level per-cap. (Inferred — this is an operator-internal metric; Brite prospect discovery calls will need to elicit it directly.)

### 3.4 Killington $3B village development — the tentpole ICP anchor

**Killington's 25-year $3B village expansion**: 1,095 acres total, 45 acres dedicated to village, 720+ ski-in/ski-out condos + luxury townhomes + single-family residences in initial phases, two public squares as hubs for dining/retail/year-round activities, designed by Safdie Architects + PWP Landscape Architecture ([Vermont Business Magazine](https://vermontbiz.com/news/2025/january/19/killingtons-3-billion-expansion-aims-world-class-appeal-lasting-local-economic); [Vermont Public](https://www.vermontpublic.org/local-news/2024-12-31/theres-a-lot-at-stake-as-3-billion-ski-village-at-killington-takes-shape); [SAM — Killington Approval](https://www.saminfo.com/news/sam-headline-news/killington-receives-key-approval-for-new-ski-village)). Projected: 1,200 permanent jobs, $285M property-value increase over 10 years, 1,500 permanent hospitality jobs. Includes a working farm for farm-to-table dining.

**Critical ICP context.** Killington is now **local-investor-owned** (Phill Gross + Michael Ferri group, POWDR exited 2024) ([Seven Days VT](https://www.sevendaysvt.com/news/killingtons-new-owners-are-independent-local-minded-42306457/); [Storm Skiing — POWDR sale](https://www.stormskiing.com/p/powdr-to-sell-killington-pico-to)). This is a Brite-ICP-perfect target: mid-market East Coast scale, independent ownership, actively building a programmable-ready village from scratch, with a 25-year capital plan. **Killington should be a named target in outbound sequences.**

### 3.5 Recent ownership transitions = capital-planning windows

- **Killington + Pico** — POWDR → local investors, 2024 ([Storm Skiing](https://www.stormskiing.com/p/powdr-to-sell-killington-pico-to)).
- **Jay Peak** — EB-5 receivership → **Pacific Group Resorts** (Park City UT-based), $76M auction Sep 2022 ([VTDigger](https://vtdigger.org/2022/09/16/as-judge-oks-76-million-sale-of-jay-peak-resort-receiver-says-burke-mountain-resort-could-soon-be-sold/); [Pacific Group Resorts announcement](https://www.pgri.us/pacific-group-resorts-completes-purchase-of-jay-peak-resort/)).
- **Burke Mountain VT** — receivership → **Bear Den Partners** (Burke Mountain Academy + Graham family + Schaefer family of Berkshire East MA), ~$30M planned investment, May 2025 ([The Hardwick Gazette](https://hardwickgazette.org/2025/05/13/new-burke-mountain-resort-owners-plan-major-upgrades/)).
- **Ragged Mountain NH** — SF Mountain Co. local investors, Nov 2025 ([Valley News](https://vnews.com/2025/11/09/local-investors-buy-ragged-mountain/)).

**Inferred.** Post-acquisition 12-24 month window is a high-signal capital-planning moment. Brite should track ski-area ownership transitions as a recency signal (see §6).

## 4. ICP — who to target

### 4.1 NSAA macro

**2024-25 US skier visits = 61.5M (second-best season on record), 492 operating ski areas (up from 484), avg reinvestment $21.11 per skier visit** ([SAM — 61.5M Skier Visits](https://www.saminfo.com/news/sam-headline-news/u-s-ski-areas-report-61-5m-skier-visits-for-2024-25-second-best-season-on-record); [Colorado Sun](https://coloradosun.com/2025/05/12/national-skier-visits-2024-25/)). **Growth concentrated at small/medium areas** per NSAA convention report — mid-market is the growth segment.

### 4.2 Scale distribution

## 8. Good-vs-bad program taxonomy

### 8.1 Programmable / dynamic vs static

- **Good** — SkyBridge 200K individually-controllable nodes, season-long programming, shoulder-season re-programming. Beaver Creek Thursday Night Wonder larger-than-life art installations with weekly musicians/performers/art walks.
- **Bad** — Seasonal-only static warm-white strings installed via turnkey holiday-lighting service (Colorado Christmas Lights, Above All Holiday Lighting). Static, takedown every April, no compounding across seasons.

### 8.2 Snow-dependent vs snow-independent

- **Good** — village-integrated lighting works regardless of natural snow conditions (Boyne SkyBridge operates Nov-April regardless of snow quality; Spruce Peak Lights Festival operates village center). Hedges bad-snow-year ancillary decline.
- **Bad** — on-mountain-only installations that require snowpack (torchlight parades require skiable terrain, mountain-coaster requires grooming). Useful but snow-dependent.

### 8.3 Season length

- **Good** — Nov through April (~6 months = full ski season plus shoulder). Boyne SkyBridge is the gold standard. Extend to May-October summer and you get year-round amortization on the same infrastructure.
- **Bad** — 6-week holiday-only windows (mid-November through New Year's). Most regional holiday-lighting-installer products.

### 8.4 Village-traffic activation vs "pretty"

- **Good** — measurable F&B / retail per-cap lift via dwell time extension, one-way or looped crowd-flow design, proximity to village F&B tenants. Beaver Creek Thursday Night Wonder uses performers + art walks to move foot traffic through Village businesses.
- **Bad** — photo-backdrop installations that extract a photo and move visitors back to parking lots. Zero per-cap lift.

### 8.5 Vendor tenure / site switching cost

- **Good** — MK Illumination Seefeld since 2019 = 7 consecutive years, with accretive additions (train station partnership, whale installation 2023). Moment Factory Vallea Lumina opened 2018, permanent installation.
- **Bad** — turnkey holiday-lighting installer-of-the-year rotations. No compounding narrative, no platform lock-in, every RFP starts from scratch.

### 8.6 Compression-week optimization

- **Good** — programming that layers on top of existing compression-week anchors (NYE fireworks + torchlight + village light overlay = higher willingness to pay for premium lift tickets / F&B reservations). Okemo's NYE stack (music + fireworks + torchlight) is the template.
- **Bad** — programming that competes with compression-week anchors instead of amplifying them.

### 8.7 Shoulder-season / multi-season programmability

- **Good** — infrastructure re-programmable for summer music festivals, fall foliage, mud-season maker events. Boyne SkyBridge's Nov-April = partial; Moment Factory Vallea Lumina year-round = benchmark.
- **Bad** — holiday-only installations idle for 6+ months.
