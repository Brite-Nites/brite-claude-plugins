# risk-reversal | Sports Stadiums | Brite Labs (plaza pilot zone)

## Hook (vertical-specific recency waterfall)

Anchor on a recency signal that also suggests budget-risk sensitivity or ownership visibility: a capital-project approval with a tight non-sport-event programming milestone (USF $348.5M precedent, greenfield non-sport programming intent), a sponsor-visibility commitment already promised on one plaza or concourse surface, an owner-operator or athletic-director transition with a near-term P&L review, a Diamond Baseball Holdings acquisition in month 3-9 of post-acquisition capital-plan refresh, or a Director of Revenue appointment within 90 days. Tone: specific and committee-safe, not playful. The hook names the pilot zone and signals that the pilot carries its own guarantee so the GM does not carry budget risk internally.

## Vertical anti-slop

- Do not use photo-share or social-media-share metrics as the guarantee. Per BC-5879 ledger § Offer A fix #2, photo-share is gimmicky and disconnected from stadium revenue success. Guarantee metric must be stadium-native: non-sport event dwell time, F&B per-cap attach in pilot-adjacent concessions, post-event foot traffic in programmed hours, or sponsor-activation attach within the pilot zone.
- Don't frame the pilot as a replacement of the incumbent. Frame as a "specialist add-on" or "complementary zone"; internal positioning with the existing holiday installer or Musco Show-Light service contract matters on T4 as much as on T2.
- Don't pitch Facilities or pro-team front-office on the pilot guarantee. GM or Director of Events is the pilot-authorizing decision-maker; Director of Revenue often routes measurement review. Facilities is wrong-P&L; pro-team front-office is wrong-zone.
- Don't over-specify incumbent vendor type in body copy. Use "existing seasonal-program vendor", "incumbent holiday installer", or "current lighting vendor"; never a sub-category shorthand that surfaced from the vendor-landscape research.
- Don't anchor on NFL, MLB, NBA, NHL, or Power 4 FBS venues as pilot-zone peers. Use MiLB (Diamond Baseball Holdings and independent-league), Group of 5 FBS, MLS, USL Championship, or regional multipurpose anchors.
- Don't over-promise performance metrics beyond Labs' documented case studies and the transferable Hogle Zoo economics. The guarantee is credible only if Brite can defend the specific threshold with real data and honest transferability framing.
- Avoid guarantee language that triggers ownership, board, or athletic-department legal review ("refund in full", "zero-cost until delivered", "money-back"). Use outcome-gated framing: "case-study rights clause voids and you walk with the install".
- Don't frame as sport-programming. The pilot guarantee is on the off-season non-sport surface; sport-programming framing puts Brite in Musco's zone and invalidates the guarantee frame.
- Don't use "iconic", "legendary", "hallowed ground", "storied franchise", "the roar of the crowd", or sport-fandom consumer-marketing vocabulary. T4 pilot conversations open on the zone, the measurement method, and the guarantee structure.
- Don't use broadcast-spec or game-day sport-lighting language (telecast-compliance, FCC sport-lighting, field-of-play, "ring of steel", "FOH tower", "truss and rig", "mothership"). That framing collapses the pilot scope.
- Don't name competitors in body copy (Musco, Enchant Christmas, Magic of Lights, Skylands Light Show, Moment Factory, MK Illumination, Diversified, AVI-SPL). T4 is Brite's own commitment, not a head-to-head pitch.
- Don't name-drop artists or specific events as default proof points. The guarantee threshold and measurement method are the proof; names are not.
- Don't use urgency tactics. Pilot-zone decisions under ownership or athletic-department scrutiny run on their own cadence; urgency signals vendor desperation.
- Don't cite Brite Nites seasonal residential case studies. Labs experiential or capital installations only.
- Frame S4 Lights as Brite's partner, not a competitor. S4 is the DMX-pixel component supplier; T4 credibility leans on the supply-chain partnership for hardware assurance under a pilot guarantee.
- Don't reference installers or property management. Supply-excluded per handbook canon.
- Frame the commercial structure confidently, not supplicantly. The guarantee is Brite carrying the risk; the case-study rights exchange is the reciprocity, not a favor being asked.
- Lead with the measurement method and guarantee threshold; destination-branding or legacy narrative belongs later if anywhere.
