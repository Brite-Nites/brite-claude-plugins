## Vertical anti-slop

- Do not use photo-share, social-media-share, or impression-count metrics as the guarantee. Per BC-5879 ledger § Offer A fix #2, photo-share is gimmicky and disconnected from non-gaming scoreboard. Guarantee metric must be casino-native: carded-player attach rate in pilot zone during programming hours, F and B per-cap lift in pilot-adjacent F and B operations, or loyalty-tier conversion rate on pilot-zone guests.
- Do not use gaming-floor-proximal metrics as the guarantee. Gaming-floor, pit, table-games, slot-array, count-room, cage, and surveillance zones are regulated out-of-zone per playbook anti-slop rule 3; pilot-zone guarantees must name a non-gaming zone metric.
- Don't frame the pilot as a replacement of the incumbent AV-integrator or holiday-lighting vendor. Frame as a "specialist add-on" or "complementary zone"; internal positioning with the existing vendor relationship matters.
- Don't over-specify incumbent vendor type in body copy. Use "existing full-property AV-integrator", "incumbent", or "current holiday-lighting vendor". Never use "lantern vendor", "projection vendor", "turnkey AV installer", or any sub-category shorthand.
- Don't over-promise performance metrics beyond Labs' documented case studies. The guarantee is credible only if Labs can defend the specific attach-rate, F and B per-cap, or loyalty-tier-conversion threshold with real data (Seminole Hard Rock Hollywood ceiling-in-scope precedent, S4 venue portfolio, and the pilot-zone motion).
- Avoid guarantee language that triggers casino finance, ownership legal, or Tribal Gaming Commission review: "refund in full", "zero-cost until delivered", "money-back". Use outcome-gated framing: "case-study rights clause voids and you walk with the install".
- Don't pitch gaming-floor, pit, table games, slot arrays, count room, cage, or surveillance programmable-media. Pilot zones are non-gaming-zone-only: F and B patio, pool deck, porte-cochere, retail promenade, hotel tower exterior, boardwalk facade.
- Don't pitch Marketing or Facilities as primary. Pilot-zone decision-makers are Director of F and B or property GM.
- Don't anchor on Las Vegas Strip or Atlantic City Boardwalk flagships. Ceiling-only and paired with in-scope mid-market anchor.
- No "luxury", "opulent", "high-roller-only", or "VIP-only" buzzwords.
- No "experience upgrade" standalone buzzword. T4 tone especially rewards naming the specific non-gaming-economic metric the pilot-zone buyer tracks.
- No generic "ROI uplift" without a named casino-specific metric. T4 tone rewards specificity: carded-player attach-rate delta, F and B per-cap lift in pilot-adjacent operations, or loyalty-tier conversion delta on pilot-zone guests.
- Don't name competitors in body copy (Moment Factory, MK Illumination, Tianyu, Christie Digital, Limelight Art, CCS, Nanolumens, HB Communications). T4 is Brite's own commitment, not a head-to-head pitch.
- Don't use urgency tactics. Pilot-zone decisions under ownership or Council scrutiny run on their own cadence; urgency signals vendor desperation.
- Don't cite Brite Nites seasonal residential case studies. Cite Labs experiential or capital installations only.
- Don't reference installers or property management. Supply-excluded per handbook canon.
- Frame S4 Lights as Brite's partner, not a competitor. S4 is the DMX-pixel component supplier; the supply-chain partnership underwrites hardware assurance on the guarantee.
- Frame the commercial structure confidently, not supplicantly. The guarantee is Brite carrying the risk; the case-study rights exchange is the reciprocity, not a favor being asked (per BC-5879 ledger § Offer A fix #3).
- Respect tribal sovereignty in framing at Tribal Class III prospects, even at pilot-zone scope. Council-aware etiquette in the guarantee conversation if the pilot earns expansion; "we're structuring the guarantee to be Council-ready if the pilot earns the next zone" is appropriate at tribal properties.
