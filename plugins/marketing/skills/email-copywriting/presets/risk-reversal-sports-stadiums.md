## Vertical anti-slop

- Don't pitch Facilities or pro-team front-office. T4 credibility depends on pitching the right P&L. GM primary, Director of Revenue co-primary, Owner-Operator at DBH-portfolio or family-owned (Athletic Director at Group of 5 FBS), Director of Events as coalition influencer.
- Don't anchor on NFL, MLB, NBA, NHL, or Power 4 FBS as standalone proof. Even on T4 the rule holds: enterprise flagships are ceiling references only, always paired with a mid-market ICP anchor in the same sentence.
- Don't over-promise performance metrics beyond Labs' documented case studies and the transferable Hogle Zoo economics. The guarantee is credible only if Brite can defend the specific breakeven threshold with real data and honest transferability framing.
- Avoid guarantee language that triggers owner-operator or athletic-department legal review ("refund in full", "zero-cost until delivered", "money-back"). Use outcome-gated framing: "fee not billed until breakeven".
- Don't frame as sport-programming. T4 is the full-season production-finance commitment across the non-sport calendar (holiday activations, concerts, corporate / wedding rentals, international friendlies at FBS, MLS plaza activations, community events). Sport-programming framing collapses the commercial model.
- Don't use "iconic", "legendary", "hallowed ground", "storied franchise", "the roar of the crowd", or "where legends are made" adjectives. Consumer-marketing warm-glow vocabulary zeros out credibility on a committee evaluating a multi-season revenue-share commitment.
- Don't use sport-fandom framing or broadcast-spec / game-day sport-lighting language. T4 specifically lives outside Musco's field-of-play zone; any drift toward that vocabulary invalidates the commercial frame.
- Don't name competitors in body copy (Musco, Enchant Christmas, Magic of Lights, Skylands Light Show, Moment Factory, MK Illumination, Diversified, AVI-SPL). T4 is Brite's own commitment, not a head-to-head pitch. Even implied comparisons reduce credibility.
- Don't name-drop artists or specific events as default proof points on T4. Breakeven accounting is the proof; names are not.
- Don't use generic "ROI uplift" without a named stadium-specific metric. T4 doubles down on specificity: incremental non-sport event revenue, sponsor-inventory sell-through, anchor F&B tenant co-marketing revenue, plaza pixel ad-revenue pre-commits, prior-year-comparable baseline.
- Don't cite Brite Nites seasonal residential case studies. Labs experiential or capital installations only; residential analogs undercut the commercial T4 tone.
- Frame S4 Lights as Brite's partner, not a competitor. T4 credibility leans on the supply-chain partnership for hardware assurance under a multi-season guarantee; stadium-specific S4 venue name-drops pending BC-5939.
- Don't reference installers or property management. Supply-excluded per handbook canon.
- Don't use urgency tactics. T4 commitments at stadium run through ownership / board / athletic-department review on multi-month cycles; urgency reads as vendor-desperate.
- Lead with per-event revenue and calendar-fill economics; destination-branding, press-worthiness, and legacy narrative belong in paragraph 2 or later, never the hook.
