# risk-reversal | Hotels & Resorts | Brite Labs

## Hook (vertical-specific recency waterfall)

Anchor on the procurement-weight signal: ownership-group announcement, REIT earnings-call commentary on asset strategy, major renovation close-out, or a named multi-year capital plan reported in Skift / Hotel News Now / Asian Hospitality. Template hook: "Saw the {CAPITAL_SIGNAL} at {COMPANY} post on {DATE_SIGNAL}." Tone shift from T2: less playful, more respectful of the 3-year contract term and the year-1 guarantee structure. {GM_NAME} anchors the greeting-merged first sentence, with Director of Revenue CC-readiness implied by the guarantee vocabulary. The hook should read as if written by someone who understands that a Branded Seasonal Program is a three-year vendor relationship from day one.

## Vertical anti-slop

- Don't over-promise performance metrics beyond what Brite Labs can defend with a Fairmont-class or Hotel-del-class case study. The guarantee is credible only if the peak-weekend ADR-lift number cites a specific precedent or a defensible revenue pro-forma. Generic "uplift" numbers zero out credibility with a Director of Revenue.
- Don't propose guarantee structures that would require ownership-level legal review beyond standard vendor contracts (e.g. "refund in full", "zero-cost until delivered"). Use outcome-based framing: "year 2 not billed until year 1 delivers peak-weekend ADR lift over comp-set baseline."
- Don't name-drop Marriott, Hilton, Hyatt, Ritz-Carlton, Four Seasons, or Waldorf Astoria as guarantee precedents. Enterprise flagships run multi-year RFPs and 8-15-stakeholder decision surfaces. Boutique and regional independents do not procure on that model and those brand anchors signal the vendor mis-read the property.
- Don't address "VP Revenue Management" or "VP of Experience" in the salutation. Those titles do not exist at 100-400 key independents. Pitch GM as primary and Director of Revenue as the numerate buyer who evaluates the guarantee math.
- Don't use "romantic", "ambiance", "magical", "enchanting", "dazzling", or "mesmerizing" in the guarantee framing. A T4 risk-reversal pitch reads from the revenue-manager scorecard, not the guest-facing brochure. These words instantly recode the email as low-trust vendor copy.
- Don't lead with wedding-revenue guarantees. Wedding-venue economics are a separate cost center with a separate decision-maker (Events Sales). If the Director of Revenue routes the email to Events Sales, the multi-year program conversation dies.
- Don't mention competitors by name. T4 is about Labs' own commitment to the year-1 floor, not a head-to-head pitch against Holiday Outdoor Décor or MK Illumination. Comparison framing dilutes the guarantee and signals a fee-for-service mental model instead of a revenue-share one.
- Don't use urgency tactics ("limited availability", "last chance", "only N contracts left"). Ownership-level capital review runs on its own calendar and urgency reads as vendor-desperate in a multi-year scoping conversation.
