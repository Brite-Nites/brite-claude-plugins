# risk-reversal | Zoos | Brite Labs

## Hook (vertical-specific recency waterfall)

Anchor on the procurement-weight signal: a large named sponsor already in pipeline, a published economic-impact study (Denver ceiling citation for hotel-room-nights), a city economic-dev partnership with public dollars attached, or a multi-year capital-plan vote. Tone shift from T2 list-building: less playful, more respectful of the multi-season commitment and the committee process. {FIRST_NAME} anchors the greeting-merged first sentence. The hook should read as if written by someone who understands that signing Offer E is a board-visible decision.

Base skeleton B adaptation. Guarantee shape: first-season-breakeven-or-no-pay. ## Step 2 bump

Tone: specificity as credibility. The bump names which line items count toward breakeven, so the committee can evaluate the guarantee structure against real accounting rather than marketing language.

**Subject:** `Re: {Season guarantee|Program underwrite|Break-even terms}`

**Body:**

{Following up|Circling back}, {FIRST_NAME}. A guarantee like this {takes time|deserves a real look|runs through committee}, which is fair. To be specific about breakeven: gate-scan attendance revenue plus sponsor-inventory paid commitments plus ad-revenue pre-commits on pixel displays, measured against the production-finance fee at season close. ## Vertical anti-slop

- Don't over-specify incumbent vendor type in body copy. Use "existing lighting vendor", "incumbent", or "current seasonal-program vendor", never "lantern vendor", "projection vendor", or any sub-category shorthand that surfaced from the vendor-landscape research.
- Don't over-promise performance metrics beyond Labs' documented case studies. The guarantee is credible only if Labs can defend the specific outcome with real data.
- Avoid guarantee language that triggers nonprofit legal review ("refund in full", "zero-cost until delivered", "money-back"). Use outcome-gated framing: "fee not billed until breakeven".
- No generic "ROI uplift" without a named metric. Zoo economics are specific: hotel-room-nights attributable, membership-conversion rate, per-cap spend, ticket-revenue delta, sponsor-inventory sell-through.
- Use "program" not "event" or "activation". Zoos run multi-season programs with YoY refresh logic; T4 tone especially rewards long-horizon vocabulary.
- Don't cite Brite Nites seasonal residential case studies. Cite Labs experiential or capital installations only; residential analogs undercut the commercial T4 tone.
- Don't name competitors in body copy (Tianyu Arts & Culture, Illuminight Lighting, Moment Factory, Limelight Art). T4 is Brite's own commitment, not a head-to-head pitch. Even implied comparisons reduce credibility.
- Don't name species in prose (tigers, elephants, giraffes). Use "your collection", "your flagship exhibits", or the specific public-program name.
- No "magical", "enchanting", "wondrous", or "mesmerizing" adjectives. Consultant-speak zeros out credibility on an economic-outcome committee.
- Don't use urgency tactics. Capital commitments at zoos run through boards and development committees on multi-month cycles; urgency reads as vendor-desperate.
- Don't reference installers or property management. Supply-excluded per handbook canon.
- Lead with Operations, Development, or CEO; Marketing is a secondary or parallel touch on T4. Offer E P&L authority sits with Operations / Development / CEO / Director of Corporate Partnerships, not Marketing.
- Frame S4 Lights as Brite's partner, not a competitor. S4 is the DMX-pixel component supplier; T4 credibility leans on the supply-chain partnership for hardware assurance under a guarantee.
