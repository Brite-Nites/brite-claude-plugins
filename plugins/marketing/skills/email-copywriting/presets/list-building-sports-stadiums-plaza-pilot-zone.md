## Vertical anti-slop

- Don't frame the pilot as a replacement of the incumbent. Frame as a "specialist add-on" or "complementary zone". Positioning matters for the GM's and Director of Events' internal politics with the existing holiday installer or Musco Show-Light service contract.
- Don't pitch Facilities or pro-team front-office, even on a narrow pilot. GM or Director of Events is the pilot-authorizing decision-maker; pitching Facilities drops the email into capex-maintenance review and dies there, pitching pro-team front-office puts Brite in Musco's field-of-play zone.
- Don't over-specify incumbent vendor type in body copy. Use "existing seasonal-program vendor", "incumbent holiday installer", or "current lighting vendor"; never "Enchant-model vendor", "turnkey installer", or a sub-category shorthand that surfaced from the vendor-landscape research.
- Don't anchor on NFL, MLB, NBA, NHL, or Power 4 FBS venues as pilot-zone peers. Use MiLB (Diamond Baseball Holdings and independent-league), Group of 5 FBS, MLS, USL Championship, or regional multipurpose anchors.
- Don't frame as sport-programming. The pilot is on the off-season non-sport surface (plaza, concourse, club-level, exterior gateway), never the playing surface. Sport-programming framing puts Brite in Musco's zone and torpedoes the pilot-zone scope.
- Don't use "iconic", "legendary", "hallowed ground", "storied franchise", "the roar of the crowd", or sport-fandom consumer-marketing vocabulary. Pilot-zone conversations open on the specific zone, the concept board, and the measurement plan.
- Don't use broadcast-spec or game-day sport-lighting language (telecast-compliance, FCC sport-lighting, field-of-play, "ring of steel", "FOH tower", "truss and rig", "mothership"). Stadium plazas aren't concert-rig venues; either framing collapses credibility.
- Frame the commercial structure confidently, not supplicantly. Brite underwrites the install cost; the case-study usage rights are the exchange, not a favor being asked.
- The deliverable is a rendered concept board (a tangible 24"×36" artifact), not a "visualization" or "3D walkthrough" promise. Tangible artifact only, per BC-5879 ledger § Offer A fix #1.
- Do not use photo-share or social-media-share metrics as the measurement. The pilot is measured on stadium-native metrics: non-sport event dwell time in the pilot zone (sensor or head-count), F&B per-cap attach in pilot-adjacent concessions, post-event foot traffic in programmed hours, or sponsor-activation attach within the pilot zone.
- Don't use generic "ROI uplift" without a named metric. Pilot-zone economics are specific: dwell time, F&B per-cap attach, post-event foot traffic, sponsor-activation attach.
- No "magical", "enchanting", "wondrous", or "mesmerizing" adjectives. Consumer-marketing consultant-speak reads as the wrong vendor archetype.
- Don't use urgency tactics. Pilot-zone decisions run through GM and Director of Events on their own cadence; urgency signals desperation and disrespects the incumbent relationship.
- Frame S4 Lights as Brite's partner, not a competitor. S4 is the DMX-pixel component supplier whose supply-chain credibility underwrites the pilot-zone hardware.
- Don't name competitors in body copy (Musco, Enchant Christmas, Magic of Lights, Skylands Light Show, Moment Factory, MK Illumination, Diversified, AVI-SPL). The pilot is Brite's own narrow commitment; even implied comparisons reduce credibility.
- Don't name-drop artists or specific events as default proof points. The concept board is the proof; names are not.
- Don't cite Brite Nites seasonal residential case studies. Labs experiential or capital installations only.
- Don't reference installers or property management. Supply-excluded per handbook canon.
- Lead with the specific zone and the concept board deliverable; destination-branding or legacy narrative belongs later if anywhere.
