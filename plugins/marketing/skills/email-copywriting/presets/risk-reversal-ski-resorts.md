## Vertical anti-slop

- Don't pitch Lift Ops, Mountain Ops, Skier Services, Ski Patrol, or Mountain Manager. Brite's zone is the village, after lift close. Lift-line and on-mountain are the wrong persona, wrong P&L, wrong motion.
- Use "General Manager", "F&B Director", "Director of Sales & Marketing", "Owner-Operator", or (at 300K+ visit properties only) "Senior Director Base Area" or "Senior Director Village Operations" as the salutation. Do NOT use "VP Village Operations", "VP Base Area Operations", or "VP Non-Gameday Revenue"; those titles are enterprise-only.
- Don't anchor on Vail, Deer Valley, Aspen, Park City, Breckenridge, or Whistler-Blackcomb as standalone framing. Ceiling references only, paired with a specific Eastern or Midwestern mid-market ICP anchor in the same sentence. Growth is at small and medium areas per NSAA.
- Don't frame as holiday-only. Use "season-long programming", "Nov-April multi-season", or "shoulder-season amortization".
- Don't over-promise performance metrics beyond Labs' documented case studies. The guarantee is credible only if Labs can defend the specific threshold with real data (Boyne SkyBridge install-scale precedent, S4 venue portfolio, and the shared production-finance motion).
- Avoid guarantee language that triggers resort finance or ownership legal review: "refund in full", "zero-cost until delivered", "money-back". Use outcome-gated framing: "fee not billed until breakeven".
- No "magical", "magic of the mountains", "winter wonderland", "alpine wonderland", "enchanting snowscape", "winter paradise", "holiday charm", or "holiday magic" adjectives. Consumer-marketing warm-glow vocabulary zeroes out credibility on a capital committee.
- No ski-technical consumer vocabulary: "fall-line", "catch an edge", "bluebird day", "powder day", "freshies", "gnarly", "carving", "corduroy". Operator trade press (SAM, NSAA) does not use these in business-of-skiing contexts.
- No stadium or concert-rig language: "ring of steel", "truss and rig", "FOH tower", "mothership". Ski-resort villages aren't concert venues.
- No generic "ROI uplift" without a named ski-specific metric. T4 tone especially rewards specificity: ancillary-per-visit, F&B tenant retention rate, village dwell time, post-lift-close foot traffic, compression-week premium, shoulder-season amortization ratio, bad-snow-year ancillary-decline delta.
- Don't name competitors in body copy (Moment Factory, MK Illumination, Tianyu, Christie Digital, Limelight Art, Above All Holiday Lighting, Colorado Christmas Lights). T4 is Brite's own commitment, not a head-to-head pitch. Even implied comparisons reduce credibility on the ownership review.
- Frame S4 Lights as Brite's partner, never a competitor. S4 is the DMX-pixel component supplier whose supply-chain underwrites hardware assurance on the guarantee.
- Don't use urgency tactics. Capital commitments at ski resorts run through ownership groups and capital committees on multi-month or multi-season cycles; urgency reads as vendor-desperate.
- Don't cite Brite Nites seasonal residential case studies. Cite Labs experiential or capital installations only; residential analogs undercut the commercial T4 tone.
- Don't reference installers or property management. Supply-excluded per handbook canon.
- Lead ancillary-per-visit economics; destination-branding and press-worthy legacy live in paragraph 2 or later, not the hook.
