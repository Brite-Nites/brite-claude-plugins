# list-building | Ski Resorts | Brite Labs (pilot zone)

## Hook (vertical-specific recency waterfall)

Anchor on a recency signal that suggests the incumbent holiday-lighting vendor has a bounded blind spot. Top of the waterfall: a new village F&B tenant opening without integrated lighting scope, an après-ski plaza refresh the holiday installer didn't touch, a single-attraction sponsor-visibility gap, or a Director of F&B or Director of Sales & Marketing role change within 90 days. Second tier: a base-lodge courtyard or village plaza refresh announcement where the scope didn't include programmable lighting.

Tone: conversational and specific, not pitching a replacement. The incumbent relationship stays clean and the pilot is framed as a specialist add-on. {FIRST_NAME} anchors the greeting-merged first sentence. Template: `"{saw|noticed} {COMPANY}'s {ZONE_OR_SIGNAL}"`.

## Step 1 skeleton

Base skeleton A adaptation tuned for Offer A (pilot-zone tactical complement). The three Offer A fixes from the BC-5879 zoos ledger are load-bearing at ski and apply verbatim per the ski playbook § Offer candidates A review: frontend deliverable is a rendered concept board (a tangible 24"×36" artifact, not a "visualization" or "3D walkthrough" promise); the guarantee metric is village-native (dwell time in the pilot zone, après-ski F&B per-cap lift in pilot-adjacent F&B operations, or post-lift-close foot traffic in programmed hours; never photo-share, which is gimmicky and disconnected from resort success); the commercial structure is a confident underwrite, not a supplicant case-study ask. `{PILOT_ZONE_AREA}` is operator-filled with a specific zone at the village (for example, "the base-lodge courtyard", "the après-ski plaza near {F&B_TENANT}", or "the village plaza gateway"). `{LABS_PEER_VENUE}` defaults to Boyne SkyBridge as the mid-market specialist-add-on anchor; `{S4_PARTNER}` fills as "S4 Lights".

## Vertical anti-slop

- Don't frame the pilot as a replacement of the incumbent holiday-lighting vendor. Frame as a "specialist add-on" or "complementary zone". Positioning matters for the decision-maker's internal politics with the existing vendor relationship.
- Don't over-specify incumbent vendor type in body copy. Use "existing holiday-lighting vendor", "incumbent", or "current seasonal-program vendor". Never use "lantern vendor", "projection vendor", "turnkey holiday installer", or any sub-category shorthand surfaced from the vendor-landscape research.
- The deliverable is a rendered concept board (a tangible 24"×36" artifact), not a "visualization", "3D walkthrough", or "simulation". Tangible artifact only, per BC-5879 ledger § Offer A fix #1.
- Don't pitch Lift Ops, Mountain Ops, Skier Services, Ski Patrol, or Mountain Manager. Brite's zone is the village, after lift close.
- Use "General Manager", "F&B Director" or "Director of Food & Beverage", or "Director of Sales & Marketing" as the salutation at the pilot-zone tier. Do NOT use "VP Village Operations" or other enterprise-only titles.
- Don't anchor on Vail, Deer Valley, Aspen, Park City, Breckenridge, or Whistler-Blackcomb. Ceiling-only and paired with mid-market ICP.
- Don't frame as holiday-only. A pilot zone spans at minimum the Nov-April baseline; name shoulder-season re-programming upside as part of the pilot economics.
- No "magical", "magic of the mountains", "winter wonderland", "alpine wonderland", "enchanting snowscape", "winter paradise", "holiday charm", or "holiday magic" adjectives. Consumer-marketing warm-glow vocabulary reads as the wrong vendor type.
- No ski-technical consumer vocabulary: "fall-line", "catch an edge", "bluebird day", "powder day", "freshies", "gnarly", "carving", "corduroy".
- No stadium or concert-rig language: "ring of steel", "truss and rig", "FOH tower", "mothership".
- Frame the commercial structure confidently, not supplicantly. Brite underwrites the install cost; the case-study usage rights are the exchange, not a favor being asked (per BC-5879 ledger § Offer A fix #3).
- Pilot-zone guarantee metric is village-native: dwell time in the pilot zone (sensor or head-count), après-ski F&B per-cap lift in pilot-adjacent F&B operations, or post-lift-close foot traffic in programmed hours. Never photo-share or social-media-share (per BC-5879 ledger § Offer A fix #2).
- No generic "ROI uplift" without a named ski-specific metric. Pilot-zone economics are specific: village dwell-time delta, F&B per-cap lift in pilot-adjacent operations, or post-lift-close foot traffic in programmed hours.
- Don't use urgency tactics. Pilot-zone decisions run through GM and F&B Director on their own cadence; urgency signals vendor desperation and disrespects the incumbent relationship.
- Frame S4 Lights as Brite's partner, not a competitor. S4 is the DMX-pixel component supplier whose supply-chain underwrites the pilot-zone hardware.
- Don't cite Brite Nites seasonal residential case studies. Cite Labs experiential or capital installations only.
- Don't reference installers or property management. Supply-excluded per handbook canon.
