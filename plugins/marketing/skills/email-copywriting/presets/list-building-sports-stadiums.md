## Vertical anti-slop

- Don't pitch Facilities or pro-team front-office. Facilities is wrong-P&L (capex maintenance, target for LED-retrofit vendors); pro-team front-office is wrong-zone (sport-critical procurement, Musco's zone). Address GM as primary, Director of Revenue as co-primary where the role exists, Director of Events as coalition influencer, Owner-Operator at DBH-portfolio and family / local-investor-owned properties.
- Don't anchor on NFL, MLB, NBA, NHL, or Power 4 FBS (Alabama, Ohio State, Texas, Michigan, Penn State, Auburn) as standalone proof. Enterprise flagships are ceiling references only, and only paired in the same sentence with a mid-market ICP anchor. The ICP is MiLB (Diamond Baseball Holdings and independent-league), Group of 5 FBS, MLS, USL Championship, and regional multipurpose / amphitheater venues.
- Don't frame as sport-programming. Brite's zone is the off-season programmable-media layer (plaza, concourse, club-level, exterior gateway). Sport-programming framing puts Brite in Musco's zone and reads as vendor fundamentally misunderstanding stadium non-sport economics. Use "non-sport calendar", "year-round plaza programming", "revenue-diversification layer", or "off-season activation" as the frame.
- Don't use "iconic", "legendary", "hallowed ground", "storied franchise", "the roar of the crowd", "where legends are made", "magical atmosphere", or "iconic venue" adjectives. Consumer-marketing warm-glow vocabulary zeros out credibility on a per-event-revenue / ancillary-attach / calendar-fill buyer.
- Don't use sport-fandom framing ("packed house", "stadium shakes", "fans on their feet", "sea of fans", "electric atmosphere", "nothing like a home game"). Operator trade press (Sports Business Journal, Stadia Magazine, Paciolan) does not use this vocabulary; a vendor using it signals trying-too-hard.
- Don't use broadcast-spec or game-day sport-lighting language (telecast-compliance, FCC sport-lighting, Class I / Class II field-of-play, "ring of steel", "FOH tower", "truss and rig", "mothership"). That is Musco's zone or concert-rig vocabulary; either framing positions Brite as the wrong vendor archetype.
- Don't name-drop artists or specific events as default proof points. Brite's credibility is the production-finance motion and the infrastructure scale, not an artist or a single marquee event.
- Don't use generic "ROI uplift" without a named stadium-specific metric. Stadium economics are specific: per-event revenue lift, non-sport calendar fill rate, F&B attach rate, parking attach, sponsor-activation pricing, post-event plaza dwell, event-day foot-traffic lift, DBH portfolio comp-set benchmarks.
- Don't cite Brite Nites seasonal residential case studies. Cite Labs experiential or capital installations only; residential analogs misread the Offer E commercial motion.
- Frame S4 Lights as Brite's partner in body copy, not a competitor. S4 is the DMX-pixel component supplier; stadium-specific portfolio name-drops wait on BC-5939 customer-list retrieval.
- Don't reference installers or property management. Supply-excluded per handbook canon.
- Lead with per-event revenue and calendar-fill economics; destination-branding, press-worthiness, and legacy narrative belong in paragraph 2 or later.
- Don't use urgency tactics. Stadium capital and non-sport programming run on 6-9 month GM-authority cycles (12-18 months at capital tier); urgency reads as vendor-desperate to a GM fielding dozens of vendor pitches per quarter.
