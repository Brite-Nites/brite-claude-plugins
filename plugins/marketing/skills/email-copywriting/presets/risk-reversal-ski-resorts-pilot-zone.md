# risk-reversal | Ski Resorts | Brite Labs (pilot zone)

## Hook (vertical-specific recency waterfall)

Anchor on a recency signal that also carries budget-risk sensitivity on the specific pilot zone. Top of the waterfall: a new village F&B tenant opening with a tight launch window, a sponsor-visibility commitment already promised on one attraction, a board-level ancillary-per-visit target on a specific zone, a post-acquisition ownership-group audit within 12 months, or a Director of F&B or Director of Sales & Marketing role change within 90 days.

Tone: specific and committee-safe, not playful. The hook names the pilot zone and signals that the pilot carries its own guarantee so the decision-maker does not carry budget risk internally and the incumbent holiday-lighting vendor relationship stays clean. {FIRST_NAME} anchors the greeting-merged first sentence.

## Vertical anti-slop

- Do not use photo-share, social-media-share, or impression-count metrics as the guarantee. Per BC-5879 ledger § Offer A fix #2, photo-share is gimmicky and disconnected from resort success. Guarantee metric must be village-native: dwell time in the pilot zone, après-ski F&B per-cap lift in pilot-adjacent operations, or post-lift-close foot traffic in programmed hours.
- Don't frame the pilot as a replacement of the incumbent holiday-lighting vendor. Frame as a "specialist add-on" or "complementary zone"; internal positioning with the existing vendor relationship matters.
- Don't over-specify incumbent vendor type in body copy. Use "existing village holiday-lighting vendor", "incumbent", or "current seasonal-program vendor". Never use "lantern vendor", "projection vendor", "turnkey holiday installer", or any sub-category shorthand.
- Don't over-promise performance metrics beyond Labs' documented case studies. The guarantee is credible only if Labs can defend the specific threshold with real data (Boyne SkyBridge precedent, S4 venue portfolio, and the pilot-zone motion).
- Avoid guarantee language that triggers resort finance or ownership legal review: "refund in full", "zero-cost until delivered", "money-back". Use outcome-gated framing: "case-study rights clause voids and you walk with the install".
- Don't pitch Lift Ops, Mountain Ops, Skier Services, Ski Patrol, or Mountain Manager. Brite's zone is the village, after lift close.
- Use "General Manager", "F&B Director", or "Director of Sales & Marketing" as the salutation at the pilot-zone tier. Do NOT use "VP Village Operations" or other enterprise-only titles.
- Don't anchor on Vail, Deer Valley, Aspen, Park City, Breckenridge, or Whistler-Blackcomb. Ceiling-only and paired with mid-market ICP.
- Don't frame as holiday-only. Even a pilot zone spans Nov-April minimum; name shoulder-season re-programming as part of the pilot economics.
- No "magical", "magic of the mountains", "winter wonderland", "alpine wonderland", "enchanting snowscape", "winter paradise", "holiday charm", or "holiday magic" adjectives.
- No ski-technical consumer vocabulary: "fall-line", "catch an edge", "bluebird day", "powder day", "freshies", "gnarly", "carving", "corduroy".
- No stadium or concert-rig language: "ring of steel", "truss and rig", "FOH tower", "mothership".
- Don't name competitors in body copy (Moment Factory, MK Illumination, Tianyu, Christie Digital, Limelight Art, Above All Holiday Lighting, Colorado Christmas Lights). T4 is Brite's own commitment, not a head-to-head pitch.
- Don't use urgency tactics. Pilot-zone decisions under ownership scrutiny run on their own cadence; urgency signals vendor desperation.
- Don't cite Brite Nites seasonal residential case studies. Cite Labs experiential or capital installations only; residential analogs undercut the commercial T4 tone.
- Don't reference installers or property management. Supply-excluded per handbook canon.
- Frame S4 Lights as Brite's partner, not a competitor. S4 is the DMX-pixel component supplier; the supply-chain partnership underwrites hardware assurance on the guarantee.
- Frame the commercial structure confidently, not supplicantly. The guarantee is Brite carrying the risk; the case-study rights exchange is the reciprocity, not a favor being asked (per BC-5879 ledger § Offer A fix #3).
