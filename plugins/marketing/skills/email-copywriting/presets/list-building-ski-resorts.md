# list-building | Ski Resorts | Brite Labs

## Hook (vertical-specific recency waterfall)

Anchor on the highest-signal ski-specific recency surface. Top of the waterfall: a named ownership transition with a post-acquisition capital-plan window (Burke 2025 Bear Den Partners, Ragged 2025 SF Mountain Co., Killington 2024 local-investor group, Jay Peak 2022 Pacific Group Resorts), a new GM appointment mid-audit-cycle (SAM Snow People monthly column), or a village-expansion / base-lodge-refresh announcement (Killington $3B 25-year village is the tentpole: local-investor-owned, 45 acres dedicated village, actively building programmable-ready infrastructure).

Second tier: a Director of F&B or Director of Sales & Marketing role change within 90 days, a bad-snow-year ancillary-decline press beat (F&B down mid-teens YoY while lift rises is the structural wedge signal at GM level), or an Indy Pass member resort without a prior village-programming program. Open with ancillary-per-visit economic outcome; destination-branding and press-worthy legacy live in paragraph 2, not the hook (per playbook voice rule 6 + anti-slop rule 5).

## Vertical anti-slop

- Don't pitch Lift Ops, Mountain Ops, Skier Services, Ski Patrol, or Mountain Manager. Brite's zone is the village, after lift close. Lift-line and on-mountain are the wrong persona, wrong P&L, wrong motion; outbound that lands there is the fastest way to eliminate trust at a ski-resort prospect.
- Use "General Manager", "F&B Director" or "Director of Food & Beverage", "Director of Sales & Marketing", or "Owner-Operator" as the salutation. Do NOT use "VP Village Operations", "VP Base Area Operations", or "VP Non-Gameday Revenue"; those titles are enterprise-only (Vail Resorts, Alterra). At regional independents the title is GM, Senior Director, or family-owner.
- Don't anchor on Vail, Deer Valley, Aspen, Park City, Breckenridge, or Whistler-Blackcomb as standalone framing. They are ceiling references only, named paired with a specific Eastern or Midwestern mid-market ICP anchor in the same sentence. Growth is at small and medium areas per NSAA; the ICP is independent regional mid-markets with branded villages.
- Don't frame as holiday-only. Use "season-long programming" or "Nov-April multi-season" or "shoulder-season amortization". A November-only frame signals vendor who hasn't read ski economics, and the ancillary wedge compounds across the full season plus shoulder, while the bad-snow-year F&B hedge value comes from multi-season operation.
- No "magical", "magic of the mountains", "winter wonderland", "alpine wonderland", "enchanting snowscape", "winter paradise", "holiday charm", or "holiday magic" adjectives. Consumer-marketing warm-glow vocabulary zeroes out credibility on an ancillary-per-visit or compression-economics buyer. If the programming matters, the numbers describe it; if the numbers aren't there, adjectives don't substitute.
- No ski-technical consumer vocabulary: "fall-line", "catch an edge", "bluebird day", "powder day", "freshies", "gnarly", "carving", "corduroy". Operator trade press (SAM, NSAA) does not use these in business-of-skiing contexts. A vendor using this vocabulary signals trying-too-hard and reads as pretending to be an insider.
- No stadium or concert-rig language: "ring of steel", "truss and rig", "FOH tower", "mothership". Ski-resort villages aren't concert venues; the framing collapses credibility.
- No generic "ROI uplift" without a named ski-specific metric. Ski economics are specific: ancillary-per-visit, F&B tenant retention rate, village dwell time, post-lift-close foot traffic, compression-week premium, shoulder-season amortization ratio, bad-snow-year ancillary-decline delta. Generic "ROI" signals Brite hasn't done ski-vertical homework.
- Lead ancillary-per-visit economics; destination-branding and press-worthy legacy live in paragraph 2 or later, not the hook. A GM measured on ancillary-per-visit on the Monday after Christmas-NYE reads destination-branding openers as agency-speak.
- Frame Moment Factory as a primary ski-resort competitor with a distinct wedge, not as adjacent. Vallea Lumina, Alta Lumina, and Tonga Lumina are SAM-endorsed Lumina installations at Whistler-Blackcomb, Les Gets, and Mont Tremblant. Brite's differentiator is form-factor: MF sells ticketed destination-pathway attractions, Brite sells ambient village-integrated programmable infrastructure. Do NOT lift the zoos playbook's "rare at zoos" framing without re-grounding for ski.
- Frame MK Illumination as adjacent-not-competitive at US ski villages. European Seefeld Austria alpine-tourism Christmas-market precedent only; no evidenced US ski-resort base-village book of business. Do NOT lift the hotels playbook's "MK is a direct competitor" inversion into ski copy without a named US ski-resort MK engagement.
- Frame S4 Lights as Brite's partner, never a competitor. S4 is the DMX-pixel component supplier whose venue portfolio Brite name-drops for breadth credibility and whose supply-chain secures the pixel hardware.
- Don't use urgency tactics. Ski capital and seasonal programs run on multi-month and multi-year cycles; urgency reads as vendor-desperate and under-researched.
- Don't cite Brite Nites seasonal residential case studies. Cite Labs experiential or capital installations only (Boyne SkyBridge is the primary anchor; S4 venue-portfolio second). Residential analogs misread the Offer E motion and read as scale-mismatched.
- Don't reference installers or property management. Supply-excluded per handbook canon.
