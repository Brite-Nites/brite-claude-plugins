# list-building | Zoos | Brite Labs

## Hook (vertical-specific recency waterfall)

Anchor on the strongest public signal. Top of the waterfall: a named title-sponsor pledge (Invesco QQQ / ComEd / CPS Energy / Meijer precedent), a published economic-impact figure (Denver Zoo's 85.1% net-new economic activity and 16,502 hotel-room-nights/yr is the ceiling citation), or a new Director of Corporate Partnerships / CEO / Executive Director role change within 90 days.

Second tier: program on-sale date if the season window is near, a sponsor-pipeline line on the zoo's annual report, or a capital-plan vote in city-council minutes. Greeting-merges into the first sentence. Template: "{saw|noticed} {COMPANY}'s {RECENCY_ANCHOR}". Open with economic outcome; conservation and AZA framing lives in paragraph 2, not the hook (per BC-5920 gate confirmation).

## Vertical anti-slop

- Don't over-specify incumbent vendor type in body copy. Use "existing lighting vendor", "incumbent", or "current seasonal-program vendor", never "lantern vendor", "projection vendor", or any sub-category shorthand that surfaced from the vendor-landscape research.
- Don't name-drop Disney, Universal, or theme-park analogs. Stay within zoo-precedent anchors: Lincoln Park ZooLights, Columbus Zoo, Nashville Zoolumination, Hogle Zoo.
- Use "program" not "event" or "activation". Zoos run multi-season programs with YoY refresh logic; "event" signals agency-speak and misreads the buying cycle.
- Don't name species in prose (tigers, elephants, giraffes). Use "your collection", "your flagship exhibits", or the specific public-program name if the zoo has one.
- No "magical", "enchanting", "wondrous", or "mesmerizing" adjectives. Consultant-speak zeros out credibility on an economic-outcome buyer.
- Lead with economic outcome (hotel-room-nights, sponsor-pipeline health, per-cap spend, membership conversion), not aesthetic outcome. Aesthetic framing signals Brite hasn't done the vertical homework.
- Lead with Operations, Development, or CEO; Marketing is a secondary or parallel touch. Offer E P&L authority sits with Operations / Development / CEO / Director of Corporate Partnerships, not Marketing.
- No generic "ROI uplift" without a named metric. Zoo economics are specific: hotel-room-nights attributable, membership-conversion rate, per-cap spend, ticket-revenue delta, sponsor-inventory sell-through.
- Frame S4 Lights as Brite's partner in body copy, not a competitor. S4 is the DMX-pixel component supplier whose venue list Brite name-drops for breadth credibility.
- No stadium or concert-rig language ("ring of steel", "truss and rig", "FOH tower", "mothership"). Zoos aren't concert venues; the framing collapses credibility.
- Don't use urgency tactics. Zoo capital and seasonal programs run on long cycles; urgency reads as vendor-desperate.
- Don't cite Brite Nites seasonal residential case studies. Cite Labs experiential or capital installations only; residential analogs misread the Offer E motion.
- Conservation and mission framing surface as supporting beats in paragraph 2 or later, never as the hook. Opening with mission-speak from a vendor reads as performative to a CEO or Corporate Partnerships Director.
