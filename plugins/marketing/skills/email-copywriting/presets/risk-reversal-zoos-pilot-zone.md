# risk-reversal | Zoos | Brite Labs (pilot zone)

## Hook (vertical-specific recency waterfall)

Anchor on a recency signal that also suggests budget-risk sensitivity: a new exhibit announcement with a tight opening window, a sponsor-visibility commitment already promised on one attraction, a board-level attendance target on a specific zone, or a Marketing or Corporate-Partnerships role change within 90 days. Tone: specific and committee-safe, not playful. The hook names the pilot zone and signals that the pilot carries its own guarantee so the decision-maker does not carry budget risk internally.

## Step 1 skeleton

Base skeleton B adaptation tuned for Offer A (pilot-zone tactical complement) with a venue-native guarantee metric. Pick ONE metric and name it in body copy: dwell time in the pilot zone (sensor or head-count), attendance uplift in programmed hours (gate-scan data), or sponsor-visibility impressions (pilot-zone branding counted via photo or computer-vision pass). Default shown below names dwell time (`{DWELL_THRESHOLD}` operator-filled, e.g., "15 minutes average"). NEVER photo-share as a metric; per BC-5879 ledger § Offer A fix #2, photo-share is gimmicky and disconnected from venue success. `{PILOT_ZONE_AREA}` and `{LABS_PEER_VENUE}` (default Hogle Zoo) and `{S4_PARTNER}` ("S4 Lights") fill as in the T2 pilot-zone preset.

## Vertical anti-slop

- Do not use photo-share or social-media-share metrics as the guarantee. Per BC-5879 ledger § Offer A fix #2, photo-share is gimmicky and disconnected from venue success. Guarantee metric must be venue-native: dwell time, attendance uplift in programmed hours, or sponsor-visibility impressions.
- Don't frame the pilot as a replacement of the incumbent. Frame as a "specialist add-on" or "complementary zone"; internal positioning with the existing vendor matters.
- Don't over-specify incumbent vendor type in body copy. Use "existing lighting vendor", "incumbent", or "current seasonal-program vendor", never "lantern vendor", "projection vendor", or any sub-category shorthand.
- Don't over-promise performance metrics beyond Labs' documented case studies. The guarantee is credible only if Labs can defend the specific threshold with real data.
- Avoid guarantee language that triggers nonprofit legal review ("refund in full", "zero-cost until delivered", "money-back"). Use outcome-gated framing: "case-study rights clause voids and you walk with the install".
- Don't name-drop Disney, Universal, or theme-park analogs. Stay within zoo-precedent anchors: Lincoln Park ZooLights, Columbus Zoo, Nashville Zoolumination, Hogle Zoo.
- Use "program" not "event" or "activation".
- Don't name species in prose (tigers, elephants, giraffes). Use "your collection", "your flagship exhibits", or the specific zone name.
- Don't name competitors in body copy (Tianyu Arts & Culture, Illuminight Lighting, Moment Factory, Limelight Art). T4 is Brite's own commitment, not a head-to-head pitch.
- Don't use urgency tactics. Pilot-zone decisions under committee scrutiny run on their own cadence; urgency signals vendor desperation.
- Don't cite Brite Nites seasonal residential case studies. Cite Labs experiential or capital installations only; residential analogs undercut the commercial T4 tone.
- Don't reference installers or property management. Supply-excluded per handbook canon.
- Frame S4 Lights as Brite's partner, not a competitor. S4 is the DMX-pixel component supplier; the supply-chain partnership underwrites the hardware assurance on a guarantee.
- Frame the commercial structure confidently, not supplicantly. The guarantee is Brite carrying the risk; the case-study rights exchange is the reciprocity, not a favor being asked.
