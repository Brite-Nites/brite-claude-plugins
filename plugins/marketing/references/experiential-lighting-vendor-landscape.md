# Experiential lighting vendor landscape

Brite-originated competitive reference for outbound marketing skills working on experiential-lighting prospects across verticals — zoos, aquariums, casinos, hotels, resorts, ski resorts, stadiums, theme parks, museums, botanical gardens, and adjacent venue types. Enumerates four vendor archetypes with named companies, typical project scope, commercial model, and what each does vs. doesn't compete on. Consumed by vertical playbooks (Phase 2 / R-4..R-9) and preset composition issues (Phase 4 / R-10..R-15) so future skill authors don't re-run the research.

This file is vertical-agnostic. Per-vertical program economics (ticket prices, attendance benchmarks), offer recommendations, and Brite pitch plays all live in `vertical-playbooks/*.md`, not here.

---

## Vendor archetypes

Four archetypes cover the vendor landscape an experiential-lighting prospect is likely to have already encountered or be actively evaluating. A prospect's current-vendor archetype (or lack of one) determines which Brite offer is the right wedge — see § How to use this reference.

### Lantern festival producers

Named companies: **Tianyu Arts & Culture** (dominant incumbent — Chinese-lantern festival producers; turnkey design-build-staff with rev-share or seasonal-lease model) and **Illuminight Lighting** (Chicago; Lincoln Park ZooLights vendor of record, 22 years). Illuminight straddles this archetype and the holiday-installer archetype below.

- **Typical project scope:** full-venue seasonal trail (Nov–Jan standard; summer Jul–Sep variants exist in warm climates), animated lantern sculptures, custom fabrication, on-site crew through the run.
- **Commercial model:** Tianyu = rev-share or seasonal-lease, turnkey, vendor-owns-IP — the venue rents the experience. Illuminight = mid-market fee-for-service with commercial LED strings + animated sculptures + arches/pole trees.
- **Serves:** venues that want a *ready-made narrative attraction* delivered end-to-end and are willing to trade IP ownership and programmable flexibility for execution certainty.
- **Does not serve:** venues that want programmable-media infrastructure, permanent architectural lighting, ad-monetization-compatible installs (static lanterns can't carry dynamic ad inventory), or net-new revenue partnerships where the venue retains upside.
- **Client examples cited:** Tulsa Zoo, Potawatomi Zoo, Maryland Zoo, Memphis Zoo, Saint Louis Zoo (Animals Aglow), Nashville Zoo (Zoolumination), Dallas Zoo (Illuminature), Columbus Zoo, Woodland Park (WildLanterns), Franklin Park (Boston Lights). Illuminight: Lincoln Park ZooLights. (All citations from BC-5879 zoos-ledger § Research grounding.)
- **Production cost anchor:** approximately $400K–$1M+ per season for Tianyu-tier installations (inferred from Oakland Glowfari's $100K tariff hit reported in trade press; full-production cost scales several multiples above the tariff line-item — inferred from BC-5879 session research).

### Projection / immersive studios

Named companies: **Moment Factory**, **Limelight Art**, **Christie Digital**.

- **Typical project scope:** projection-mapping at architectural scale, large-scale immersive installations, narrative-driven media experiences. IALD-award territory; flagship-marketing-asset-for-the-venue budget levels.
- **Commercial model:** fee-for-service at six- to low seven-figure per installation. Some studios carry content-refresh service contracts post-install.
- **Serves:** venues wanting *flagship storytelling attractions* that register as cultural events in their own right (Gardens by the Bay Rainforest Lumina, OdySea Aquarium 360° lobby — both cited in BC-5879 session research).
- **Does not serve:** mid-market budgets, operational-lighting needs (façade wash, wayfinding, pathway), or venues needing year-round architectural infrastructure. Projection gear is purpose-built for the installed experience and isn't repurposable.
- **Client examples cited:** Gardens by the Bay Rainforest Lumina (Moment Factory), OdySea Aquarium 360° lobby (citation from BC-5879 session research).

### Holiday specialty installers

Named companies: regional commercial holiday-lighting installers that often run year-round in commercial-landscape-lighting with a heavy seasonal pivot in Q4. **Illuminight Lighting** (Chicago) sits at the boundary of this archetype and the lantern-festival archetype with its Lincoln Park ZooLights work — cited in both. Specific national-brand names beyond Illuminight did not surface from the BC-5879 research agent; treat this category as regionally fragmented (inferred category from BC-5879 session research).

- **Typical project scope:** deploy–uplight–takedown operations, string lights, LED nets, lobby displays, exterior façade washes. Seasonal footprint (Nov–early Jan), light crew, minimal site-permanent infrastructure.
- **Commercial model:** per-event fee-for-service; install + remove + store is the core unit. Low barrier to entry, fragmented regional vendor base.
- **Serves:** venues wanting a *dependable seasonal refresh* at mid-market cost with minimal narrative ambition.
- **Does not serve:** venues wanting narrative / animated / programmable / ad-monetized experiences. Also does not serve venues that already have an incumbent holiday installer with a season of site-access knowledge — once a regional installer has learned the site, switching costs are meaningful (inferred from BC-5879 session research).

### LED-retrofit vendors

Named companies: **Goodlight** (UK), **Orphek**, **EcoMedia**.

- **Typical project scope:** back-of-house habitat lighting, functional LED conversion for energy efficiency, fixture replacement. Spec-driven, utility-code-aligned.
- **Commercial model:** product-sale (fixtures + retrofit services) with utility-rebate underwriting common. Different buyer (Facilities VP / energy manager) and different budget cycle (capital-replacement / energy-reduction) from the experiential-lighting buyer (Marketing / Development / Events).
- **Serves:** venues on energy-reduction or capital-replacement cycles who need to move kilowatt-hours down.
- **Does not serve:** experiential-attraction budgets, narrative-driven events, or any public-facing entertainment programming. Wrong buyer, wrong P&L line, wrong motion.
- **Marker — adjacent, not competitive:** these vendor names surface in general research on "lighting for [venue type]" but should NOT be treated as competitors to Brite's experiential-lighting motion. Included in this archetype list specifically so skill authors can recognize and set them aside.

## Explicitly adjacent, not competitive

Two vendor names surface frequently in general searches for commercial lighting at experiential venues and must be explicitly marked as off the competitive map. Any future skill treating either as a Brite competitor is reading the landscape upside-down.

**S4 Lights.** Component manufacturer — makes DMX-pixel light curtains, pixel trees, and programmable commercial-grade LED products. S4 is not a vendor competitor to Brite; it is a **Brite partnership** (per BC-5879 zoos-ledger § A3 case studies: "Brite is a partner with S4 (the DMX-pixel tree + light-curtain component manufacturer surfaced in the research)"). Brite sources from S4 when composing programmable-media installations and carries name-drop rights across the S4 venue portfolio. When S4 surfaces in prospect research, the correct framing is supply-chain credibility, not competitive overlap.

**MK Illumination.** Retail- and urban-streetscape decorative-lighting specialist. Wrong buyer (city BIDs, shopping-center operators) and wrong motion (decorative streetscape, not experiential-venue programming). Surfaces in general searches for "decorative lighting at scale" but does not overlap with Brite's experiential-venue Marketing / Development / Events motion.

## What Brite competes on

*(Synthesized from BC-5879 zoos-ledger § A1 Offer E decision + § Research grounding.)*

Brite's position within this landscape is **programmable-media infrastructure paired with production-finance orchestration** — not fabrication scale, not projection-studio prestige, not energy-retrofit spec work.

- **Architectural commercial programmable-media.** Color-controllable, ad-compatible, pixel-mapped installations (DMX-pixel trees and light curtains, sourced via S4 supply). Static lanterns can't carry dynamic ad inventory. Retrofit LEDs can't do narrative. Projection gear isn't repurposable year-round. Brite's tech stack is built to do narrative seasonal programming AND architectural year-round lighting AND ad-monetized dynamic content on the same infrastructure.
- **Production-finance orchestration.** Brite can finance installation against multi-stream pipeline revenue — title sponsor plus economic-development partner plus corporate-donor pipeline plus (year-2 onward) ad pre-commits. This is a capital model Tianyu can't match (turnkey-lease ties Tianyu to hardware depreciation, not upside) and fee-for-service vendors structurally won't offer. The finance layer itself is a credibility signal — Brite only underwrites when the numerator checks out.
- **Architectural commercial partnership with a delivered-at-a-zoo precedent.** Hogle Zoo (Salt Lake City, UT) is a confirmed Brite Labs delivery — AZA-accredited, fits Brite's existing UT footprint, single named anchor a prospect can Google. Combined with the S4 partnership (hardware supply secured, venue-portfolio name-drop rights), Brite answers the "can you execute at this kind of venue?" question with a real referenceable delivery.

## When Brite wins vs loses

*(Synthesized from BC-5879 zoos-ledger § A1 Offer E decision + § Research grounding.)*

**Wins when:**

- The venue wants architectural or gateway-level infrastructure that earns back across multiple uses (seasonal program + year-round facility value + sponsor-inventory surface).
- Ad-monetized programmable-media is part of the value proposition — sponsor-logo animations, brand-sync shows, scrolling content on pixel-mapped displays.
- Production-finance relieves a capex-committee bottleneck — the venue wants the program but can't defend the upfront line-item in a Q2 board meeting for a Q4 event.
- The venue values net-revenue partnership over a turnkey-expense-line vendor relationship, and is willing to share upside for shared downside.

**Loses when:**

- The venue wants full-venue lantern-festival fabrication scale — Tianyu's lane, Brite shouldn't chase. Delivery risk is loss-even-if-we-win.
- The venue has a deeply entrenched multi-year incumbent holiday installer plus a short decision window. (Offer A "$25K Pilot Zone" tactical complement may still apply — flag for the vertical playbook; don't lead outbound with the production-finance motion here.)
- The budget is strictly energy-retrofit or facilities-driven. Wrong buyer, wrong P&L, wrong motion.
- Projection-mapping narrative-studio budget levels (Moment Factory / Christie Digital lane). Different capability tree — Brite doesn't have the projection design-fabrication stack and shouldn't pretend otherwise.

Honest read: Brite wins on differentiated *commercial model and tech-stack*, loses on *fabrication-scale and narrative-studio prestige*. Pick motions that lean into the first pair and avoid head-on collisions with the second.

## How to use this reference

**For vertical playbooks (Phase 2 / R-4..R-9):** each vertical playbook distills this landscape against a specific venue type — zoos lens, aquariums lens, casinos lens, hotels-resorts lens, ski-resorts lens, sports-stadiums lens. When composing a playbook's § Vendor landscape section, pull from this reference and layer in per-vertical specificity (e.g., zoos have Tianyu-tier incumbent concentration; aquariums' humid tank galleries rule out outdoor lantern-fabrication entirely; casinos layer in projection-mapping precedent from the gaming-floor side of the house). Do not copy-paste this reference into a playbook — distill plus re-frame against the venue type.

**For preset composition (Phase 4 / R-10..R-15):** when composing `email-copywriting/presets/<vertical>/*.md`, cite the archetype the prospect's current vendor (if any) sits in, and frame Brite's positioning against that specific archetype. If the prospect has a Tianyu-tier lantern-festival incumbent, Offer E production-finance is the wedge (differentiated commercial model + programmable infrastructure). If the prospect has a holiday-installer-only incumbent with a short decision window, Offer A pilot-zone may apply (specialist add-on, not whole-program replacement). If the prospect has no current experiential-lighting vendor (greenfield), Offer E fits with minimal friction — no incumbent switching-cost drag.

**Anti-pattern flag:** if a preset or playbook treats S4 Lights or MK Illumination as a competitor, it has mis-read the § Explicitly adjacent, not competitive section above. Send back.
