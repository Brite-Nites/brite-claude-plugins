# Ski resorts vertical playbook

Brite-originated reference for all future ski-resorts marketing work — outbound email copywriting, account research, situation mining, creative-angle generation, TAM mapping. Distills the BC-5922 research grounding into a reusable playbook so future skill authors don't re-run the research. Consumed by `email-copywriting` preset composition (BC-5936), `tam-mapping` when ported (BC-5946 / BC-5950), and `situation-mining` when ported.

Vertical scope is **US regional mid-market independent ski resorts** (Vermont / Michigan / Montana / New York / New Hampshire) with branded villages — Boyne, Killington, Stowe, Okemo, Mount Snow, Jay Peak, Stratton, Sugarbush, Loon, Waterville Valley, Bretton Woods, Holiday Valley, Crystal Mountain MI, Whitefish, Big Sky, and peers in the 75K–800K skier-visits/year band. Enterprise flagships (Vail Resorts, Alterra, Aspen Skiing Co, Boyne corporate flagship properties run as RFP motion) are out of scope per BC-5922 issue directive — centralized procurement, brand-standards compliance, multi-property RFPs, 12–18 month cycles make them a fundamentally different motion than mid-market independent outbound. Enterprise flagships surface in this playbook as ceiling-scale references (Beaver Creek Thursday Night Wonder, Vail Village, Park City Historic Main Street) but are not ICP.

Ski-resort lighting is structurally different from zoos and from hotels (see [`zoos.md`](./zoos.md) and [`hotels-resorts.md`](./hotels-resorts.md) for peer playbook shapes). Zoos sell ticketed attendance against a sponsor-and-economic-dev stack; hotels sell rate-premium and booking-window extension against a revenue-management scorecard; ski resorts sell **village-dwell extension and après-ski F&B per-cap lift** against an ancillary-revenue scorecard where ancillary (F&B, retail, lessons, rentals) is 48% of resort revenue and F&B is more bad-snow-sensitive than lift revenue (−15.9% F&B vs lift rising in the 2024-25 early-season low-snow window — [Summit Daily](https://www.summitdaily.com/news/skier-visits-down-lift-revenue-up-at-vail-resorts-properties-for-2024-25-season/)). **Brite's zone is the village — not lift-line, not on-mountain operations.** The offer library, voice rules, and anti-slop list all reflect that boundary.

## Vendor landscape (ski lens)

The experiential-lighting vendor archetypes defined in [`../experiential-lighting-vendor-landscape.md`](../experiential-lighting-vendor-landscape.md) apply to ski resorts with two meaningfully different weightings than zoos or hotels: (a) the lantern-festival archetype is decisively absent at US ski villages (venue topology mismatch); (b) the projection/immersive archetype is a **primary ski-resort competitor**, inverted from the zoos-playbook framing where projection sits as "rare at zoos." See the inversion subsection below.

**Projection / immersive studios — primary competitor at ski resorts (inversion vs zoos framing).** Moment Factory has an established, SAM-endorsed beachhead at ski resorts with three named Lumina installations: **Vallea Lumina** at Whistler-Blackcomb (opened 2018, 1-mile night-walk through Cougar + Rainbow mountains old-growth forest, operates year-round — [Vallea Lumina](https://vallealumina.com/)); **Alta Lumina** at Les Gets in the French Alps (1-km pathway explicitly framed as "a diversification strategy to increase visitor offerings across all four seasons" — [Moment Factory Les Gets](https://momentfactory.com/news/alta-lumina-les-gets)); **Tonga Lumina** at Mont Tremblant (1.5-km pathway beginning with a Flying Mile chairlift ride, concluding in pedestrian village — [Tonga Lumina](https://tongalumina.tremblant.ca/en/)). Ski Area Management (the industry's primary trade publication) has editorially endorsed Moment Factory's Lumina format as a ski-resort summer/shoulder-season diversification solution ([SAM — Lumina Enchanted Night Walks](https://www.saminfo.com/page/lumina-enchanted-night-walks-immersive-year-round-experiences)). **Brite's differentiator from Moment Factory at ski is form-factor, not capability:** Moment Factory sells ticketed destination-pathway attractions (standalone venue inside the resort); Brite sells ambient village-integrated programmable infrastructure (embedded in F&B and retail row, daily-operations après-ski layer). Both compete for the same resort capital-allocation conversation, but on distinct wedges — the outbound frame must name the distinction explicitly or the prospect will default to "we already talked to Moment Factory." Christie Digital and Limelight Art surfaced no specific US ski-resort references in R-8 research.

**Holiday specialty installers — regional, fragmented, Colorado-concentrated named vendors.** Above All Holiday Lighting and Colorado Christmas Lights serve Vail / Beaver Creek / Avon / Bachelors Gulch ([Colorado Christmas Lights — Vail install](https://www.coloradochristmaslights.com/vail-holiday-christmas-lights-installation); [Above All Holiday Lighting](https://aboveallholidaylighting.com/who-we-serve)) with turnkey seasonal install — lights, labor, materials, lift rental, full-season service, takedown included. Static warm-white and multicolor strings; not programmable, not dynamic. Northeast (VT/NH/NY) and Midwest (MI) regional installers at ski-resort-scale did not surface by name in R-8 research; treat this tier as regionally fragmented commercial-holiday generalists with town-and-district tenure (inferred from BC-5922 research). Switching costs compound after one season of learned site access.

**Lantern-festival producers — decisively absent at US ski villages.** Tianyu Arts & Culture's public venue list names zoos / fairgrounds / botanical gardens / urban greenfield lots — no ski-resort venue cited ([Tianyu about](https://tianyuculture.us/about-us/); [Tianyu Dallas debut](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/tianyu-arts--culture-shines-bright-with-larger-than-life-lantern-festival-debut-in-dallas-302236686.html)). The structural reason: lantern-festival pathway format requires flat parkable 6–8-week footprints; ski-resort villages are steep, irregular, snow-bound. Illuminight / Illuminights / Lightscape run zoo + botanical-garden routes only. Zigong and Lantern Festival Company have no US ski-resort deployment. This is "more decisively adjacent than at zoos" — at zoos lantern-festival is a real competitor; at ski-resorts it's structurally off the map.

**LED-retrofit vendors — adjacent, same framing as zoos and hotels.** Goodlight / Orphek / EcoMedia serve Facilities buyers on energy-reduction cycles (base-lodge back-of-house, lift-terminal LED, parking-lot pole replacement). Wrong buyer (facilities, not village operations), wrong P&L (capex energy reduction, not revenue programming), wrong motion. Surfaces in "lighting for ski resorts" searches and must be set aside.

### Explicitly adjacent, not competitive (ski-specific)

Two vendor names require a ski-specific restatement of the vendor-landscape file's top-level guard, plus an explicit call-out that one of them (Moment Factory) has been **inverted** out of this subsection and into the primary archetype list above.

**Moment Factory — inversion from zoos framing; now primary, not adjacent.** The zoos playbook places Moment Factory inside "projection / immersive studios — rare at zoos." At US ski resorts that framing is wrong: Vallea Lumina / Alta Lumina / Tonga Lumina are real, SAM-endorsed, ski-resort-deployed Lumina installations. Any ski-vertical composition that lifts the zoos "rare at zoos" framing without re-grounding it for ski should be sent back. The correct ski framing is: **Moment Factory is the primary competitor at ski resorts**, with a distinct wedge (ticketed destination-pathway attraction) from Brite's wedge (ambient village-integrated programmable infrastructure). Second instance of the cross-vertical framing inversion pattern established by BC-5921 task-1 (hotels MK Illumination inversion) — see `docs/precedents/BC-5921.md` for the inversion mechanism.

**MK Illumination — adjacent at US ski villages; active-watch on European alpine expertise.** Unlike the BC-5921 hotels inversion (where MK IS a direct hospitality competitor), at US ski villages MK returns to the zoos-style adjacent-not-competitive framing. MK has deep European alpine-village Christmas-market practice anchored by its Seefeld (Tirol, Austria) account — illuminated since 2019, originated at the 52nd FIS Nordic World Ski Championships ([MK Illumination Global](https://www.linkedin.com/company/mk-illumination-global)): 3 km of Magic String Lites, 50,000 light points in trees, custom "SEEFELD" Tourism-Association-branded lettering, historic train-station lighting in partnership with Austrian Railway, ski-jumper and cross-country-skier sculptures, 2023-added animated whale installation in Kurpark. But Seefeld is a Tyrolean tourism village around a ski area, not a US base-village mid-market independent — and R-8 research surfaced **no evidenced US ski-resort base-village book of business for MK**. Classify MK as adjacent-not-competitive at US ski villages today with an **active-watch flag**: if MK enters the US ski-village market with a named account, reclassify. Any ski-resort preset composition that lifts the BC-5921 hotels "MK is a direct competitor" framing without US ski-village evidence should be sent back.

**S4 Lights — supply-chain partner, not competitor.** Same framing as at zoos and hotels. Brite is a partner with S4 (per BC-5879 zoos-ledger § A3 case studies — partnership surfaces S4 venue-portfolio name-drop rights and secures Brite's DMX-pixel hardware supply chain). When S4 surfaces in ski-resort prospect research, the framing is supply-chain credibility, not competitive overlap.

## Buyer personas

Four primary personas plus an optional fifth at larger mid-markets cover the decision surface at US regional independent ski resorts with branded villages. The persona set **departs from the BC-5922 issue body directive** ("VP Village Operations / Director of Guest Services / F&B Director") — R-8 research grounding confirmed that VP-titled Village Operations roles are enterprise-only (Mike Lewis Senior Director of Base Area and Village Operations at Park City before VP promotion, Vail Resorts — [SAM](https://www.saminfo.com/faces-and-places/new-leaders-named-at-vail-owned-resorts); Michael Rosenfeld Senior Director of Village Operations at Purgatory — [SAM Snow People Spring 2024](https://www.saminfo.com/faces-and-places/snow-people-spring-2024)). At VT/NH/NY/MI/MT regional independents (Brite's ICP), the dominant top title is **General Manager** — confirmed at Burke (Kevin Mack), Mad River Glen (Matt Lillard under 9-member co-op board), Ragged NH (Erik Barnes), Magic VT, Cannon NH (state-owned). Mis-naming the title in outbound reads as enterprise-VP copy-paste and is a tell. User gate-confirmed the research-backed set at pre-compose time. Documented here for downstream readers; full reasoning in `docs/precedents/BC-5922.md`.

**General Manager (GM) — primary decision-maker at regional independents.** Owns property-level P&L, signs vendor contracts, and at boutique and smaller regional properties routinely wears multiple hats (revenue, commercial, operations coordination). Cares about skier visits, ancillary-per-visit, F&B and retail tenant performance, compression-week revenue, shoulder-season amortization. Signs **Offer E (Village Production Finance)** as primary decision authority and authorizes **Offer A (Village Pilot Zone)** directly. Fears signing: unrecoverable capex without a defensible village-dwell or F&B-per-cap uplift story; owner-operator pushback on vendor spend that doesn't move ancillary revenue in a low-snow year.

**F&B Director / Director of Food & Beverage — secondary signer on F&B-activation offers.** Confirmed at Stowe (Chris Goss — [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/in/chris-goss-awakenedhospitality/)), Burke (Howard Wein F&B advisor per new ownership — [Unofficial Networks](https://unofficialnetworks.com/2025/06/03/burke-mountain-new-leadership/)), Steamboat (Christina Gumbiner). The per-cap / ancillary-uplift pitch lands here as the numerate evaluator who can quantify what a programmable village layer does to après-ski F&B spend, tenant retention, post-lift-close capture. GM will route the vendor deck to F&B Director for ancillary-math review before signing. Pitch F&B Director as co-primary at properties with dedicated village F&B operations (Spruce Peak at Stowe, Killington base village, Jay Peak village).

**Director of Sales & Marketing (DOSM) / Director of Sales & Guest Services — influencer and coalition-builder.** Midwest Family Ski Resorts' Amy Staven holds the combined "Sales & Guest Services" title ([RocketReach org chart](https://rocketreach.co/midwest-family-ski-resorts-management_b79aafdac56e6623)); the Sales & Marketing or Sales & Guest Services pattern is the real ICP analog to hotels' DOSM. Owns destination-marketing, direct-channel conversion, package mix, brand differentiation in the Indy Pass / IKON-adjacent regional comp set. DOSM is rarely the decision-maker but is critical for surfacing Offer E internally with compression-week booking-lift and direct-channel-mix framing. Avoid pitching DOSM as sole decision-maker — that's the enterprise-outbound mistake.

**Owner-Operator — final sign-off at family / local-investor-owned properties.** Real at Schaefer family (Berkshire East MA, now Burke VT via Bear Den Partners — [Hardwick Gazette](https://hardwickgazette.org/2025/05/13/new-burke-mountain-resort-owners-plan-major-upgrades/)), Killington's 2024 local-investor group (Phill Gross and Michael Ferri — [Seven Days VT](https://www.sevendaysvt.com/news/killingtons-new-owners-are-independent-local-minded-42306457/)), Kircher family (Boyne Resorts), Mad River Glen co-op board, POWDR's Cumming family. At sub-300K-visit properties below the "enough management layer to delegate vendor selection" line, the owner-operator signs vendor contracts personally. Cares about multi-year P&L of village programming, comp-set differentiation story, press-worthy programming upside, and (especially) legacy narrative for family / investor communications. When account research surfaces private-ownership or family-operated structure, pitch owner-operator as primary with GM as implementation partner.

**[Optional] Sr Director Base Area or Village Operations — primary operational evaluator at 300K+ visit properties with dedicated village real estate.** Exists at properties with substantial owned village infrastructure: Killington post-$3B village development (Safdie Architects + PWP Landscape Architecture master-planned 45 acres of dedicated village — [VermontBiz](https://vermontbiz.com/news/2025/january/19/killingtons-3-billion-expansion-aims-world-class-appeal-lasting-local-economic)), Stowe Spruce Peak, Big Sky, Jay Peak, Okemo Jackson Gore. At enterprise this is a VP title; at mid-markets it's Senior Director or Director. Not present at sub-300K properties; skip this persona at Burke, Ragged, Magic, Mad River Glen scale.

**Explicitly NOT a target — per BC-5922 issue directive and confirmed by R-8 research:** Director of Mountain Operations, Director of Skier Services, Mountain Manager, Ski Patrol Director, Lift Operations Director, VP Mountain Operations. Brite's wedge is the village, not lift-line or on-mountain operations. Outbound that targets **Lift Operations** reads as vendor fundamentally mis-understanding Brite's zone — it is the wrong persona, wrong P&L, wrong motion, and the fastest way to eliminate trust at a ski-resort prospect.

## Recency signals ski resorts leak publicly

Ski resorts leak signals through trade press (Ski Area Management / saminfo.com, SnowBrains, Unofficial Networks, Storm Skiing, Peak Rankings), NSAA announcements, regional newspapers (Vermont Business Magazine, Mountain Times VT, Valley News, Valley Reporter), chamber-of-commerce releases, and resort PR announcements. Eight recency signals matter; decay windows cross-reference [`../shelf-life-patterns.md`](../shelf-life-patterns.md).

**Ownership transitions + capital-planning windows** (decay: 12–24 months, strongest in first 6). The single highest-signal recency surface at ski resorts. Recent transitions — Killington + Pico → local-investor group 2024 ([Storm Skiing](https://www.stormskiing.com/p/powdr-to-sell-killington-pico-to)); Jay Peak → Pacific Group Resorts 2022 ($76M auction — [VTDigger](https://vtdigger.org/2022/09/16/as-judge-oks-76-million-sale-of-jay-peak-resort-receiver-says-burke-mountain-resort-could-soon-be-sold/)); Burke VT → Bear Den Partners 2025 (~$30M planned investment — [Hardwick Gazette](https://hardwickgazette.org/2025/05/13/new-burke-mountain-resort-owners-plan-major-upgrades/)); Ragged Mountain NH → SF Mountain Co. 2025 ([Valley News](https://vnews.com/2025/11/09/local-investors-buy-ragged-mountain/)). Post-acquisition 12–24 month window opens capital plans; Offer E lands well at properties 3–12 months into new ownership.

**New GM / new ownership appointments** (decay: 6–12 months, strongest in first 6). SAM's "Snow People" column tracks appointments and promotions monthly ([SAM Snow People April 2026](https://www.saminfo.com/faces-and-places/snow-people-april-2026)). New GMs run audit cycles in months 3–9 of tenure; outbound landing in that window fits the audit cadence. LinkedIn role-change alerts and resort PR announcements are the primary surfaces.

**Village expansions + base-lodge refreshes** (decay: 3–6 months for "new" framing; evergreen + annual refresh for multi-year programs). Killington's $3B 25-year village expansion is the tentpole — 1,095 acres total, 45 acres dedicated to village, 720+ ski-in/ski-out residences, two public squares designed as dining/retail/year-round hubs ([SAM — Killington approval](https://www.saminfo.com/news/sam-headline-news/killington-receives-key-approval-for-new-ski-village)). Killington K-1 base lodge $30M+ opened pre-2022. Watch SAM off-season (April–September) for lift replacements and village-construction announcements.

**Season-pass pricing announcements** (decay: annual, new pricing drops March–May). Epic Pass 2026-27 ~$1,089 (+3.6% YoY); Ikon $1,399 full / $949 base; **Indy Pass $369 flagship / $399 Indy+** (Indy reduced Indy+ pricing) ([SnowBrains Pass Wars](https://snowbrains.com/the-pass-wars-have-begun-breaking-down-prices-for-the-2026-27-ikon-epic-and-other-competing-mega-passes/)). **Indy Pass membership is the strongest independent-mid-market ICP proxy** — Indy Pass resort = independent mid-market by definition ([OnTheSnow Indy Buyer's Guide](https://www.onthesnow.com/news/indy-pass-buyers-guide/)). Cross-reference Indy's 300+ resort member list against target geography as a list-building heuristic.

**Village F&B tenant announcements** (decay: 3 months). New restaurant, bar, retail openings in village surfaced via regional newspapers (Vermont Business Magazine, Mountain Times VT), chamber-of-commerce announcements, resort PR. Tenant churn is a bad-health signal; new tenant signings are an ancillary-revenue-investment signal. (Inferred — specific sources vary by region.)

**Compression-week programming press** (decay: evergreen pattern, refresh annually). Resorts publish operating-hours and holiday-programming press releases annually in October–November for Thanksgiving week, Christmas–NYE window (Dec 20–Jan 1 at Boyne Mountain — [Boyne holiday week](https://www.boynemountain.com/upcoming-events/holiday-week)), MLK weekend, Presidents Week, Spring Break. Okemo's NYE Celebration & Fireworks at Jackson Gore Courtyard, Mount Snow NYE Torchlight Parade + Fireworks, Killington NYE at K-1 Base Lodge, Jay Peak tramside fireworks all run the same compression-week pattern ([Ski Vermont NYE](https://skivermont.com/new-years-celebrations)). Tells you the property has built a compression calendar around village programming.

**Bad-snow-year ancillary-revenue decline** (decay: 3–6 months; drives off-season procurement). The 2024-25 early-season low-snow window surfaced **ski school revenue −14.9% YoY, F&B dining −15.9% YoY** at Vail-owned properties while lift revenue rose ([Summit Daily](https://www.summitdaily.com/news/skier-visits-down-lift-revenue-up-at-vail-resorts-properties-for-2024-25-season/)). Low-snow seasons are when village-programming Offer E conversations become urgent at GM level — the ancillary scoreboard makes the case.

**NSAA season-end summaries** (decay: annual). 2024-25 US skier visits 61.5M (second-best on record), 492 operating ski areas, avg reinvestment $21.11/visit ([SAM — 61.5M Skier Visits](https://www.saminfo.com/news/sam-headline-news/u-s-ski-areas-report-61-5m-skier-visits-for-2024-25-second-best-season-on-record)). **Growth concentrated at small/medium areas per NSAA** — mid-market is the growth segment, which empirically confirms the Brite ICP exclusion of enterprise-only flagships.

## Program economics

Anchor numbers for ski-resort outbound work. Every number below is either public-source (cited) or inferred from BC-5922 research grounding (marked).

**Ancillary revenue share.** Lift revenue = 52% of total resort revenue; ancillary (F&B, retail, lessons, rentals) = 48%, four consecutive years at majority-of-revenue trajectory ([SAM — Take It All Into Account](https://www.saminfo.com/archives/2020-2029/2024/may-2024/take-it-all-into-account); [PeakRankings — Vail Resorts Financials FY24](https://www.peakrankings.com/content/vail-resorts-financials-unpacked-fiscal-year-2024)). Per-skier total revenue: **$60–$120** across North America and Europe (lift + lessons + rentals + F&B + retail). Season-pass holders represent 46% of lift revenue.

**Skier-visit ranges by ICP band.**
- Sub-75K visits/year: too small, limited village / limited F&B (Magic Mountain VT, Nub's Nob MI, Shanty Creek, Red Lodge) — long-tail owner-operator motion.
- 75K–300K visits/year: **core Brite ICP** — mid-market independents with branded villages (Burke, Ragged, Bretton Woods, Crystal Mountain MI, Whitefish, Windham, Belleayre, Jay Peak, Stratton, Waterville Valley, Loon).
- 300K–800K visits/year: large mid-market independents with robust villages and F&B tenants (Stowe, Killington, Okemo, Mount Snow, Sugarbush, Hunter, Boyne Mountain, Big Sky).
- 800K+: enterprise flagship ceiling — Breckenridge 1.7M ([Unofficial Networks resort stats](https://unofficialnetworks.com/2025/11/13/skiers-guide-to-resort-statistics/)) — ceiling references only, NOT ICP.

**Pass pricing pressure (2026-27).** Epic ~$1,089; Ikon $1,399 full / $949 base; Indy $369 / $399 ([SnowBrains](https://snowbrains.com/the-pass-wars-have-begun-breaking-down-prices-for-the-2026-27-ikon-epic-and-other-competing-mega-passes/)). Independent mid-markets compete against Epic/Ikon on destination experience and Indy Pass value, not on lift-capacity scale — this is the strategic frame for programming ROI conversations.

**Bad-snow-year sensitivity (the Brite-wedge numerical anchor).** Vail Resorts saw a **20% visitation drop during dismal Western snowfall**; 5 lowest-snow years 2001–2016 = 5.5M below-average skier visits across the industry ([Grist](https://grist.org/energy/ski-resorts-are-increasingly-reliant-on-snowmaking-but-at-what-cost/); [Bank of America Institute](https://institute.bankofamerica.com/sustainability/snow-trends-impact-on-ski-resorts.html)). The 2024-25 early low-snow window: **ski school −14.9% YoY, F&B −15.9% YoY, lift revenue rising** ([Summit Daily](https://www.summitdaily.com/news/skier-visits-down-lift-revenue-up-at-vail-resorts-properties-for-2024-25-season/)). Ancillary is more sensitive than lift; village programming is a snow-independent revenue lever and a relative-share gainer in low-snow years. This is the single most quantitatively-defensible reason a GM signs Offer E in a bad-snow October.

**Shoulder-season / summer amortization.** Steamboat Springs 35% of annual revenue from summer; Aspen ~$180M/year; Spirit Mountain ~33% gross ([SAM — State of Summer Operations](https://www.saminfo.com/archives/2020-2029/2025/january-2025/the-state-of-summer-operations)). 29% of resorts have mountain coasters, 34% zip lines, 21% ropes courses. One Northeast resort quoted: "The operating window for summer operations is profitable, however, when you layer in the shoulder seasons, summer becomes a break even." Mud-season (April–May) is the documented weak link — infrastructure re-programmable for summer/fall/mud-season converts holiday capex into year-round amortization.

**Installation-cost anchors (limited public data).** No US ski-village programmable-media installation cost publicly disclosed. Closest existing precedent: **Boyne Mountain SkyBridge "Lights in the Sky" — 200,000 individually-controllable LED nodes on a 1,203-ft timber-towered suspension bridge, Nov–April multi-season operation**, bundled with chairlift access ([Boyne SkyBridge](https://www.boynemountain.com/skybridge-michigan); [Spectrum News MI](https://spectrumlocalnews.com/mi/michigan/news/2025/12/11/skybridge-boyne-mountain-michigan)). Cost undisclosed; inferred high-six-figure to low-seven-figure install plus annual operating cost given scale. Moment Factory Lumina installations are inferred multi-million-dollar engagements at 1-km+ pathway scale (undisclosed).

**Ceiling anchors (use sparingly, always paired with mid-market).** Beaver Creek Thursday Night Wonder — weekly recurring larger-than-life art installations with musicians/performers/art walks ([Beaver Creek West](https://www.beavercreekwest.com/best-spots-to-see-holiday-lights-in-vail-and-avon/); [Beaver Creek 2021-22 release](https://news.beavercreek.vailresorts.com/mountain/beavercreek/resort-news/beaver-creek-announces-2021-22-winter-event-calendar.htm)) — strongest ceiling for village-recurring programmable installations. Vail Kris Kringle Market + 10th Mountain Legacy Parade + Tree Lighting. Park City 59th-annual Torchlight Parade at Canyons Village + Historic Main Street lamp-post lights.

## Good-vs-bad program taxonomy

Six dimensions distinguish compounding ski-village programs from burning-out ones. Derived from BC-5922 R-8 research grounding (SAM editorial + operator-facing trade press + Moment Factory + Boyne precedent).

**Programmable / dynamic vs static.** Good: Boyne SkyBridge 200K individually-controllable LED nodes, season-long re-programming, shoulder-season extension; Beaver Creek Thursday Night Wonder weekly-rotating art installations. Bad: static warm-white or multicolor strings installed via turnkey holiday-lighting service (Colorado Christmas Lights, Above All Holiday Lighting model), no programmability, no compounding across seasons, takedown every April. This is the single strongest predictor of village-programming longevity and cost-per-season efficiency.

**Snow-independent vs snow-dependent.** Good: village-integrated lighting works regardless of natural snow conditions (Boyne SkyBridge Nov–April regardless of snow quality; Spruce Peak Lights Festival village-center programming). Hedges the bad-snow-year ancillary decline. Bad: on-mountain-only installations requiring skiable terrain. Torchlight parades are beloved operator traditions but snow-dependent; Brite-class infrastructure should complement, not replace, torchlight.

**Season length.** Good: Nov through April (~6 months = full ski season plus shoulder); extend to May–October summer and you get year-round amortization on the same infrastructure (Moment Factory Vallea Lumina operates year-round). Bad: 6-week holiday-only windows (mid-November through New Year's), idle 10+ months. Most regional holiday-lighting-installer products.

**Village-traffic activation vs "pretty."** Good: measurable F&B / retail per-cap lift via dwell-time extension, one-way or looped crowd-flow design, proximity to village F&B tenants — Beaver Creek Thursday Night Wonder uses performers + art walks to move foot traffic through Village businesses. Bad: photo-backdrop installations that extract a photo and move visitors back to parking lots; zero per-cap lift, no F&B-tenant activation, no compounding tenant-retention signal.

**Compression-week amplification vs competition.** Good: programming that layers on top of existing compression-week anchors — NYE fireworks + torchlight + village programmable overlay = higher willingness to pay for premium lift tickets and F&B reservations. Okemo's NYE stack (music + fireworks + torchlight) is the template. Bad: programming that competes with compression-week anchors instead of amplifying them (another fireworks vendor the same night as the resort's own fireworks).

**Vendor tenure and site switching cost.** Good: multi-year accretive vendor relationships with YoY additions — MK Illumination Seefeld since 2019 with 2023-added animated whale installation; Moment Factory Vallea Lumina permanent since 2018. Bad: turnkey holiday-lighting installer-of-the-year rotations, no compounding narrative, no platform lock-in, every RFP starts from scratch.

## Offer candidates

Four offer structures surfaced from BC-5922 research + Zoos/Hotels pattern adaptation. Each evaluated through Hormozi value-equation and frontend/backend lens per [`../offer-design-frameworks.md`](../offer-design-frameworks.md). Statuses in § V1 offer picks below.

### Offer A — Village Pilot Zone (tactical complement)

**One-line statement.** Brite installs a single-zone programmable-media demonstration at a resort village with an incumbent holiday-lighting installer, underwriting the install cost in exchange for case-study footage, metrics, and resort-name usage rights.

**Hormozi mapping.**
- Dream Outcome: measurable proof-of-concept zone the resort can use to justify expanding the scope or replacing the incumbent at the next capital cycle.
- Perceived Likelihood: high on narrow scope (one pilot zone, one season) — failure is bounded, resort isn't betting the whole village.
- Time Delay: low (pilot install in 4–8 weeks, results measurable within one operating month).
- Effort + Sacrifice: very low (single-zone scope, Brite handles everything, resort contributes site access and measurement partnership).

**Frontend deliverable.** A rendered concept board (approximately 24"×36") for a specific pilot zone at the resort village, named by location (village plaza, base-lodge courtyard, après-ski plaza) and scope. Tangible artifact, not a visualization-required promise — apply the BC-5879 zoos-ledger Offer A fix #1 discipline.

**Backend commitment.** Pilot-zone install with a **village-native guarantee metric** — village dwell-time in the pilot zone (sensor / head-count), après-ski F&B per-cap lift in pilot-adjacent F&B operations, post-lift-close foot traffic in programmed hours. Not photo-share metrics — those are gimmicky and disconnected from resort success (per BC-5879 zoos-ledger Offer A fix #2).

**Commercial structure.** Brite underwrites install cost in exchange for case-study usage rights — footage, metrics, resort name. Phrased confidently: "We'll underwrite the install cost. In exchange, we use the results — footage, metrics, resort name — in our sales material." (Per BC-5879 zoos-ledger Offer A fix #3.)

**When it fits.** Resort has a long-tenured incumbent holiday-lighting installer AND a decision-maker (typically GM or F&B Director) open to a low-commitment test but won't move on a full-village replacement from a cold-outbound touch.

### Offer B — Permanent Village Backbone, Seasonal Dress (pending Brite Facilities sales-motion confirmation)

**One-line statement.** Year-round architectural programmable-media infrastructure on village gateway, plaza, or primary pedestrian pathway that doubles as seasonal-program entry and shoulder-season re-programmable surface — subscription-backed, Sr Director Base Area / Village Ops-owned or Owner-Operator-owned at properties with village real estate.

**Hormozi mapping.**
- Dream Outcome: year-round village programmable infrastructure upgrading everyday guest experience AND amortizing into holiday / shoulder-season programming without a second capital cycle.
- Perceived Likelihood: moderate — the dual-use framing is novel and requires Village Ops + F&B + DOSM coordination on a shared asset.
- Time Delay: high (capital cycle 12–24 months, installation 8–12 weeks).
- Effort + Sacrifice: moderate-high (capital commitment, cross-department coordination).

**Frontend deliverable.** A village-infrastructure site survey + year-round lighting design document with the seasonal overlay and shoulder-season programming already mapped.

**Backend commitment.** Subscription-model architectural install (multi-year contract) with scheduled holiday, shoulder-season, and summer programming deployments included. Brite retains infrastructure ownership; resort licenses year-round use.

**When it fits.** Property has a Sr Director Base Area / Village Ops title (300K+ visit properties with dedicated village real estate — Killington post-$3B, Stowe Spruce Peak, Big Sky, Okemo Jackson Gore), is in a capital-planning window (often triggered by ownership transition), and village site has a gateway or plaza ready for permanent infrastructure. **Pending Brite Facilities-VP equivalent sales-motion confirmation** (matches Zoos Offer B pending R-19 pattern).

### Offer D — Superseded by E

A production-orchestration variant without the multi-stream finance layer (Brite designs and runs the program for a flat fee; resort keeps ancillary revenue) was evaluated and folded into Offer E. Without the finance layer, the offer loses its core differentiator — near-zero resort capital commitment — and collapses into fee-for-service against Moment Factory's tier (which Brite is not competitively positioned to win on scale of ticketed-pathway fabrication). Retained here as a named entry so future readers don't re-propose it as a separate offer.

### Offer E — Village Production Finance (V1 primary)

**One-line statement.** Brite orchestrates multi-stream funding — local tourism / destination-marketing-organization partnership + F&B tenant co-marketing contribution + regional sponsor (outdoor apparel, regional bank, beverage) + ad revenue on DMX-pixel village displays — designs the programmable-media composition, runs the programming, and takes revenue-share on incremental village ancillary (F&B + retail per-cap lift vs prior-year baseline). Resort contributes near-zero capital.

**Hormozi mapping (Value Equation lever: Effort + Sacrifice dominant).**
- Dream Outcome: village programming that makes the resort money (not costs it); ancillary-per-visit lift + F&B tenant retention + shoulder-season amortization + ad revenue all built in. Snow-independent revenue lever hedges bad-snow years.
- Perceived Likelihood: proven by pipeline — sponsor pledges + tourism-bureau letter + F&B tenant co-marketing commitments surfaced BEFORE install. Finance itself is a credibility signal.
- Time Delay: 6–9 months resort-commit to program-live; year-2 shoulder-season re-programming upside.
- Effort + Sacrifice: resort near-zero capital, near-zero execution risk, operations limited to hosting and site access.

**Frontend deliverable.** A village-production-finance deck with a named tourism / destination-marketing partnership letter of intent, at least one anchor F&B tenant co-marketing commitment, a regional sponsor pledge, and a pro-forma revenue model for Year 1 (ancillary-per-visit lift + F&B attach + sponsor + ad-revenue line items, with a bad-snow-year downside case stress-tested at −15.9% F&B to show the snow-independent hedge math). The deck itself is the proof that Brite has done the multi-party close work, not just the design work.

**Backend commitment.** Full village-programming installation + season operation. Brite finances against pipeline revenue. Resort contributes site access, permits, operations hosting, F&B tenant introductions. Revenue-share on incremental village ancillary over a defined baseline (prior-year same-compression-window attach + per-cap).

**Tech stack anchor.** DMX-pixel light curtains + pixel trees + architectural façade washes (sourced from S4 Lights or equivalent component supplier). Pixel-mapped content including programmable ad inventory. Static lanterns can't carry dynamic ad inventory; Moment Factory projection installations aren't repurposable as village ambient infrastructure; LED retrofits don't do narrative. Brite's architectural-commercial-programmable-media stack is the only archetype on the vendor landscape that supports seasonal narrative AND year-round village ambient AND ad-monetized dynamic content on the same infrastructure.

**Why this beats A / B / D against Moment Factory.** Capital from Brite near-zero. Resort gets net-positive-revenue partnership, not expense line. Moment Factory's ticketed-pathway form factor requires resort capital commitment AND separate ticketing infrastructure; Brite's village-ambient form factor requires neither. Brite's edge isn't fabrication scale or projection-studio prestige; it's production-finance orchestration + ad-monetized programmable-media infrastructure integrated into existing village F&B / retail row.

**Open risks flagged (not dealbreakers, but real).**
- Sponsor + tourism-bureau close capability: Brite must demonstrate it can close regional sponsors and tourism-bureau partnerships, not just install lights.
- Tourism-bureau funding cycles are slow and political (6–18 months); Year 1 may not have this unlock.
- Ad revenue on village pixel installations is unproven at scale at ski resorts (zoo Lights ad inventory + hotel lobby digital signage as analogs).
- First resort is hardest — trust earned through relationships, not cold outbound; the outbound email has to carry this complexity without overwhelming.
- Snow-independent hedge math needs a real baseline — a prospect without reliable ancillary-per-visit reporting won't be able to verify the lift.

**Credibility anchors.** **Boyne Mountain SkyBridge "Lights in the Sky"** is the single strongest existing US mid-market precedent for permanent-programmable village-scale infrastructure (200K individually-controllable LED nodes, Nov–April multi-season — [Boyne SkyBridge](https://www.boynemountain.com/skybridge-michigan)); Brite's Offer E composes the village-ambient layer Boyne SkyBridge implies at bridge scale. **Killington's $3B 25-year village development** is a Brite-ICP-perfect named outbound target — local-investor-owned (2024), 45 acres of dedicated village designed by Safdie Architects + PWP, actively building programmable-ready village infrastructure from scratch. S4 Lights partnership — hardware supply secured, venue-portfolio name-drop rights.

## Voice rules

Six voice disciplines cumulative from BC-5922 issue body + R-8 research grounding + BC-5920 / BC-5921 transferable operator confirmations. All six apply to outbound email composition, account-research note-taking, and creative-angle generation consuming this playbook.

1. **Don't pitch Lift Operations.** The Brite zone is the village — après-ski, F&B, village dwell, post-lift-close capture. Lift Ops / Mountain Ops / Skier Services / Ski Patrol are wrong-persona every time; outbound that lands there reads as a vendor who hasn't understood where Brite's edge sits. Target GM primarily, F&B Director and DOSM as coalition, Owner-Operator at family / local-investor-owned properties. (Source: BC-5922 issue directive + R-8 persona research.)

2. **Don't anchor US ski outbound on Colorado or Utah enterprise flagships.** Vail, Deer Valley, Aspen, Park City, Whistler-Blackcomb, Beaver Creek, Breckenridge are ceiling references only — name them paired with a specific Eastern / Midwestern mid-market ICP anchor in the same sentence, never as standalone framing. Growth is at small/medium areas per NSAA; the ICP is independent regional mid-markets with branded villages. (Source: BC-5922 issue directive + NSAA 2024-25 data.)

3. **Don't frame as holiday-only.** Ski-resort village programming compounds over the **full ski season plus shoulder** (Thanksgiving through April standard; extend to summer and mud-season for year-round amortization). A holiday-only framing signals vendor who hasn't understood ski economics — the $60–$120 per-skier-visit revenue band is earned season-long, and the bad-snow-year F&B hedge value comes from multi-season operation, not November-December. Use "season-long programming" or "multi-season amortization" instead. (Source: BC-5922 issue directive + SAM summer-operations + Moment Factory four-season framing.)

4. **Describe the anti-slop discipline in terms of what TO say, not what NOT to say.** Voice rules that enumerate banned adjectives (see § Anti-slop rules for the full list) should be phrased at the discipline level — "lead with ancillary-per-visit economics, not warm-glow consumer-marketing language" — without quoting the banned phrases themselves. The anti-slop section is the one place banned phrases appear in-text (in "don't" bullets); elsewhere the voice discipline is modeled positively. (Source: BC-5920 task-3 literal-grep-trap precedent.)

5. **Use operator vocabulary, not consumer / ski-media vocabulary.** Operator terms (per-cap, ancillary, compression, après-ski, base area, shoulder season, skier visits, dwell time, Indy Pass / Epic / Ikon) signal that Brite has done the homework. Consumer / ski-media terms (bluebird day, powder day, fall-line, carving, corduroy, catch an edge) signal vendor trying too hard and erode the credibility the rest of the email is building. (Source: BC-5922 R-8 voice research — SAM trade-press vocabulary audit.)

6. **Lead with economic outcome, not aesthetic outcome.** Ancillary-per-visit lift, F&B tenant retention, village dwell-time extension, post-lift-close F&B capture, compression-week premium-pricing, shoulder-season amortization — not "beautiful lighting," "stunning visuals," "transforming the village." Economic outcome is the GM / F&B Director / DOSM / Owner-Operator vocabulary; aesthetic outcome is vendor-speak that signals Brite hasn't read the P&L. (Source: BC-5920 voice rule #5 generalized; R-8 confirms at ski.)

## Anti-slop rules

Five rules with ski-specific grounding. Rule 1 is the one place the banned consumer-marketing phrases appear in-text — the "don't" bullet format is the only section allowed to quote them (per BC-5920 task-3 literal-grep-trap precedent). Voice rules and prose elsewhere describe the discipline without quoting.

1. **No "magical" / "magic of the mountains" / "winter wonderland" / "alpine wonderland" / "enchanting snowscape" / "winter paradise" / "holiday charm" / "holiday magic" adjectives.** Consumer-marketing warm-glow language that zeroes out credibility on an ancillary-per-visit / compression-economics / bad-snow-hedge buyer. If the programming matters, the numbers describe it (dwell-time extension, F&B per-cap lift, compression-week premium). If the numbers aren't there, adjectives don't substitute. (Source: BC-5922 issue directive + Brite-voice discipline extension.)

2. **No ski-technical jargon mis-use.** "Fall-line", "catch an edge", "bluebird day", "powder day", "freshies", "gnarly", "carving", "corduroy" are consumer and ski-media vocabulary. Operator trade-press (SAM, NSAA, Ski Area Management) does not use them in business-of-skiing contexts. A vendor using this vocabulary signals trying-too-hard; the GM / F&B Director reads it as pretending to be an insider. (Source: BC-5922 R-8 voice research.)

3. **No stadium / concert-rig language.** "Ring of steel", "truss and rig", "FOH tower", "mothership", and similar concert-production vocabulary positions Brite as the wrong vendor type. Ski-resort villages aren't concert venues; the framing collapses credibility. (Source: BC-5920 anti-slop #1 — transfers to ski.)

4. **No generic "ROI uplift" without a named ski-specific metric.** Ski-resort economics are specific: ancillary-per-visit, F&B tenant retention rate, village dwell time, post-lift-close foot traffic, compression-week premium, shoulder-season amortization ratio, bad-snow-year ancillary-decline delta. Generic "ROI" or "returns" phrasing signals Brite hasn't done ski-vertical homework. Use the specific metric the GM or F&B Director tracks on the ancillary scoreboard. (Source: BC-5922 R-8 + BC-5920 anti-slop #4 generalization.)

5. **Lead with ancillary-per-visit economics; destination-branding / press-worthiness lives in paragraph 2+, not the hook.** Destination-branding, four-season diversification narrative, and press-worthy legacy are real and owner-operator-relevant, but opening cold outbound with destination-branding narrative reads as agency-speak to a GM who is measured on ancillary-per-visit on the Monday after Christmas-NYE. Open with the ancillary economic outcome (bad-snow F&B hedge, compression-week per-cap premium, village dwell extension) and introduce destination-branding as a supporting beat once the economic frame has landed. (Source: BC-5920 anti-slop #5 — transfers to ski.)
