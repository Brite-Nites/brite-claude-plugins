## Vendor landscape (zoos lens)

The experiential-lighting vendor archetypes defined in [`../experiential-lighting-vendor-landscape.md`](../experiential-lighting-vendor-landscape.md) apply to zoos with a vertical-specific weighting. This section filters those archetypes through zoo-specific evidence and calls out which archetype dominates which zoo segment.

**Lantern festival producers — dominant at zoos.** Tianyu Arts & Culture has cornered the large-AZA-zoo outdoor-lights segment: named zoo clients include Tulsa Zoo, Potawatomi Zoo, Maryland Zoo, Memphis Zoo, Saint Louis Zoo (Animals Aglow), Nashville Zoo (Zoolumination), Dallas Zoo (Illuminature), Columbus Zoo, Woodland Park (WildLanterns), and Franklin Park (Boston Lights) (inferred from BC-5879 session research). Production cost sits in the $400K–$1M+ per season band for Tianyu-tier installations (inferred from BC-5879 session research, anchored to the Oakland Glowfari $100K tariff line-item reported in trade press). Illuminight Lighting (Chicago) holds the Lincoln Park ZooLights vendor-of-record slot and has for 22 years — a meaningful segment of mid-market zoos will name Illuminight-style regional operators as their incumbent.

**Projection / immersive studios — rare at zoos, more common at aquariums.** Moment Factory, Limelight Art, and Christie Digital serve flagship-storytelling attractions (Gardens by the Bay Rainforest Lumina; OdySea Aquarium 360° lobby — both cited in BC-5879 session research). When a zoo hires at this tier it's a capital event — zoo-wide press, board-level decision, six- to low-seven-figure budget. Uncommon but not impossible; worth flagging in account research.

**Holiday specialty installers — present at smaller zoos, regionally fragmented.** Regional commercial holiday-lighting vendors run year-round commercial-landscape-lighting with a heavy Q4 pivot. They handle deploy–uplight–takedown on string lights, LED nets, façade washes. At smaller or mid-market zoos they're the incumbent by default. Specific national brand names beyond Illuminight did not surface from the BC-5879 research agent; treat this category as regionally fragmented (inferred category from BC-5879 session research). Switching costs compound after a season — the installer has learned the site, the staging area, the electrical runs.

**LED-retrofit vendors — adjacent, not competitive.** Goodlight (UK), Orphek, EcoMedia serve back-of-house habitat lighting, exhibit tank lighting, capital-replacement energy-reduction cycles. Facilities VP / energy manager buyer, utility-rebate underwriting, spec-driven sales motion. They surface in "lighting for zoos" searches but do not overlap with Brite's experiential-lighting motion (wrong buyer, wrong P&L line, wrong motion).

### Explicitly adjacent, not competitive (zoos-specific)

Two vendor names surface frequently in zoos-vertical research and must be marked off the competitive map. This mirrors the vendor-landscape file's top-level guard but restates it in the zoos context.

**S4 Lights.** DMX-pixel light-curtain and pixel-tree component manufacturer. Brite is a partner with S4 (per BC-5879 zoos-ledger § A3 case studies — the partnership surfaces the S4 venue-portfolio name-drop rights and secures Brite's hardware supply chain for programmable-media installations). When S4 surfaces in zoos prospect research, the framing is supply-chain credibility, not competitive overlap. Any future playbook or preset treating S4 as a Brite competitor should be sent back as mis-reading the landscape.

**MK Illumination.** Retail- and urban-streetscape decorative-lighting specialist (shopping-center operators, city BID districts). Wrong buyer, wrong motion, wrong P&L. Surfaces in general "decorative lighting at scale" searches but does not overlap with zoos experiential-venue programming. If a future composition names MK as a Brite competitor in zoos outbound, send back.

## Buyer personas

Five personas cover the decision surface at a typical AZA-accredited US zoo. Each has a distinct remit, a distinct P&L authority, and fits a distinct Brite offer. The offer-to-persona mapping matters more than the titles themselves — small zoos collapse these into two or three people, large zoos fragment them further, but the authority-to-offer mapping holds.

**CEO / President.** Board-facing leader; owns institutional strategy, fundraising narrative, and multi-year capital plans. Cares about attendance + mission fulfillment + sponsor-pipeline health + press-worthy programs. Signs **Offer E (production finance)** when the pitch arrives with a title-sponsor pledge already framed and a city/destination-marketing-bureau partnership angle named (inferred from BC-5879 § A1 Offer E decision). Fears signing: capital commitment to an unproven program, reputational risk from vendor underperformance at a public-facing event.

**Executive Director.** At smaller AZA-accredited zoos (sub-$30M operating budget), the CEO role is titled Executive Director and operates the same decision surface. Same offer-persona mapping as CEO. Flag when doing account research: if the zoo has an Executive Director title instead of CEO, it's likely smaller-budget and the Offer E sponsor-pipeline math needs to fit the smaller scale.

**Director of Corporate Partnerships.** Sponsor-inventory buyer; owns the title-sponsor, presenting-sponsor, and activation-sponsor pipeline. This persona lights up when precedent is cited verbatim — Invesco QQQ and ComEd at Lincoln Park ZooLights, CPS Energy at San Antonio Zoo, TXU Energy in broader Texas venue programs, Meijer at Cleveland Metroparks Zoo (all cited in BC-5879 session research). These are 5-to-7-figure title-sponsor commitments and every zoo's Director of Corporate Partnerships has a pipeline document that looks like those deals. Offer E's sponsor-finance story lands here; they are the coalition-builder who co-sells Offer E to the CEO.

**Marketing Director.** Event-attendance funnel buyer; owns ticket-revenue projections, membership-acquisition campaigns, press-worthy imagery. Cares about hotel-room-nights (Denver Zoo economic-impact study: 85.1% net-new economic activity, 16,502 hotel-room-nights/yr attributable — cited in BC-5879 session research) and membership conversion (LA Zoo: 170K event attendees, 70% over goal — cited in BC-5879 session research). Not the direct P&L authority for Offer E capital commitments but a critical coalition-builder who gets the deck in front of the CEO with momentum.

**Facilities VP / Director of Operations.** Year-round infrastructure buyer; owns architectural lighting, pathway lighting, permanent site infrastructure. Offer B (Permanent Backbone, Seasonal Dress) buyer when the zoo has year-round architectural ambition and a capital cycle ready to fund it. Pending R-19 confirmation on whether Brite's Facilities-VP sales motion is a funded go-to-market — until that lands, Offer B stays pending and this persona is secondary.

## Recency signals zoos leak publicly

Zoos are public institutions — many are 501(c)(3) nonprofits or municipal entities — and their planning cycles, announcements, and role changes are discoverable from trade press, city council agendas, IRS Form 990s, and zoo PR releases. Six recency signals matter. Decay windows below inform whether a signal still has shelf-life. Cross-reference [`../shelf-life-patterns.md`](../shelf-life-patterns.md) for the decay framework.

**Program announcements** (decay: 3–6 months). Trade press coverage (Blooloop, IAAPA trade publications) announcing Nov–Jan holiday light programs or Jul–Sep warm-climate summer variants (Cleveland Metroparks Zoo runs summer — inferred from BC-5879 session research). Zoo PR releases announcing new program names, vendor changes, or expanded footprints. Shelf life: a program announced 6+ months ago is live; a program announced 2 weeks ago is too recent for Offer E outreach (decision already made for the upcoming season).

**Capital-plan votes** (decay: 12–24 months). City council agenda items approving zoo capital expansion, bond-measure approvals, capital-campaign public launches. These signal multi-year planning cycles; Offer B (Permanent Backbone, Seasonal Dress) slots into capital-plan windows but Offer E's sponsor-finance model sometimes undercuts the need for capital.

**Role changes** (decay: 6–12 months). New Director of Corporate Partnerships, new CEO, new Executive Director, new Director of Development. New hires run audit cycles in months 3–9; outbound landing in that window fits the audit cadence. LinkedIn role-change alerts and zoo PR announcements are the primary surfaces.

**Ticket-on-sale dates** (decay: window-of-use, not decay). Public announcement of program pricing and dates tells you the program cost tier ($18–$31 adult outdoor; $50–$60 aquarium 21+), the length of the run (30 nights standard; Cleveland variants longer), and the scale the zoo has underwritten. Cross-reference with § Program economics for benchmark fit.

**Economic-impact study publication** (decay: 24+ months; high signal, rare). Denver Zoo and LA Zoo both publish economic-impact reports; the Denver study's 85.1% net-new-activity figure and 16,502 hotel-room-nights anchor (inferred from BC-5879 session research) signal that the zoo has an existing tourism-bureau partnership and an economic-development lens already established. Offer E's city economic-dev funding channel becomes dramatically more plausible at these zoos.

**Sponsor-pipeline announcements** (decay: 6 months). Corporate donor newsletters, sponsor-hall-of-fame pages on zoo websites, annual-report sponsor lists. Tells you who the Director of Corporate Partnerships is already closing. If Invesco QQQ or CPS Energy are already in the sponsor stack, Offer E's title-sponsor story has direct precedent to cite verbatim.

## Program economics

Anchor numbers for zoos outbound work. Every number below is either public-source (cited) or inferred from BC-5879 session research (marked).

**Ticket price ranges.**
- Outdoor zoo holiday lights: $18–$31 adult, $15–$22 child (inferred from BC-5879 session research).
- Premium lantern festivals (Tianyu-tier): $22–$30 adult (inferred from BC-5879 session research).
- Family-of-four value ceiling observed: approximately $100 before churn rises sharply (inferred from BC-5879 session research; TripAdvisor review-volume signal).

**Program length.** ~30 nights, Nov–Jan standard. Summer variants Jul–Sep at warm-climate zoos (Cleveland Metroparks Zoo — inferred from BC-5879 session research).

**Production cost.**
- Tianyu-tier full-venue outdoor trails: $400K–$1M+ per season (inferred from BC-5879 session research, anchored to Oakland Glowfari $100K tariff line-item from trade press).
- Mid-market regional installer: $150K–$400K per season (inferred from BC-5879 session research; Illuminight-tier operators).
- Projection studio installation: six- to low-seven-figure per install (inferred from BC-5879 session research).

**Attendance benchmarks.**
- LA Zoo event attendance ceiling: 170K attendees in a single month, 70% over the zoo's planning goal (inferred from BC-5879 session research).
- Cleveland Metroparks Zoo summer program: 105K attendees (inferred from BC-5879 session research).
- Lincoln Children's Zoo winter program: 77K attendees (inferred from BC-5879 session research).
- Cumulative program attendance at long-running programs: Columbus Zoo 37-year tenure, Lincoln Park ZooLights 30-year tenure, Cleveland 1M+ cumulative attendees (inferred from BC-5879 session research).

**Sponsor economics.** Title-sponsor and presenting-sponsor commitments land in the 5-to-7-figure band. Named precedents: Invesco QQQ at Lincoln Park, ComEd at Lincoln Park, CPS Energy at San Antonio Zoo, TXU Energy in Texas venue programming, Meijer at Cleveland Metroparks Zoo (all cited in BC-5879 session research). A single title sponsor can underwrite a meaningful fraction of Tianyu-tier production cost; Offer E stacks multiple sponsor streams against the production math.

**Economic-impact anchor.** Denver Zoo published an 85.1% net-new economic activity figure and 16,502 hotel-room-nights/yr attributable to their program (inferred from BC-5879 session research). This is the ceiling citation for the economic-development funding channel — when a zoo's city or destination-marketing bureau hears "hotel-room-nights," the Denver precedent is the anchor.

## Good-vs-bad program taxonomy

Five dimensions distinguish a program that compounds (YoY attendance growth, longevity, sponsor retention) from a program that burns out (TripAdvisor complaints, attendance decay, sponsor churn). All five derived from BC-5879 session research grounding (TripAdvisor review signal + trade press coverage).

**Static vs dynamic.** Uplit trees, static LED nets, and decorative stringing age poorly and produce a "been there" effect after year two — bad. Animated, kinetic, narrative-driven installations produce YoY refresh momentum and compounding press coverage — good. This is the single strongest predictor of program longevity.

**Crowd flow.** One-way loop paths with clear pacing work — good. Two-way paths with uncontrolled flow become crowd-management disasters at >1K simultaneous attendees and generate the majority of one-star reviews (inferred from BC-5879 session research). Zoos that added holiday lights without redesigning crowd flow often shut programs down within 2 seasons.

**Value ceiling.** Family-of-four ticket spend above ~$100 pushes the program out of mass-market reach; above ~$120 it becomes a corporate-outing market only (inferred from BC-5879 session research). Tianyu-tier pricing at $22–$30 adult with kids $15–$22 holds just under this ceiling for a family of four. Above the ceiling the program converts fewer members to attendees.

**Animal-welfare integration communicated upfront.** Dallas Zoo Illuminature, Columbus Zoo, and Nashville Zoolumination all publish explicit animal-welfare notes upfront (lighting schedules that respect habitats, sound thresholds, exhibit exclusions) — good. Programs that skip this framing lose press momentum when welfare questions surface mid-season.

**Year-over-year refresh vs raw scale.** The strongest programs aren't the biggest; they're the longest-running with meaningful YoY changes. Columbus Zoo's 37 years of Wildlights and Lincoln Park's 30 years of ZooLights compound because each season adds new zones, new lanterns, or new installations while retaining the signature silhouette (inferred from BC-5879 session research). Programs that chase raw scale without YoY refresh plateau and decay.

## Offer candidates

Four offer structures surfaced from BC-5879 research + ledger decisions. Each evaluated through Hormozi frontend/backend lens per [`../offer-design-frameworks.md`](../offer-design-frameworks.md). Statuses in § V1 offer picks below.

### Offer A — Pilot Zone (tactical complement)

**One-line statement.** Brite installs a single-zone programmable-media demonstration at a venue that already has an incumbent lighting vendor, underwriting the install cost in exchange for case-study footage, metrics, and venue-name usage rights.

**Hormozi mapping.**
- Dream Outcome: a measurable proof-of-concept zone that the venue can use to justify expanding the incumbent scope or replacing them at the next capital cycle.
- Perceived Likelihood: high on narrow scope (one pilot zone, one season) — failure is bounded; the venue isn't betting the whole program.
- Time Delay: low (pilot install in 4–8 weeks; results measurable within one operating month).
- Effort + Sacrifice: very low (single-zone scope, Brite handles everything, venue contributes site access and the measurement partnership).

**Frontend deliverable.** A rendered concept board (approximately 24"×36") for a specific pilot zone at the venue, named by location and scope. Tangible artifact, not a visualization-required promise (per BC-5879 zoos-ledger § Offer A fix #1).

**Backend commitment.** Pilot-zone install with a **venue-native guarantee metric** — dwell time in the pilot zone (sensor / head-count), attendance uplift in programmed hours (gate-scan data), or sponsor-visibility impressions if sponsor branding is present on the pilot zone. Not photo-share metrics — those are gimmicky and disconnected from venue success (per BC-5879 zoos-ledger § Offer A fix #2).

**Commercial structure.** Brite underwrites install cost in exchange for case-study usage rights — footage, metrics, venue name. Phrased confidently, not supplicantly: "We'll underwrite the install cost. In exchange, we use the results — footage, metrics, venue name — in our sales material." (Per BC-5879 zoos-ledger § Offer A fix #3.)

**When it fits.** Venue has a long-tenured incumbent lighting vendor AND a decision-maker (typically Marketing Director or Director of Corporate Partnerships) who is open to a low-commitment test but won't move on a full-program replacement from a cold-outbound touch.

### Offer B — Permanent Backbone, Seasonal Dress (pending R-19)

**One-line statement.** Year-round architectural programmable-media infrastructure on gateway / plaza / primary pathway that doubles as seasonal-program entry — subscription-backed, Facilities-VP-owned.

**Hormozi mapping.**
- Dream Outcome: year-round architectural lighting that upgrades the venue's everyday experience AND amortizes into seasonal programming without a second capital cycle.
- Perceived Likelihood: moderate — the dual-use framing is novel and requires Facilities and Marketing to coordinate on a shared asset.
- Time Delay: high (capital cycle is 12–24 months; installation 8–12 weeks).
- Effort + Sacrifice: moderate-high (capital commitment, Facilities-procurement cycle, cross-department coordination).

**Frontend deliverable.** An architectural-lighting site survey + year-round lighting design document with the seasonal-programming overlay already mapped.

**Backend commitment.** Subscription-model architectural install (multi-year contract) with scheduled seasonal program deployments included. Brite retains infrastructure ownership; the zoo licenses year-round use.

**When it fits.** Facilities VP buyer, zoo in a capital-planning window, site has a gateway or plaza ready for permanent infrastructure. Pending R-19 confirmation on whether Brite's Facilities-VP sales motion is a funded go-to-market.

### Offer D — Superseded by E

Original Offer D was a production-orchestration variant without the sponsor-finance layer — Brite designs and runs the program for a flat fee, venue keeps ticket revenue. The BC-5879 zoos-ledger § A1 decision folded D into E because without sponsor-finance orchestration the offer loses its core differentiator (near-zero venue capital commitment) and collapses into a fee-for-service motion Brite isn't competitively positioned for against Tianyu. D is retained here as a named entry so future readers understand the fold-in and don't re-propose it as a separate offer.

### Offer E — Production Finance (V1 primary)

**One-line statement.** Brite orchestrates multi-stream funding — title sponsor + city economic-development partner + zoo-side corporate-donor pipeline + ad revenue on DMX-pixel displays — designs the composition and ad stack, runs the show, and takes revenue-share on net. Venue contributes near-zero capital.

**Hormozi mapping (from BC-5879 zoos-ledger § A1 verbatim).**
- Dream Outcome: seasonal program that makes the zoo money (not costs it); attendance + sponsor inventory + ad revenue + city partnership all built in.
- Perceived Likelihood: proven by pipeline (sponsor pledges + city letter + ad pre-commits before install).
- Time Delay: 6–9 months venue-commit to program-live; year-2 renewal default upside.
- Effort + Sacrifice: venue near-zero capital, near-zero execution risk, operations limited to hosting.

**Frontend deliverable.** A production-finance deck with a named title-sponsor pledge, a city or destination-marketing-bureau partnership letter of intent, and a pro-forma revenue model for year one (attendance + sponsor + ad-revenue line items). The deck itself is the proof that Brite has done the sponsor-close work, not just the design work.

**Backend commitment.** Full-program installation + operation. Brite finances against pipeline revenue. Venue contributes site access, permits, operations hosting. Revenue-share on net.

**Tech stack anchor.** DMX-pixel light curtains + pixel trees (sourced from S4 Lights or equivalent component supplier). Pixel-mapped content including ad inventory — the programmability is the differentiator. Static lanterns cannot carry dynamic ad inventory; projection installations aren't repurposable year-round; LED retrofits don't do narrative. Brite's architectural-commercial-programmable-media stack is the only archetype on the vendor landscape that supports all four requirements simultaneously.

**Why this beats A / B / D.** Capital from Brite near-zero (removes the balance-sheet question that sinks most cold-outbound vendor pitches). Venue gets net-positive-revenue partnership, not expense line. Tianyu can't match (lanterns are static, can't carry ad inventory, vendor-owns-IP model prevents venue monetization). Brite's edge isn't fabrication scale; it's production-finance orchestration + ad-monetized programmable-media infrastructure (from BC-5879 zoos-ledger § A1).

**Open risks flagged (not dealbreakers, but real) — from BC-5879 zoos-ledger § A1.**
- Sponsor-sales capability: Brite must demonstrate it can close sponsors, not just install lights.
- City economic-dev funding cycles are slow and political (6–18 months); year-1 may not have this unlock.
- Ad revenue on pixel installations is unproven at scale at zoos (stadium jumbotron + mall digital signage as analogs).
- First venue is hardest — trust earned through relationships, not cold outbound; the outbound email has to carry this complexity without overwhelming.
- Multi-revenue-stream rev-share accounting requires infrastructure (ticketing integration, sponsor accounting, ad reconciliation).

**Credibility anchors.** Hogle Zoo (Salt Lake City, UT) — confirmed Brite Labs zoo delivery, AZA-accredited, fits Brite's existing UT footprint (from BC-5879 zoos-ledger § A3). S4 Lights partnership — hardware supply secured, name-drop rights across S4's venue portfolio once the customer list is confirmed (BC-5939 retrieves the list; BC-5940 confirms the Hogle program specifics).

## Voice rules

Five voice disciplines cumulative from BC-5879 zoos-ledger + Tier-A-and-beyond operator confirmations. All five apply to outbound email composition, account-research note-taking, and creative-angle generation consuming this playbook.

1. **Don't over-specify incumbent vendor type in body copy.** Use "existing lighting vendor", "incumbent", or "current seasonal-program vendor" — never a specific sub-category shorthand that surfaced from the vendor-landscape research (lantern-festival producer, projection-studio operator, LED-retrofit installer). Research-anchored specificity reads as regurgitation; general framing reads as understanding the landscape. (Source: BC-5879 zoos-ledger §124 — operator correction 2026-04-21.)

2. **Don't name-drop enterprise entertainment venues as analogs.** Disney, Universal, and large theme-park operators read as wrong-category to a zoo CEO; the analog falls flat because zoo economics and buying cycles are fundamentally different. Stay within zoo-precedent anchors — Lincoln Park ZooLights, Columbus Zoo, Nashville Zoolumination, Hogle Zoo. (Source: BC-5920 issue body.)

3. **Lead with Operations / Development / CEO; Marketing is a secondary touch.** The Offer E P&L authority sits with Operations, Development, CEO, or Director of Corporate Partnerships — not Marketing. Marketing Directors are coalition-builders who can surface the deck internally but rarely sign capital commitments. First-touch sequencing should target CEO + Corporate Partnerships primarily; Marketing as a parallel or secondary touch. (Source: BC-5920 gate confirmation 2026-04-22.)

4. **Use "program" not "event" or "activation."** Zoos operate on multi-season programs with year-over-year refresh logic, not one-off events. "Program" signals understanding of the long-horizon economics; "event" or "activation" signals agency-speak and misreads the buying cycle. (Source: BC-5879 research grounding — Columbus 37-year, Lincoln Park 30-year program longevity.)

5. **Lead with economic outcome, not aesthetic outcome.** Hotel-room-nights, sponsor-pipeline health, membership conversion, attendance — not light quality, design beauty, visitor delight. Economic outcomes are the CEO's and Corporate Partnerships Director's vocabulary; aesthetic outcomes are vendor-speak that signals Brite hasn't done the homework. (Synthesized from BC-5879 § A1 Dream Outcome definition + § Real buyer motivations.)

## Anti-slop rules

Five rules cumulative from BC-5920 issue body + operator gate confirmations 2026-04-22.

1. **No stadium / concert-rig language.** "Ring of steel", "truss and rig", "FOH tower", "mothership", and similar concert-production vocabulary positions Brite as the wrong vendor type. Zoos aren't concert venues; the framing collapses credibility. (Source: BC-5920 issue body.)

2. **No species name-dropping.** Don't pepper prose with "tigers", "elephants", "giraffes", or specific species names to signal on-topic engagement. Zoo directors know what lives at their zoo; vendor name-drops read as trying too hard and erode the credibility the rest of the email is building. Refer to "your collection", "your flagship exhibits", or the specific public-program name if the zoo has one. (Source: BC-5920 issue body.)

3. **No "magical" / "wondrous" / "enchanting" / "mesmerizing" adjectives.** Consultant-speak that zeros out credibility on an attention-economy / economic-outcome buyer. If the program matters, the numbers describe it. If the numbers aren't there, adjectives don't substitute. (Brite-voice discipline extension from BC-5879 zoos-ledger operator voice rule.)

4. **No generic "ROI uplift" without named metric.** Zoo economics are specific (hotel-room-nights attributable, membership-conversion rate, per-cap spend, ticket-revenue delta, sponsor-inventory sell-through). Generic "ROI" or "returns" phrasing signals Brite hasn't done zoo-vertical homework. Use the specific metric the zoo's Director of Corporate Partnerships or CFO tracks. (Source: BC-5879 research grounding — Denver economic-impact study precedent.)

5. **Lead with economic outcome; conservation / AZA framing lives in paragraph 2+, not the hook.** Conservation framing is real and board-relevant at AZA-accredited zoos, but opening a cold outbound email with mission-speak from a vendor reads as performative to a CEO or Corporate Partnerships Director who fields dozens of vendor pitches. Open with the economic outcome (hotel-room-nights, sponsor-pipeline, attendance) and introduce conservation / mission alignment as a supporting beat once the economic frame has established credibility. (Source: BC-5920 gate confirmation 2026-04-22 — softened from the original "no AZA framing as opener" to a sequencing rule.)

Both are explicitly named in § Vendor landscape — Explicitly adjacent, not competitive as off the competitive map. Silent omission fails the fresh-reader test; named exclusion is the guard.
