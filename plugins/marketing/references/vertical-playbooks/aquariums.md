# Aquariums vertical playbook

Brite-originated reference for all future aquariums-vertical marketing work — outbound email copywriting, account research, situation mining, creative-angle generation, TAM mapping. Distills the BC-5929 research grounding into a reusable playbook so future skill authors don't re-run the research. Consumed by `email-copywriting` preset composition (BC-5933), `tam-mapping` when ported (BC-5946 / BC-5950), and `situation-mining` when ported.

Vertical scope is mid-market / independent US aquariums ($10M–$80M operating budget, 200K–1M annual visitors). Enterprise-flagship aquariums (Georgia Aquarium, Monterey Bay Aquarium, Shedd Aquarium, New England Aquarium, Seattle Aquarium) are out of ICP — architectural-lighting-design-firm incumbents + multi-year capital cycles + 8-figure program budgets make them a fundamentally different motion than cold outbound. Enterprise flagships surface in this playbook as ceiling-scale references (e.g., Georgia Glow Nights attendance, Shedd Jellies by Lightswitch, Seattle Ocean Pavilion by HLB) but are not prospects.

Aquariums are structurally different from zoos (see [`zoos.md`](./zoos.md) for peer shape), from hotels (see [`hotels-resorts.md`](./hotels-resorts.md) for the indoor-F&B peer), and from ski resorts (see [`ski-resorts.md`](./ski-resorts.md) for the shoulder-season revenue-diversification peer). Zoos sell family-daytime holiday lights against sponsor-pipeline economics; hotels sell peak-weekend ADR lift against revenue-management scorecards; ski resorts sell off-peak ancillary attach against compression-calendar economics; aquariums sell 21+ after-hours adult-event revenue ($39.95–$59.99 ticket range) + summer Glow Nights + holiday overlay against revenue-diversification economics. Tank lighting is husbandry-controlled — architectural-gallery-only is the Brite zone. Offer library, voice rules, and anti-slop list differ in material ways from all three peer verticals.

## Vendor landscape (aquariums lens)

The four baseline archetypes defined in [`../experiential-lighting-vendor-landscape.md`](../experiential-lighting-vendor-landscape.md) apply with one addition (architectural-lighting-design firms — aquariums-unique) and meaningfully different weighting than zoos / hotels / ski. This section filters the baseline archetypes through aquarium-specific evidence + names the fifth archetype that surfaces at aquariums but not in peer verticals.

**Lantern-festival producers — explicitly excluded at aquariums.** Tianyu Arts & Culture, Hanart Culture, and Zigong Lantern Group all have portfolios dominated by zoos, botanical gardens, theme parks, and outdoor fairgrounds. Zero named US aquarium deployments ([Tianyu about](https://tianyuculture.us/about-us/)). Three compounding exclusion mechanisms: (a) high ambient humidity in tank galleries degrades fabric and paper lantern substrates; (b) enclosed tank-room footprints don't accommodate sprawling outdoor lantern paths that zoos and botanical gardens use; (c) Tianyu's own vertical-case-study pages list zoos, botanical gardens, theme parks, and sports stadiums — no aquariums. The exclusion is mechanical, not just rare — any future playbook or preset re-proposing Tianyu at aquariums should be sent back.

**Projection / immersive studios — rare at US aquariums, capital-anchored when present.** OdySea Aquarium's 360° lobby is the one named US aquarium projection install — built by Malvern Entertainment (creative) + Christie Digital (technology; 12 HS-series projectors, 282,000 lumens) + CCS (integration) ([Christie Digital OdySea spotlight](https://www.christiedigital.com/spotlight/odysea/), [InPark Magazine](https://www.inparkmagazine.com/odysea-aquarium-new-lobby-experience/)). Christie Digital also powered Xpark Aquarium in Taiwan ([rAVe Pubs](https://www.ravepubs.com/heres-how-projection-mapping-transformed-the-xpark-aquarium/)). Projection-mapping at aquariums is capital-heavy lobby-scoped installation — not programmable seasonal overlay. Budget anchored at $1M+ per install; ICP is flagship-only. Prior session research previously misattributed OdySea's lobby to Moment Factory — correction documented here and in § Explicitly adjacent, not competitive below to prevent future authors from carrying the error forward.

**Holiday specialty installers — thinner presence at aquariums than at zoos.** Most aquarium holiday overlays are produced in-house — Georgia Aquarium's 40-ft live tree + twinkle program ([Holidays at Georgia Aquarium](https://www.georgiaaquarium.org/holidays-at-georgia-aquarium/)); Maritime Aquarium at Norwalk's Flurry Zone + Shark-Diving Santa ([Maritime Aquarium newsroom](https://www.maritimeaquarium.org/newsroom/posts/where-snow-falls-indoors-and-santa-swims-with-sharks-holidays-at-the-maritime-aquarium/)); Adventure Aquarium's Christmas Underwater with Scuba Santa ([Adventure Aquarium](https://www.adventureaquarium.com/explore/special-events/christmas-underwater/)) — or contracted to local commercial-lighting generalists. Illuminight-tier regional installers surface occasionally but lack named aquarium-specific case studies. The mid-market holiday-installer archetype is thinner at aquariums than at zoos.

**LED-retrofit vendors — adjacent, not competitive.** Goodlight (UK), Orphek, EcoMedia surface for habitat-gallery retrofit work but buy Facilities / husbandry / energy-reduction dollars — wrong P&L, wrong buyer, wrong motion. They appear in general "lighting for aquariums" searches but do not overlap with Brite's architectural-gallery-overlay motion.

**Architectural-lighting-design firms — aquariums-unique 5th archetype.** Humidity-rated exhibit-and-gallery lighting specialists with named US aquarium credits are a genuine aquarium-specific archetype that does not surface at zoos, hotels, or ski. Named firms: **Lightswitch** (Shedd Aquarium Jellies + Amphibians + Polar Play Zone + At Home on the Great Lakes + Fantasea — five named exhibits — [Lightswitch Shedd portfolio](https://lightswitch.net/stories/view/lightswitch-architectural-sheds-new-light-on-the-shedd)); **Fisher Marantz Stone** (Georgia Aquarium — all five galleries — [FMS Georgia Aquarium](https://www.fmsp.com/projects/georgia-aquarium/)); **CD+M Lighting** (Georgia Aquarium 4-D theater, lantern wall, plaza, ballroom ceiling — [CD+M portfolio](https://cdmlight.com/index.php/portfolio-item/georgia-aquarium/)); **Horton Lees Brogden** (Seattle Aquarium Ocean Pavilion — 50,000 sq ft, LEED Gold, opened 2024 — [HLB Seattle Ocean Pavilion](https://hlblighting.com/project/seattle-aquarium-ocean-pavilion/)); **Available Light** (museum / experiential-environment specialist since 1992 — [Available Light](https://www.availablelight.com/)). Typical project scope: humidity-rated exhibit + architectural-gallery lighting design-and-spec, integrated with marine-biology husbandry policies. Commercial model: design-and-spec fee-for-service at $50K–$300K range, often partnering with AV integrators or commercial-lighting installers for delivery. **Serves:** flagship / capital-campaign aquariums on multi-year exhibit-refresh cycles. **Does not serve:** mid-market independents on seasonal-program budgets (capex mismatch) — which is precisely Brite's ICP opening.

## Buyer personas

**The persona set departs from the BC-5929 issue body directive** ("VP Revenue / Director of Adult Programs / Director of Operations / Director of Guest Experience"). R-5 research grounding confirmed VP-titled Revenue roles are enterprise-only and do not exist at the 200K–1M-visitor mid-market independent target. "Director of Adult Programs" also did not surface at any researched mid-market aquarium — adult programming at mid-market lives under Marketing / Events or Guest Experience as a portfolio, not a standalone directorate. Director of Operations AND Director of Guest Experience ARE real at mid-market — confirmed at Newport Aquarium KY (Michael Craig and Kristopher Thomas respectively) — and are retained. User gate-confirmed the research-backed set at pre-compose time 2026-04-23.

Six personas cover the decision surface at mid-market / independent US aquariums. Small aquariums collapse these into three people; flagship aquariums fragment further — the authority-to-offer mapping holds.

**Executive Director / President & CEO. Primary decision-maker.** At many mid-market independents this role is dual-hatted as "VP & Executive Director" — confirmed at Newport Aquarium KY with Rebecca Foster and Adventure Aquarium NJ with Jennifer Porter ([Adventure Aquarium press release](https://www.adventureaquarium.com/media/adventure-aquarium-names-jennifer-porter-new-vice-president-executive-director/)). Owns institutional P&L at the property level, board-facing, signs vendor contracts, owns fundraising narrative + multi-year capital plans. Cares about mission-fulfillment + attendance + revenue diversification + sponsor-pipeline health. Signs **Offer 1 (Glow-Native 21+ Revenue-Share)** directly; co-signs **Offer 2 (Production Finance)** with Director of Corporate Partnerships. Fears signing: capex without defensible revenue return, reputational risk from vendor underperformance at a public-facing program.

**Director of Marketing & Admissions Revenue. Co-primary at properties with the role** (typical at 200K+-visitor aquariums). Title often lives as a combo role (Marketing + Admissions + Revenue merged) — confirmed at Adventure Aquarium NJ with Jeff Leinen. Owns ticket-revenue projections, event-attendance funnel, direct-channel mix (.com vs OTA), membership-acquisition campaigns, press-worthy imagery. The 21+ after-hours-revenue pitch lands here first — they are the numerate buyer who can quantify Glow-Native program economics against install cost. Routes the deck to Executive Director / CEO for signature after internal evaluation. Pitch co-primary; let them become the internal champion.

**Director of Corporate Partnerships. Sponsor-inventory buyer** — owns the title-sponsor + presenting-sponsor + activation-sponsor pipeline. At aquariums, this persona lights up when Offer 2 precedent is cited verbatim — GEICO presents YuleTides at Florida Aquarium ([Florida Aquarium YuleTides](https://www.flaquarium.org/news-events/attend-an-event/calendar/yuletides/)); UPMC Health Plan presents Wild Illuminations at Pittsburgh Zoo & Aquarium ([Wild Illuminations](https://www.pittsburghzoo.org/event/wild-illuminations/)); Abita Brewing sponsors Scales & Ales at Audubon New Orleans ([Audubon Scales & Ales](https://audubonnatureinstitute.org/scales-ales)). Sponsor economics typically 5-to-6-figure per partner. Offer 2's title-sponsor story lands here; they co-sell to Executive Director.

**Director of Development. Fundraising-adjacent; owns donor-pipeline, capital-campaign communications.** Surfaces when Offer 2's multi-stream funding model requires capital-campaign coordination, or when the aquarium is in a capital-renovation cycle (Seattle Ocean Pavilion 2024 precedent). Not the decision-maker for seasonal-program offers but a coalition partner for multi-year commitments and architectural-program sponsor-pipeline work.

**Director of Operations. Year-round infrastructure buyer** — owns architectural lighting + pathway / facade + permanent exhibit infrastructure. At Newport Aquarium KY this is Michael Craig. Offer 3 (Permanent Canvas, Offer B-analog) buyer pending R-19 confirmation on Brite's Facilities-motion readiness. Until that lands, Director of Operations is secondary and Offer 3 stays deferred.

**Director of Guest Experience. Coalition-builder on event-program delivery** — owns exhibit flow, ticketing, F&B, merchandise. At Newport Aquarium KY this is Kristopher Thomas. Cares about crowd management through narrow tank-gallery corridors, F&B attach per-cap, member vs walk-up conversion. Not the P&L signer but a critical veto — if Guest Experience flags a program as operationally unsupportable (crowd bottleneck, welfare risk, F&B overflow), the Executive Director won't override.

## Recency signals aquariums leak publicly

Aquariums leak signals through trade press (Blooloop, IAAPA, AZA publications), annual-impact reports, capital-campaign press, sponsor announcements, and program PR. Six recency signals matter. Decay windows cross-reference [`../shelf-life-patterns.md`](../shelf-life-patterns.md).

**Program announcements** (decay: 3–6 months). Press releases and trade coverage announcing Glow Nights (summer) / Night Dive (year-round adult) / Aquarium After Dark 21+ / Voyages adult chapters / Christmas Underwater (holiday overlay) / Sea of Lights / Aquarium Aglow programs. Shelf life: a program announced 6+ months ago is live and qualifying; a program announced 2 weeks ago is too recent for Offer 1 outreach (decision already made for the upcoming season).

**Capital campaigns + renovations** (decay: 12–24 months). Seattle Aquarium's 50,000 sq ft Ocean Pavilion opened 2024 (LEED Gold — [HLB Seattle Ocean Pavilion](https://hlblighting.com/project/seattle-aquarium-ocean-pavilion/)) is the flagship-tier precedent; mid-market analogs include exhibit-refresh campaigns at SC / Virginia / Oregon Coast. Signals multi-year planning cycles; Offer 3 (Permanent Canvas) slots into capital-plan windows pending R-19.

**Role changes** (decay: 6–12 months). New Executive Director / President & CEO / Director of Marketing & Admissions Revenue / Director of Corporate Partnerships. Jeffrey Flocken taking the Aquarium of the Pacific helm Jan 2026 ([blooloop](https://blooloop.com/aquarium-pacific-new-ceo-president/)) is the archetypal example — new hires run audit cycles in months 3–9 of tenure; outbound landing in that window fits the audit cadence. LinkedIn role-change alerts + aquarium PR announcements are the primary surfaces.

**Ticket-on-sale dates** (decay: window-of-use, not decay). Public announcement of 21+ after-hours event pricing ($39.95–$59.99 range) and dates tells you the program cost tier + run length + scale the aquarium has underwritten. Newport After Dark $59.99 (7:30–11:00pm, food stations + animal meet-and-greets — [Newport After Dark](https://www.newportaquarium.com/things-to-do/aquarium-events/aquarium-after-dark-21-and-over/)) and Aquarium of the Pacific Night Dive $44.95 general / $39.95 member (7:30–11:00pm with DJs + food trucks — [Tripster Night Dive](https://www.tripster.com/travelguide/event/night-dive-at-aquarium-of-the-pacific/)) are the anchor data points. **Aquarium of the Pacific is in Long Beach, CA** — not Newport Beach (common confusion with Newport Aquarium KY).

**Economic-impact study publication** (decay: 24+ months; high signal). Aquariums publish impact studies more frequently than zoos. Named precedents: **National Aquarium Baltimore** — $430M statewide economic activity, $36M state / local tax, 3,400 jobs in 2024 ([2024 Impact Report](https://aqua.org/about/reports-and-financials/mission-impact)); **Virginia Aquarium & Marine Science Center** — $250M annual economic benefits ([2024 Annual Report](https://virginiaaquarium.com/annual-report-2024)); **SC Aquarium** — $378M projected regional spending impact for Charleston region + 30K out-of-town visitors ([Post & Courier](https://www.postandcourier.com/business/study-finds-sc-aquarium-drawing-more-visitors-and-economic-impact/article_7e26c4c2-fa3f-11ed-a18a-eb82d631b62c.html)); **Great Lakes Aquarium** — ~200K annual out-of-area visitors spending ~$30M on lodging / dining ([UMD BBER study](https://conservancy.umn.edu/items/51ad7326-7d31-4c6c-90d0-28c3da06dd35)). Offer 2's city / CVB funding channel becomes dramatically more plausible at aquariums with published impact studies.

**Sponsor-title announcements** (decay: 6 months). Named title sponsors surface in aquarium press releases, program pages, and annual reports. GEICO / YuleTides, UPMC / Wild Illuminations, Abita / Scales & Ales are live precedents to cite verbatim when pitching Offer 2 to a Director of Corporate Partnerships.

## Program economics

Anchor numbers for aquariums outbound work. Every number below is either public-source (cited) or inferred from BC-5929 research grounding (marked).

**Ticket price ranges.**
- 21+ after-hours: **$39.95–$59.99 adult** (Newport After Dark $59.99 all-inclusive food + meet-and-greets; Aquarium of the Pacific Night Dive 18+ $44.95 general / $39.95 member with DJs + food trucks at additional cost). Mid-market independents cluster at the low-to-mid of this band ($39.95–$49.99); premium-inclusive models hit the $59.99 ceiling.
- Holiday overlay: **$10 stand-alone** (Oregon Coast Aquarium Sea of Lights — [Sea of Lights](https://aquarium.org/visit/calendar/sea-of-lights/)) to **included with general admission** (Georgia / Maritime / Adventure). Most mid-market aquariums bundle holiday overlay rather than ticketing separately — a different model than zoo holiday lights.
- Fundraiser gala with open bar: **$100+** precedent range (Audubon Scales & Ales sponsor / patron VIP tiers).

**Program length.** 21+ after-hours: typically 3–3.5 hours, evening only (7:30–11:00pm standard). Summer Glow Nights / Night Dive: one night weekly or dated-event monthly. Holiday overlay: 4–8 weeks (Nov 14–Jan 2 Georgia pattern).

**Production cost estimates.**
- Architectural-lighting-design firm engagement (Lightswitch Jellies pattern): **$50K–$300K** design-and-spec range (inferred from peer museum / exhibit fees).
- Programmable-media LED overlay (Brite's zone): **$75K–$250K** install range scales to footprint (inferred from peer hotel / zoo mid-market ranges).
- Projection install (OdySea-caliber): **$1M+ capex** per install, one-of-one, flagship-tier only — out of scope for Brite's mid-market ICP.
- Lantern fabrication: **excluded** — humidity + footprint + portfolio evidence preclude (see § Vendor landscape above).

**Attendance benchmarks.**
- SC Aquarium Aglow: **28,000+ visitors 2024** (10K YoY growth from Year 1 — [Aquarium Aglow](https://scaquarium.org/aquariumaglow/)).
- Audubon Scales & Ales: **$1.4M raised since 2010** across 14 editions (~$100K / yr net average — [Audubon Scales & Ales](https://audubonnatureinstitute.org/scales-ales)).
- Great Lakes Aquarium: **~200K annual out-of-area visitors → ~$30M lodging / dining spend** (inferred from UMD BBER study).
- Newport After Dark typical event scale: **500+ guests per event × ~12 events / year → ~$360K gross 21+ ticket revenue** (inferred from Newport $59.99 × attendance ranges common at 21+ after-hours).

**Named sponsor precedents (title-level).**
- **GEICO presents YuleTides at Florida Aquarium** ([Florida Aquarium YuleTides](https://www.flaquarium.org/news-events/attend-an-event/calendar/yuletides/)).
- **UPMC Health Plan presents Wild Illuminations at Pittsburgh Zoo & Aquarium** ([Wild Illuminations](https://www.pittsburghzoo.org/event/wild-illuminations/)).
- **Abita Brewing sponsors Scales & Ales at Audubon New Orleans** ([Audubon Scales & Ales](https://audubonnatureinstitute.org/scales-ales)).

**Economic-impact anchor.** Aquariums publish impact studies more frequently than zoos — **National Aquarium Baltimore's $430M statewide economic activity + 3,400 jobs + $36M tax** ([2024 Impact Report](https://aqua.org/about/reports-and-financials/mission-impact)) is the strongest national-scale anchor; **SC Aquarium's $378M regional spending impact** and **Great Lakes Aquarium's $30M out-of-area spend** are the mid-market anchors. When pitching Offer 2 to a Director of Corporate Partnerships, the city / CVB partnership argument has direct precedent.

## Good-vs-bad program taxonomy

Five dimensions distinguish programs that compound (YoY attendance growth, sponsor retention, membership uplift) from programs that burn out (TripAdvisor complaints, attendance decay, ownership cancellation).

**Dynamic tank-overlay vs static ambient wash.** Good: programmable DMX-pixel overlay on tank-fronting walls, lobby architecture, gallery ceilings, and facade that responds to seasons and events (summer Glow Nights hardware redeploys for winter Holiday overlay; same infrastructure, different content). Bad: static Christmas-lights-around-tank-frames that don't change YoY — become "been there" by Year 2.

**One-way path flow vs bottleneck.** Aquarium corridors are narrower than zoo pathways — tank-viewing geometry forces linear flow by design. Good programs design for one-way aquarium-native flow (Shedd Jellies gallery's one-way traverse is the archetypal example); bad programs create collision points at high-traffic tank entries and generate review complaints.

**Glow-native vs retrofit-holiday framing.** Good: bioluminescence / UV / jellies content that aquariums already market natively — Glow Nights (Georgia summer bioluminescence framing), Night Dive (Aquarium of the Pacific summer adult series), Aquarium After Dark 21+ (Newport all-season). Bad: Christmas-lights-on-fish-tank retrofit that reads derivative and misses the aquarium's native content narrative.

**Tank-welfare respecting vs disturbing.** This is a **non-negotiable hard constraint**. Good programs sync architectural-gallery lighting schedules to animal-welfare husbandry policies (overlay ends when staff-designated lights-out starts; jellies are light-sensitive; coral and kelp have circadian cycles) and **never leak light into tanks**. Bad programs treat tanks as programmable surfaces and infringe on husbandry. **Brite's zone is architectural-gallery-only — tank lighting is husbandry-controlled and out of Brite's engineering scope.** Any proposed installation that touches tank lighting gets sent back.

**YoY refresh vs one-and-done.** Good programs use programmable-media hardware that swaps content themes annually (summer Glow Nights → winter Holiday overlay → spring fundraiser-gala overlay → autumn Voyages adult series — four windows on the same infrastructure). Bad programs deploy single-use lantern arrays that require full rebuild year-over-year and plateau by Year 2 when the "new" framing collapses.

## Offer candidates

Three offer structures surface from BC-5929 research grounding + cross-vertical peer-playbook structure. Each evaluated through the Hormozi frontend / backend lens per [`../offer-design-frameworks.md`](../offer-design-frameworks.md). Status per § V1 offer picks below. **Do not lift zoos offers verbatim** (per BC-5929 issue Non-Goal) — aquarium economics are 21+ adult-event revenue + architectural-gallery overlay, not family-daytime ticket + sponsor-pipeline at zoo scale.

### Offer 1 — Glow-Native 21+ Revenue-Share (V1 primary)

**One-line statement.** Brite installs humidity-rated programmable-media architectural overlay on tank-fronting gallery walls, lobby architecture, and facade for 21+ after-hours adult events (Glow Nights summer / Night Dive year-round / Aquarium After Dark), underwriting install cost in exchange for revenue-share on incremental 21+ ticket revenue + F&B attach over prior-year baseline.

**Hormozi mapping.**
- Dream Outcome: recurring adult-ticket revenue + F&B / bar attach that compounds YoY across summer Glow Nights + winter Holiday overlay + spring fundraiser + autumn adult series windows.
- Perceived Likelihood: high — Newport After Dark $59.99, Aquarium of the Pacific Night Dive $44.95, Audubon Scales & Ales $1.4M-since-2010 prove the 21+ format works at mid-market; Brite programs overlay hardware already proven at peer venues.
- Time Delay: low (install 4–8 weeks; revenue visible at first programming event, typically 3–4 months out).
- Effort + Sacrifice: low — Brite handles hardware + humidity-rated spec + programming; aquarium owns event ops + bar + marketing + husbandry approvals.

**Frontend deliverable.** A rendered concept board (24"×36") of the named 21+ after-hours zone (typically tank-adjacent gallery + lobby + facade) + a 12-month programming calendar showing how the same hardware supports Glow Nights (summer) + Holiday overlay (winter) + spring fundraiser + autumn adult series — four windows, one infrastructure. Tangible artifact, not visualization-required promise.

**Backend commitment.** Pilot or full-scope install with a **revenue-native guarantee metric** — incremental 21+ after-hours ticket revenue over prior-year baseline clears a pre-agreed floor across Year 1, measured via aquarium ticketing-system reports (cleanest data source aquariums have). Follows BC-5932 task-2 first-season-breakeven-or-no-pay pattern: install fee not billed until the revenue baseline is met. Not photo-share or vanity metrics — those miss the Marketing & Admissions Revenue Director's scorecard.

**Commercial structure.** Brite underwrites install cost against 3–5 year programmable-media lease + revenue-share on incremental 21+ revenue (similar to zoos Offer E but keyed to adult-event revenue-stream rather than sponsor-pipeline). Phrased confidently: "We underwrite the install. Year-1 incremental 21+ revenue clears the baseline or you don't pay the install fee. After that, we share the upside."

**When it fits.** Aquarium already runs any 21+ programming (even quarterly). Existing Glow Nights / Night Dive / After Dark programming proves market fit; Brite's overlay amplifies. **Does not fit when:** aquarium has no adult-programming history — too much market-creation lift for a first install.

### Offer 2 — Production Finance E-analog (tactical-secondary)

**One-line statement.** Brite orchestrates multi-stream funding — title sponsor + city / CVB partnership + ad-inventory on DMX-pixel displays + aquarium-side corporate-donor pipeline — designs the composition and ad stack, runs the programming, and takes revenue-share on net. Aquarium contributes near-zero capital.

**Hormozi mapping.**
- Dream Outcome: seasonal program that pays for itself via title-sponsor + CVB + ad revenue; attendance + sponsor inventory + economic-impact narrative all built in.
- Perceived Likelihood: proven by pipeline precedent (GEICO / YuleTides, UPMC / Wild Illuminations, Abita / Scales & Ales) + economic-impact-study anchors (National Baltimore $430M, SC $378M, Virginia $250M).
- Time Delay: 6–9 months aquarium-commit to program-live; year-2 renewal default upside.
- Effort + Sacrifice: higher than Offer 1 — multi-stakeholder sell requires sponsor-relationship infrastructure on Brite's side; aquarium contributes site access + operations hosting only.

**Frontend deliverable.** A production-finance deck with a named title-sponsor pledge framed + city / CVB partnership letter of intent + pro-forma revenue model citing the three named anchors (sponsor precedent, economic-impact baseline, ad-inventory projection). The deck itself is proof Brite has done the sponsor-close work, not just the design work.

**Backend commitment.** Full-program installation + operation. Brite finances against pipeline revenue. Aquarium contributes site access, permits, operations hosting. Revenue-share on net. Tech-stack anchor: DMX-pixel light curtains + pixel trees + ad-compatible programmable content, sourced via S4 Lights partnership.

**Commercial structure.** Revenue-share on title-sponsor + CVB co-funding + ad-inventory net revenue. Aquarium contributes site + permits + hosting.

**When it fits.** Aquarium has existing Director of Corporate Partnerships + city / CVB relationship (Charleston / Baltimore / New Orleans / Tampa pattern) + published economic-impact study anchoring the regional-spending argument. **Does not fit when:** those relationships are absent or the aquarium is early-stage on sponsor-pipeline maturity — the 6–9 month sponsor-close cycle is too long for first-install trust-building.

### Offer 3 — Permanent Canvas (Offer B-analog, pending R-19 parallel)

**One-line statement.** Permanent programmable-media architectural installation on the aquarium's signature canvas (lobby architecture, gallery ceilings, facade, mezzanine overlay) that doubles as seasonal programming surface — capital-install, multi-year maintenance contract, amortized into seasonal programs without a second capital cycle.

## Voice rules

Five voice disciplines cumulative from BC-5879 zoos-ledger + BC-5929 research grounding + operator gate-confirmation 2026-04-23. All five apply to outbound email composition, account-research note-taking, and creative-angle generation consuming this playbook.

1. **Don't name-drop enterprise-flagship aquariums as analogs.** Georgia Aquarium, Monterey Bay Aquarium, Shedd Aquarium, New England Aquarium, Seattle Aquarium — these register as wrong-scale and wrong-buyer to a mid-market independent Executive Director; architectural-lighting-design firms own the flagship capital relationship. Stay within mid-market precedent anchors — Newport KY, Aquarium of the Pacific (Long Beach), Adventure Aquarium NJ, Oregon Coast, Texas State, National Aquarium Baltimore, SC Aquarium, Audubon New Orleans, Virginia Aquarium, Maritime Aquarium at Norwalk. (Source: BC-5929 issue body + research grounding.)

2. **Don't use enterprise-VP titles in outbound salutations.** Mid-market / independent aquariums run Executive Director / President & CEO (often dual-hatted as VP & Exec Director — Newport KY + Adventure NJ pattern) + Director of Marketing & Admissions Revenue + Director of Corporate Partnerships + Director of Development + Director of Operations + Director of Guest Experience. Addressing "VP Revenue" at a 200K-visitor independent signals templated enterprise outbound sent to the wrong property and gets instant-deleted. "Director of Adult Programs" does not exist as a standalone role at mid-market; adult programming lives under Marketing / Events. (Source: BC-5929 R-5 research grounding + user pre-compose gate 2026-04-23.)

3. **Lead with revenue-diversification + economic-impact vocabulary, not aesthetic language.** 21+ ticket revenue, F&B attach per-cap, membership conversion, incremental revenue over prior-year baseline, hotel-room-nights attributable, regional economic-impact — these are the operator-native phrasings Executive Directors and Directors of Marketing & Admissions Revenue use in board reports and budget narrative. Leading with "beautiful lighting that will transform your aquarium" signals vendor-speak; leading with "measurable 21+ ticket-revenue lift above Year-1 baseline" signals homework. (Source: BC-5929 research grounding § Voice vocabulary.)

4. **Don't pitch tank-engineering or husbandry-controlled lighting.** Brite's zone is architectural-gallery-only — tank-interior lighting is husbandry-controlled by aquarium staff and veterinary advisors. Any email, concept board, or follow-up that proposes touching tank lighting gets flagged internally as out-of-scope and sent back. The husbandry boundary is not a nice-to-have, it's the compliance line. (Source: BC-5929 issue body Non-Goal #3 + research § Tank-welfare respecting dimension.)

5. **Use aquarium-native content vocabulary, not consumer-marketing copy.** Write "bioluminescence," "UV-reactive overlay," "jelly gallery," "kelp-forest gallery," "reef-gallery facade," "architectural lobby wash," "gallery-ceiling overlay," "mezzanine programmable-media" — the operator-native phrasings Directors of Marketing & Admissions Revenue and Directors of Corporate Partnerships use when describing their own venue to their boards. Avoid consumer-marketing copy vocabulary that aquariums use for their own ticket-buyer audiences (see § Anti-slop rules for the banned list). (Source: BC-5929 research grounding § Content vocabulary.)

## Anti-slop rules

Five rules cumulative from BC-5929 issue body + research grounding § Anti-patterns. This is the **only** section where banned consumer-marketing adjectives may appear — specifically in "don't use" bullets.

1. **Don't use consumer-marketing adjectives that aquariums use in their own ticket-buyer copy.** Banned list: "magical undersea," "immersive wonder," "dive into," "dazzling," "mesmerizing," "enchanting," "shimmering," "breathtaking," "captivating depths," "ocean magic," "sea of wonder," "under the sea." These are words aquariums write in their own consumer-facing program copy — a Brite outbound email using them reads as someone who writes brochures, not someone who moves revenue. Georgia Aquarium's own holiday consumer copy uses "shimmering winter wonderland" and "ocean magic" framings; a vendor lifting that vocabulary signals they can't tell the difference between operator voice and marketing voice. (Source: BC-5929 issue body voice rule + research grounding § Content-vocabulary anti-slop candidates.)

2. **No species name-dropping.** Don't pepper prose with "sharks," "jellies," "octopuses," "seahorses," or specific tank names to signal on-topic engagement. Aquarium directors know what lives in their tanks; vendor name-drops read as trying too hard and erode credibility the rest of the email is building. Refer to "your collection," "your flagship gallery," "your tank galleries," or the specific public-program name if the aquarium has one. (Source: BC-5929 issue body Task 11 + peer BC-5920 anti-slop precedent.)

3. **No tank-engineering or husbandry-controlled pitching.** Don't propose tank-interior lighting, species-specific wavelength tuning, or aquarium-husbandry vocabulary ("chroma-match coral," "circadian-match jelly tanks") — tank lighting is controlled by aquarium staff and veterinary advisors, out of Brite's engineering scope. Brite's zone is architectural-gallery overlay only. This is a hard compliance boundary, mirrored from § Voice rule 4. (Source: BC-5929 issue body Non-Goal #3 + research grounding.)

4. **No "dive-into" / "sea of" / "tides of" / "swimming in" metaphors.** Consumer-marketing vernacular that zeros out credibility with operators who field dozens of vendor pitches weekly. Replace with operator-native verbs: "install," "program," "underwrite," "measure," "amortize." (Source: BC-5929 research grounding § Content-vocabulary anti-slop candidates.)

5. **No generic "ROI uplift" without named aquarium metric.** Aquarium economics are specific — 21+ ticket revenue, F&B / bar attach per-cap, membership conversion, regional economic-impact share, hotel-room-nights attributable, sponsor-inventory sell-through. Generic "ROI" or "returns" phrasing signals the vendor didn't do aquarium-vertical homework and doesn't know the revenue-manager's scorecard vocabulary. Use the specific metric the aquarium's Director of Marketing & Admissions Revenue or Director of Corporate Partnerships tracks. (Source: BC-5929 research grounding § Anti-patterns + peer BC-5920 + BC-5921 precedent.)
