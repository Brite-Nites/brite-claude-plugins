# Hotels & Resorts vertical playbook

Brite-originated reference for all future hotels & resorts marketing work — outbound email copywriting, account research, situation mining, creative-angle generation, TAM mapping. Distills the BC-5921 research grounding into a reusable playbook so future skill authors don't re-run the research. Consumed by `email-copywriting` preset composition (BC-5935), `tam-mapping` when ported (BC-5946 / BC-5950), and `situation-mining` when ported.

Vertical scope is independent and regional resorts, boutique hotels, and destination resort properties (Labs entity per BC-5921 issue decision — mixed Nites/Labs canon resolved to Labs for v1 preset). Enterprise chain properties (Marriott, Hilton, Hyatt, IHG flagships) are out of scope — multi-year RFP procurement, 8–15-stakeholder decision surfaces, and brand-standard-constrained programming authority make them a fundamentally different motion than cold outbound. Enterprise flagships surface in the playbook as ceiling-scale references (e.g., Gaylord Opryland at Marriott scale) but are not ICP.

Hotel lighting is structurally different from zoos (see [`zoos.md`](./zoos.md) for peer shape). Zoos sell ticketed attendance against a sponsor-and-economic-dev stack; hotels sell rate-premium and booking-window extension against a revenue-management scorecard. The offer library, voice rules, and anti-slop list all differ in material ways.

## Vendor landscape (hotels lens)

The experiential-lighting vendor archetypes defined in [`../experiential-lighting-vendor-landscape.md`](../experiential-lighting-vendor-landscape.md) apply to hotels with one addition (AV integrators' experiential practices) and a meaningfully different weighting than zoos. This section filters the shared archetypes through hotel-specific evidence and names the fifth archetype that surfaces in hospitality but not in zoos.

**Large-scale commercial lighting installers — dominant at destination resorts.** Holiday Outdoor Décor (Allentown, PA) installed Hard Rock Hollywood's "Hard Rock the Holidays" 2025 — 2M+ LED lights across a 24-acre Guitar Hotel pool deck and 20+ themed areas, 35-night Nov 29 2025 – Jan 3 2026 run ([Hard Rock press](https://casino.hardrock.com/hollywood/newsroom/2025/11/seminole-hard-rock-hotel-and-casino-hollywood-unveils-a-new-holiday-tradition-hard-rock-the-holidays-a-family-friendly-light-show-spectacular-at-the-guitar-hotel)). MK Illumination (Austrian-founded 1968, global) runs the closest hospitality-positioned peer globally with explicit US and UK hospitality vertical pages and end-to-end concept-design-manufacture-install-storage service; their pitch targets "boutique city centre hotels to country house estates" ([MK US hotels](https://www.mk-illumination.com/true-light/leisure/hotels/), [MK UK hospitality](https://mkillumination.co.uk/hospitality-christmas-lighting-decoration/)). American Christmas is referenced repeatedly in NYC holiday-hotel trade coverage as the decorator behind flagship Manhattan lobbies (inferred from BC-5921 research — specific property attribution not independently confirmed).

**Lantern-festival producers — weaker presence at hotels than zoos.** Tianyu Arts & Culture explicitly frames itself around zoos / botanical gardens / "natural science institutions" ([Tianyu about](https://tianyuculture.us/about-us/)); Zigong Lantern Group and The Lantern Festival Company mention "resort & hotel grounds" in targeting copy but lack name-brand US hotel case studies ([Zigong](https://zglanterngroup.cn/contact-us), [Lantern Festival Co](https://lanternfestivalco.com/)). This is a meaningful competitive opening: Tianyu's zoo-branded credibility doesn't transfer cleanly to hotel revenue-manager buyers.

**Projection / immersive studios — rare but real at hotels.** Moment Factory delivered The Grand Magic Hotel (Paris, 2022 launch with Schroders) — a two-year build covering Lobby + Grand Hall interior design, digital content, lighting, and sound across four day/night-transitioning immersive storyworlds ([Moment Factory](https://momentfactory.com/products/grand-magic-hotel)). This is a capital-install, six-to-seven-figure, multi-year-build tier — uncommon but worth flagging in account research when a resort is in a ground-up or major-renovation window. Limelight Art is event-weighted and does not headline hotel-as-permanent-installation work (inferred from BC-5921 research).

**Holiday specialty installers — fragmented regional tier at boutiques.** Smaller boutique properties typically buy from regional commercial-lighting generalists with a Q4 seasonal pivot — the same regional-fragmentation pattern as zoos. Switching costs compound after a season.

**AV integrators with experiential practices — unique to hospitality, absent in zoos.** AVI-SPL launched Experience Technology Group (XTG) in 2021 as a dedicated experiential-tech unit partnering with architects, experience designers, brand agencies, and fabricators ([sixteen-nine coverage](https://www.sixteen-nine.net/2021/10/26/avi-spl-launches-new-experiential-tech-business-unit/), [AVI-SPL hospitality](https://avispl.com/industries/hospitality/)); confirmed property-wide AV at Hard Rock Hotel & Casino. Diversified is the "arch rival" per trade coverage; Encore/PSAV runs event-staging rather than property integration. This archetype surfaces at hotels because hotels buy capital AV (ballroom, lobby, meeting-space) anyway and get upsold into experiential — a pattern that doesn't exist at zoos. Worth flagging in account research: if a hotel has an AV integrator already embedded, the experiential pitch may already be in motion and Brite's positioning needs to be "programmable-media partner" rather than "new vendor."

**LED-retrofit vendors — same adjacent-not-competitive status as at zoos.** Goodlight / Orphek / EcoMedia serve Facilities/engineering buyers on energy-reduction cycles. Wrong buyer (engineering, not revenue), wrong P&L (capex energy reduction, not revenue programming), wrong motion. They surface in "lighting for hotels" searches and must be set aside.

### Explicitly adjacent, not competitive (hotels-specific)

Two vendor names require a hotels-specific restatement of the vendor-landscape file's top-level guard.

**MK Illumination.** Unlike the zoos-context framing (retail / urban-streetscape — wrong motion for zoos), MK **is** directly hospitality-positioned and competes with Holiday Outdoor Décor-class installers at the destination-resort tier. In hotel outbound, MK surfaces as a credible peer in the commercial-lighting-installer archetype, not as an adjacent-not-competitive vendor. Any hotel-vertical composition that lifts the zoos-context "MK is retail / wrong-motion" framing without re-grounding it for hospitality should be sent back.

**Ambius.** Plantscaping parent with holiday-decor capability — confirmed project at The Phoenician Resort, Phoenix (Duomo-lantern entry, lobby lanterns, Santa's Workshop themed install — [Ambius Phoenician](https://www.ambius.com/projects/holiday-decor-projects/phoenician-resort)). Hotels sometimes buy holiday experiential from non-pure-play lighting vendors when there's an existing plant-and-amenity contract, which makes Ambius an incumbent-lock pattern to watch for (not a direct Brite competitor, but a vendor whose adjacency creates switching friction).

## Buyer personas

Four personas cover the decision surface at boutique, regional, and independent hotels & resorts. The persona set departs from the BC-5921 issue body directive ("VP Revenue Management / VP Experience / GM / Director of Revenue") — R-7 research grounding confirmed VP-titled Revenue Management and Experience roles are enterprise-only and do not exist at the 100–400-key independent target. Independent and boutique ICP titles run GM / Director of Revenue / DOSM / Owner-Operator. This file uses the research-grounded set.

**General Manager (GM) — primary decision-maker.** Owns P&L at the property level, signs vendor contracts, and at boutique and smaller regional properties routinely wears the revenue-management and commercial-sales hats simultaneously. Cares about ADR, RevPAR, occupancy, and peak-weekend compression. Signs **Offer E equivalent (Branded Seasonal Program)** and authorizes Offer A (Rate-Premium Pilot) directly. Fears signing: unrecoverable capex without a defensible rate-premium story; ownership pushback on vendor spend that doesn't move the revenue-manager scorecard.

**Director of Revenue / Revenue Manager — co-primary where the role exists.** Present at properties 100+ keys; below that, folded into GM or DOSM ([Lighthouse independent RM guide](https://www.mylighthouse.com/resources/insights/guide-to-revenue-management-for-independent-hoteliers), [Duetto small-hotel RM](https://www.duettocloud.com/en-us/library/revenue-management-small-hotels)). Owns booking pace, compression pricing, direct-channel mix, comp-set index. The ADR-uplift pitch lands here first — they are the numerate buyer who can quantify what a 20%+ peak-weekend ADR lift is worth against lighting program cost ([Hospitality Net Lighthouse case](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/news/4130330.html)). GM will route the vendor deck to Director of Revenue for evaluation before signing. Pitch the Director of Revenue as co-primary where the property has the role; let them become the internal champion.

**Director of Sales & Marketing (DOSM) — influencer, coalition-builder.** Owns group sales, direct-channel marketing, brand differentiation in the local comp set. Cares about F&B and spa attach rate, direct-booking conversion, package mix, and whether the property has a named, press-worthy seasonal program to lead marketing with. DOSM is rarely the decision-maker but is a critical coalition partner — they can surface the program framing internally and get the deck routed to GM + Director of Revenue with momentum. Avoid pitching DOSM as decision-maker; that's the enterprise-outbound mistake.

**Owner-Operator — decision-maker at family-owned and private-ownership boutiques.** At independent and family-owned properties below the 200-key line, the owner-operator signs vendor contracts personally. Cares about the 3-year P&L of the program, the comp-set differentiation story, and reputational upside from press-worthy programming. The research-grounded voice rule: when account research flags private-ownership or family-operated structure, pitch owner-operator as primary with GM as implementation partner. Public-company or REIT-owned boutiques run the GM-primary pattern instead.

## Recency signals hotels leak publicly

Hotels leak signals through STR reports, trade press (Skift, Hotel News Now, Hospitality Net, Asian Hospitality), REIT filings, local CVB releases, and hotel PR announcements. Six recency signals matter. Decay windows cross-reference [`../shelf-life-patterns.md`](../shelf-life-patterns.md).

**Package-marketing launches** (decay: 3–6 months). Press releases announcing "Christmas at [Property]" / "Holiday in [Theme]" programs, bundled stay-and-admission packaging like Callaway's Fantasy In Lights Lodge package (admission + overnight + 15% off + daily breakfast — [Callaway package](https://www.callawaygardens.com/packages/fantasy-in-lights-package/)). Shelf life: a program announced 6+ months ago is live and qualifying; a program announced 2 weeks ago is too recent (decision already made for the upcoming season — Offer E outreach lands wrong).

**Destination-weekend announcements** (decay: 2–4 months). Fri/Sat-only reservation windows, peak-date pricing structures like Gaylord Opryland's compression-date calendar ([adept.travel coverage](https://adept.travel/news/2025-07-24-gaylord-ice-returns)). These tell you the property has built a compression-event calendar around programming — Offer E's booking-window-extension math fits properties with existing compression muscle.

**New GM appointments** (decay: 6–12 months). Hotel GMs run audit cycles in months 3–9 of tenure; outbound landing in that window fits the audit cadence. LinkedIn role-change alerts and hotel PR announcements are the primary surfaces. Particularly valuable when paired with a known ownership group that moves GMs across properties.

**Renovation / F&B concept launches** (decay: 6–18 months). Capital-renovation press, new restaurant openings, pool-deck or lobby-refresh announcements. Signals the property is in a capital-spending window with programming-activation budget available.

**STR / RevPAR / comp-set performance reports** (decay: quarterly). Public hotel performance data (STR trend releases, REIT earnings calls, Asian Hospitality quarterly summaries — [Asian Hospitality boutique report](https://www.asianhospitality.com/boutique-hotel-report-2025-us-performance-2024/)). A property under-indexing the comp set on ADR is a stronger prospect for rate-premium programming than one already leading — the upside case is larger and the pain is named.

**OTA-commission / direct-channel commentary** (decay: evergreen within 12 months). Property operators who publicly discuss OTA commission rates (Cloudbeds cites 15–25% typical, sometimes 30% — [Cloudbeds OTA](https://www.cloudbeds.com/online-travel-agencies/commissions/); [StayFi 2026 OTA fees](https://stayfi.com/vrm-insider/2025/11/04/ota-fees/)) or direct-channel initiatives are primed for programming pitches — a branded seasonal experience is the strongest direct-channel hook independent hotels can build.

## Program economics

Anchor numbers for hotels & resorts outbound work. Every number below is either public-source (cited) or inferred from BC-5921 research grounding (marked).

**ADR ranges by ICP tier.**
- Independent luxury boutiques, 2024: ~$440 ADR, ~$295 RevPAR ([Asian Hospitality boutique report 2024](https://www.asianhospitality.com/boutique-hotel-report-2025-us-performance-2024/)).
- Independent / regional boutiques ICP range: $250–$600 ADR (inferred from BC-5921 research grounding).
- Enterprise-brand average: $180–$350 ADR; flagships $500+ (inferred from BC-5921 research grounding; noted as out-of-scope for v1 ICP).

**Rate-premium anchor.** A Lighthouse-documented boutique case study reported 20%+ ADR lift on peak weekends and €250 room-type premiums during programming-driven compression ([Hospitality Net case](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/news/4130330.html)). This is the ceiling anchor for the rate-premium framing — when pitching Director of Revenue, the Lighthouse precedent is the "what's possible" number.

**Booking-window extension anchor.** European shoulder-season bookings surged 60%+, with Oct/Nov bookings up 20% YoY ([Cvent shoulder season](https://www.cvent.com/en/blog/hospitality/what-is-shoulder-season)). Named-and-dated seasonal programs are the mechanism revenue managers cite for pulling Q4 demand into Oct/early-Nov at shoulder rates.

**OTA-commission pressure.** 15–25% typical, up to 30% per booking ([Cloudbeds](https://www.cloudbeds.com/online-travel-agencies/commissions/), [StayFi](https://stayfi.com/vrm-insider/2025/11/04/ota-fees/)). A direct-channel booking hook built around package-only access to a named program converts transactions to .com and recovers the full commission differential.

**Program scale benchmarks.**
- Hard Rock Hollywood "Hard Rock the Holidays" 2025: 2M+ LED lights, 24 acres, 20+ themed areas, 35-night run, tickets starting $35 ([Hard Rock press](https://casino.hardrock.com/hollywood/newsroom/2025/11/seminole-hard-rock-hotel-and-casino-hollywood-unveils-a-new-holiday-tradition-hard-rock-the-holidays-a-family-friendly-light-show-spectacular-at-the-guitar-hotel)).
- Callaway Resort & Gardens Fantasy In Lights: 10M+ lights across 2,500 acres, multi-decade run, Nov 14 – Jan 5 ([Callaway package](https://www.callawaygardens.com/packages/fantasy-in-lights-package/)).
- Fairmont Scottsdale Princess Christmas at the Princess: 10M+ LED lights, **15-year compounding run — strongest YoY continuity anchor in hospitality** ([Christmas at the Princess](https://www.christmasattheprincess.com/)).
- Hotel del Coronado "A Holiday in Oz" 2025: 100K+ white lights on the Victorian main structure, 12-foot turret replica, themes refresh annually (prior: "Frosted by the Sea" 2022); complimentary public access driving F&B and retail attach ([Hotel del](https://www.hoteldel.com/events/lightshow-spectacular/), [San Diego press](https://www.sandiego.org/press-release/coronado-for-the-holidays-an-enchanting-winter-getaway)).
- Gaylord Opryland Christmas 2025 (enterprise scale ceiling, out-of-ICP reference): 5M+ Christmas lights + ICE! at 2M pounds of sculpted ice across 20,000 sq ft, reservation-only resort access on compression dates ([Christmas at Gaylord Opryland](https://www.christmasatgaylordopryland.com/)).

## Good-vs-bad program taxonomy

Five dimensions distinguish programs that compound (YoY refresh, direct-booking compression, rate premium) from programs that burn out (guest-complaint tickets, attendance decay, ownership cancellation). All five derived from BC-5921 research grounding (trade press + property case studies).

**YoY narrative continuity vs one-and-done.** Programs with persistent brand and refreshed content compound — Hotel del Coronado rotates themes while the program brand persists (2022 "Frosted by the Sea," 2025 "A Holiday in Oz" — both tied to "Holiday Light Show" continuity); Fairmont Scottsdale Princess at 15 seasons and Callaway's multi-decade run are the North Stars (inferred from BC-5921 research grounding, anchored to cited URLs above). Programs debuting a "grand one-season launch" without a refresh plan are bad — ownership cuts them after year 2 when the "new" framing collapses.

**Direct-booking hook vs OTA spillover.** Good programs attach direct-channel mechanics — package-only access, past-guest exclusive viewing hours, early-booking windows on .com. Callaway's Fantasy In Lights Lodge package is the named example: admission + overnight + 15% off daily breakfast + spa/breakfast enhancements ([Callaway package](https://www.callawaygardens.com/packages/fantasy-in-lights-package/)). Bad programs drive public open-access traffic that spills into nearby properties without paying for the install. The program has to convert transactions to .com or the P&L math doesn't close.

**Brand-coherent vs generic holiday.** Good programs lean into the property's architectural or narrative identity — Biltmore Candlelight leans Gilded Age estate; Hard Rock leans guitar-tower-as-canvas; Hotel del leans Victorian-seaside heritage (the property installed the world's first electrically-lit outdoor Christmas tree in 1904 — [San Diego press](https://www.sandiego.org/press-release/coronado-for-the-holidays-an-enchanting-winter-getaway)). Bad programs run generic inflatable-Santa / rope-light installs that could be at a strip-mall; they fail the brand-coherent test and don't give the DOSM press-angle material.

**Operations-survivable vs guest-experience-degrading.** Good programs install in cleanly zoned areas that don't block guest circulation, don't trigger noise or parking or ambient-light-into-rooms complaints, and don't force overnight-guest access restrictions. Gaylord Opryland navigates overnight access with a paid reservation system at enterprise scale ([Gaylord Opryland Christmas](https://www.christmasatgaylordopryland.com/)) — most boutiques cannot afford that operational overhead. Bad programs surface in post-season TripAdvisor reviews as parking disasters, noise complaints, or guest-to-public friction (inferred from BC-5921 research grounding).

**Rate-premium-defensible vs generic seasonal discount.** Good programs justify a rate premium — the lighting program IS the compression event, and package-rate premiums (20%+ peak-weekend ADR documented — [Hospitality Net case](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/news/4130330.html)) land in the Director of Revenue's forecast. Bad programs become cost centers with no rate movement; ownership cancels after two seasons because the program doesn't pay for itself on the P&L line items the revenue manager tracks.

## Offer candidates

Three offer structures surface from BC-5921 research grounding + zoos-file structural pattern. Each evaluated through the Hormozi frontend/backend lens per [`../offer-design-frameworks.md`](../offer-design-frameworks.md). Offer statuses in § V1 offer picks below. **Do not lift zoo offers verbatim** (per BC-5921 issue non-goal) — hotel economics are rate-premium + booking-window, not ticketed-attendance + sponsor-pipeline.

### Offer A — Rate-Premium Pilot (tactical complement)

**One-line statement.** Brite installs a single-zone programmable-media demonstration at a boutique property that already has an incumbent holiday-lighting vendor, underwriting the install cost in exchange for measured peak-weekend ADR lift, compression-rate data, and direct-channel attribution — plus case-study usage rights.

**Hormozi mapping.**
- Dream Outcome: measurable, attributable peak-weekend ADR lift the Director of Revenue can defend to ownership at year-end review.
- Perceived Likelihood: high on narrow scope — single-zone pilot, one season, bounded failure mode.
- Time Delay: low (install in 4–8 weeks; results measurable within first programming weekend).
- Effort + Sacrifice: low (property contributes site access and a measurement partnership; Brite handles the rest).

**Frontend deliverable.** A rendered concept board for a named pilot zone (property-specific, e.g., "Main Lawn / Founders Lawn / Pool Deck / Porte-Cochère at [Property]") with layout, run-time schedule, and a measurement plan naming the ADR-lift benchmark and the direct-channel-mix reporting cadence.

**Backend commitment.** Pilot-zone install with a **revenue-native guarantee metric** — peak-weekend ADR lift on programming dates vs comp-set baseline, direct-channel booking mix in the Q4 programming window, or F&B / spa attach rate on package guests. Not "guest photo-shares" or vanity metrics — those miss the revenue-manager scorecard and signal vendor didn't do the homework.

**Commercial structure.** Brite underwrites install cost in exchange for case-study usage rights — property name, ADR-lift number, compression-window screenshots. Phrased confidently: "We underwrite the install. In exchange, we use the results — ADR data, booking-window math, property name — in our sales material."

**When it fits.** Property has a long-tenured incumbent holiday-lighting vendor AND a Director of Revenue (or GM doubling as RM at smaller independents) open to a low-commitment pilot that produces defensible peak-weekend data. Also fits properties in a new-GM-90-day-audit window.

### Offer B — Capital Experience Anchor (pending R-19 parallel)

**One-line statement.** Permanent programmable-media architectural installation on the property's signature canvas (lobby, porte-cochère, pool deck, grand-entry Victorian tower) that doubles as seasonal programming overlay — capital-install, multi-year maintenance contract, amortized into seasonal programs without a second capital cycle.

**Hormozi mapping.**
- Dream Outcome: year-round architectural upgrade + amortized seasonal programming capacity that compounds YoY without new capital asks.
- Perceived Likelihood: moderate — the dual-use framing is novel and requires Revenue + Ops + ownership to coordinate on a shared asset.
- Time Delay: high (capital approval 6–12 months; install 8–12 weeks; first programming season 12+ months out).
- Effort + Sacrifice: moderate-to-high (capital commitment, multi-stakeholder coordination, ownership-level approval).

**Frontend deliverable.** Architectural site survey + programmable-canvas design document with seasonal overlay already mapped against the property's marketing calendar.

**Backend commitment.** Subscription-model install with multi-year maintenance + scheduled seasonal program deployments included. Brite retains infrastructure ownership; property licenses year-round use.

**When it fits.** Property in a capital-renovation window, ownership that buys on 3-year P&L, and an architectural canvas already built for flagship-marketing-asset positioning (grand-entry, historic tower, flagship pool). **Pending status mirrors zoos Offer B**: blocked on BC-5941 confirmation of Brite's capital-install sales-motion readiness for hospitality. Do not compose Offer B presets until that confirmation lands.

### Offer E — Branded Seasonal Program (V1 primary)

**One-line statement.** Brite designs, installs, and operates a multi-season branded programmable-media experience — named after the property, YoY content refresh planned from day one, direct-channel-exclusive package structure, revenue-share on incremental ADR lift + F&B/spa attach above comp-set baseline. Property contributes site access, permits, operations hosting. Near-zero upfront capital.

**Hormozi mapping.**
- Dream Outcome: named, press-worthy seasonal program that pays for itself via peak-weekend ADR lift + booking-window pull-forward + direct-channel mix shift; YoY compounding across 3+ seasons; defensible comp-set differentiation.
- Perceived Likelihood: proven by revenue-model pro-forma (ADR-lift projection + booking-pace forecast + direct-channel capture math with citation anchors — Lighthouse 20%+ peak-weekend lift, Cvent 20% Oct/Nov YoY shoulder-bookings, Cloudbeds 15–25% OTA recovery).
- Time Delay: 6–9 months property-commit to program-live; year-2 compounding default.
- Effort + Sacrifice: near-zero capital, near-zero execution risk, operations limited to hosting.

**Frontend deliverable.** A program design deck with: named program brand (proposed), rendered canvas treatments for 2–3 property zones, YoY refresh roadmap (year 1 / 2 / 3 content themes), direct-channel package structure, and a revenue pro-forma citing the three named economic anchors (peak-weekend ADR lift, shoulder-booking extension, direct-channel mix). The deck itself is proof Brite has done the revenue-management homework, not just the design work.

**Backend commitment.** Full program install + operation + YoY refresh cadence. Brite finances against revenue-share on incremental ADR lift + attach. Property contributes site, permits, hosting. 3-year rolling contract with year-1 rate-premium guarantee structure.

**Tech stack anchor.** DMX-pixel programmable-media infrastructure — the programmability is the differentiator. Static lantern installs cannot carry YoY content refresh without full reinstall; projection installs aren't repurposable across architectural contexts; holiday-only installers can't carry year-round brand storytelling. Brite's programmable-media stack supports all three YoY requirements (narrative refresh, direct-channel hook, brand-coherent content).

**Why this beats A / B.** Capital from Brite near-zero (removes the ownership-capex question). Revenue-share aligns Brite's incentive with the Director of Revenue's scorecard. YoY compounding built in from year 1 (addresses the one-and-done taxonomy dimension directly). MK Illumination and Holiday Outdoor Décor can match the install scale but not the revenue-share financing model; their commercial model is fee-for-service capex, which leaves the property carrying the P&L risk.

**Credibility anchors (strongest).** Fairmont Scottsdale Princess Christmas at the Princess (15-year compounding run — strongest hospitality YoY anchor — [Christmas at the Princess](https://www.christmasattheprincess.com/)); Hotel del Coronado annual theme rotation + 1904 heritage ("world's first electrically-lit outdoor Christmas tree" — [San Diego press](https://www.sandiego.org/press-release/coronado-for-the-holidays-an-enchanting-winter-getaway)); Callaway Fantasy In Lights as multi-decade package-structure reference ([Callaway package](https://www.callawaygardens.com/packages/fantasy-in-lights-package/)).

**Open risks flagged (not dealbreakers, but real).**
- Revenue-share accounting requires ticketing / PMS / package-mix integration on property side — operationally non-trivial.
- First property is hardest; trust earned through rendered-concept-board precision and revenue-model defensibility, not cold outbound alone.
- Brand-coherent content design requires property-specific research, not template-lifted.
- YoY refresh commitment is a three-year vendor relationship contract from day 1 — ownership may balk at the term length before the pilot-season proof.

## Voice rules

Five voice disciplines cumulative from BC-5921 research grounding + issue directives. All five apply to outbound email composition, account-research note-taking, and creative-angle generation consuming this playbook.

1. **Don't name-drop enterprise-flagship brands as analogs.** Marriott Marquis, Waldorf Astoria, Four Seasons flagships, Ritz-Carlton — these register as wrong-category and wrong-scale to a boutique / regional / independent buyer. Stay within boutique-and-regional precedent anchors — Hotel del Coronado, Fairmont Scottsdale Princess (brand-adjacent boutique), Callaway Resort & Gardens, Biltmore Inn. (Source: BC-5921 issue body + research grounding.)

2. **Don't use enterprise-VP titles in outbound salutations.** The boutique / regional / independent decision surface runs GM + Director of Revenue + DOSM + Owner-Operator. Addressing "VP of Revenue Management" at a 180-key independent signals the vendor sent a templated enterprise outbound to the wrong property and gets instant-deleted. Pitch GM as primary, Director of Revenue or Revenue Manager as co-primary where the role exists, DOSM as influencer, Owner-Operator as primary at family-owned / private-ownership properties. (Source: BC-5921 research grounding, operator confirmation 2026-04-22.)

3. **Lead with revenue-manager vocabulary, not aesthetic language.** ADR, RevPAR, compression events, booking pace, on-the-books, attach rate, length-of-stay minimums, direct channel, comp-set index — these are the industry-insider phrasings hotel revenue and operations leaders use natively. Leading with "beautiful lighting that will transform your property" signals vendor-speak; leading with "programming-driven peak-weekend ADR lift against your comp-set baseline" signals homework. (Source: BC-5921 research grounding § Voice vocabulary.)

4. **Don't lead with wedding-market framing.** Wedding-venue positioning is a separate cost center with its own sales manager at most hotels; leading with wedding photography, wedding-venue positioning, or wedding-revenue-as-proof-point routes the email to Events Sales and dies there. The revenue-management buyer doesn't buy on wedding economics. If wedding-revenue surfaces at all, it surfaces in paragraph 2+ as an attach layer, not as the opening hook. (Source: BC-5921 issue body anti-slop rule + research grounding § Anti-patterns.)

5. **Don't use "romantic" / "ambiance" buzzwords in framing or voice.** These are guest-facing marketing-copy words hotels use in *their own* customer materials — a vendor using them in a pitch reads as someone who writes brochures, not someone who moves revenue. (These words may appear only inside explicit § Anti-slop "don't" bullets; never in the voice-rule body or framing copy of outbound email.) (Source: BC-5921 issue body voice rule.)

## Anti-slop rules

Five rules cumulative from BC-5921 issue body + research grounding § Anti-patterns.

1. **Don't use commodity "lighting upgrade" framing.** Routes the email to engineering / facilities buyers and dies there — wrong P&L (capex energy-reduction, not revenue programming), wrong buyer. If the word "upgrade" appears in a cold outbound subject line or opening line, the vendor is pitching the facilities cost-center not the revenue-management scorecard. (Source: BC-5921 issue body.)

2. **Don't use wedding-revenue as the default proof point.** Wedding photos, wedding-venue case studies, "weddings drove $X in F&B revenue" — these route to Events Sales and signal the vendor hasn't read the property as a revenue-management ICP. (Source: BC-5921 issue body + research grounding § Anti-patterns.)

3. **Don't use urgency language.** "Limited availability," "last chance," "book now before the season ends," "only N slots left" — urgency tactics are consumer-conversion-funnel vocabulary and read as low-trust vendor-sales-training-manual tactics to hotel GMs and Directors of Revenue who field dozens of vendor pitches. Urgency is earned by genuine compression-window timing, not manufactured in copy. (Source: BC-5921 issue body.)

4. **Don't use "magical" / "enchanting" / "dazzling" / "mesmerizing" adjectives.** Consultant-speak that zeros out credibility on a revenue-management buyer. If the program matters to the P&L, the numbers describe it (peak-weekend ADR lift, direct-channel mix shift, booking-window extension, attach rate). If the numbers aren't there, adjectives don't substitute. (Source: BC-5921 research grounding § Anti-patterns.)

5. **Don't use generic "ROI uplift" without named revenue-management metric.** Hotel economics are specific — peak-weekend ADR, RevPAR trajectory on compression dates, direct-channel mix shift, length-of-stay minimums on program weekends, F&B and spa attach rate on package guests. Generic "ROI" or "returns" phrasing signals the vendor didn't do hotel-vertical homework and doesn't know the revenue-manager's scorecard vocabulary. Use the specific metric the property's Director of Revenue or GM tracks. (Source: BC-5921 research grounding § Voice vocabulary + § Anti-patterns.)

## How to use this reference

Three categories of downstream consumer read this playbook. Each reads a different subset of sections.

**email-copywriting preset composition (BC-5935 and future hotels-vertical presets).** Reads § Offer candidates + § V1 offer picks + § Voice rules + § Anti-slop rules to compose Offer E-primary and Offer A-tactical preset files. Does not read § Program economics or § Recency signals in full (those inform account research, not body copy — a preset citing Fairmont Scottsdale's 15-year run in every email is over-specified; the anchor surfaces contextually only when the prospect's research note has already flagged a YoY-compounding peer).

**tam-mapping (BC-5946 / BC-5950 when ported).** Reads § Recency signals + § Program economics + § Buyer personas to build a hotels-vertical TAM database with scoring signals. Recency-signal decay windows feed the scoring logic; program-economics anchors feed the ADR-and-size qualification bar; persona titles feed the decision-maker targeting.

**situation-mining (when ported).** Reads § Recency signals + § Good-vs-bad taxonomy to distinguish hotels with compounding programs (refresh candidates for Offer A pilot-zone upsell) from hotels with decaying programs or no program at all (Offer E full-branded-program replacement or green-field targets).

**Anti-pattern flag.** If a future hotels-vertical skill, preset, or playbook names MK Illumination as an adjacent-not-competitive vendor (the zoos-context framing) — send it back. MK IS competitive in hospitality; the zoos-context framing does not transfer. Similarly, if Ambius surfaces as "primary holiday competitor," flag as mis-read — Ambius is an incumbent-lock pattern tied to plant-and-amenity contracts, not a direct Brite competitor on the programmable-media canvas.
