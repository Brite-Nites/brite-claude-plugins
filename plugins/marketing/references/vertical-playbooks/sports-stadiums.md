# Sports stadiums vertical playbook

Brite-originated reference for all future sports-stadiums marketing work — outbound email copywriting, account research, situation mining, creative-angle generation, TAM mapping. Distills the BC-5923 research grounding into a reusable playbook so future skill authors don't re-run the research. Consumed by `email-copywriting` preset composition (BC-5937), `tam-mapping` when ported (BC-5946 / BC-5950), and `situation-mining` when ported.

Vertical scope is **US regional mid-market independent stadiums and venues** with meaningful off-season event programming — Minor League Baseball (MiLB AAA/AA; Diamond Baseball Holdings portfolio, independent-league clubs), Group of 5 FBS college football, Major League Soccer, USL Championship, and regional multipurpose / amphitheater venues with 150–500K annual event-day attendance and 200–400 annual non-sport events. Named anchors in the band: Fort Wayne TinCaps, Fluor Field (Colorado Springs), PayPal Park (San Jose Earthquakes MLS), Skylands Stadium (NJ), regional FBS Group of 5 properties (Marshall, Ball State, USF pre-2027). Enterprise flagship tier (NFL / MLB / NBA / NHL; Power 4 FBS — Alabama, Ohio State, Texas, Michigan, Penn State) is out of scope per BC-5923 issue directive and R-9 capital-cycle research: Auburn's $323M Jordan-Hare renovation ([ESPN](https://www.espn.com/college-football/story/_/id/48515876/auburn-moving-forward-323m-renovation-jordan-hare-stadium)) and Penn State's $700M Beaver Stadium 3-phase rebuild (approved May 2024, completion 2027) are 12–24 month committee-driven procurement windows — fundamentally different motion than mid-market GM-authority outbound (6–9 month sales cycles at ICP tier). Enterprise flagships surface in this playbook as ceiling-scale references (Musco Show-Light® at Michigan Stadium, Real Madrid Bernabéu) but are not ICP.

Stadium lighting is structurally different from zoos, hotels, and ski resorts (see [`zoos.md`](./zoos.md), [`hotels-resorts.md`](./hotels-resorts.md), [`ski-resorts.md`](./ski-resorts.md) for peer playbook shapes). Zoos sell ticketed attendance against a sponsor-and-economic-dev stack; hotels sell rate-premium and booking-window extension against a revenue-management scorecard; ski resorts sell village-dwell and F&B per-cap lift against the ancillary-revenue scorecard. **Stadiums sell off-season calendar fill and non-sport event revenue against an operations / bookings scorecard where the core on-field sport product generates regular-season revenue and everything else (concerts, holiday activations, corporate events, graduations, community programming) is the revenue-diversification surface.** Fort Wayne TinCaps host **388 annual non-baseball events** ([The Sport Journal](https://thesportjournal.org/article/ensuring-the-business-sustainability-of-minor-league-baseball-after-the-covid-global-pandemic/)); Fluor Field plans **300+ community events for 2026** ([MiLB](https://www.milb.com/news/baseball-is-the-best-job-in-the-world)). **Brite's zone is the off-season programmable-media layer — plaza, concourse, club-level, exterior gateway — not the playing surface, not sport-critical field lighting, not the on-field event production stack.** The offer library, voice rules, and anti-slop list all reflect that boundary.

One structural departure from BC-5921 / BC-5922 pattern to flag upfront: **there is no cross-vertical vendor inversion at stadiums.** Hotels inverted MK Illumination from zoos-adjacent to primary; ski inverted Moment Factory from zoos-adjacent to primary. At US mid-market stadiums neither inversion holds — MK has no US stadium footprint, Moment Factory has no US minor-league / Group of 5 / MLS deployment (only the 2025 Real Madrid Bernabéu corridor install — [Moment Factory](https://momentfactory.com/products/realmadrid-games) — which is European pro-flagship tier). The stadium primary competitor is instead **stadium-native**: Musco Sports Lighting's Show-Light® entertainment stack. That's the inversion finding, and it sets up a different competitive frame than the hospitality / resort playbooks.

## Vendor landscape (stadium lens)

The experiential-lighting vendor archetypes defined in [`../experiential-lighting-vendor-landscape.md`](../experiential-lighting-vendor-landscape.md) apply to stadiums with three meaningfully different weightings than zoos, hotels, or ski: (a) the lantern-festival archetype is structurally absent at US stadiums (Tianyu and Lantern Festival Company run zoo / fairground / botanical routes, not stadium footprints); (b) the projection / immersive archetype is out-of-competitive-zone at US mid-market stadiums (Moment Factory's stadium footprint is European flagship only — see ICP note below); (c) a **stadium-native primary competitor emerges that has no analog in zoos / hotels / ski**: Musco Sports Lighting's Show-Light® entertainment layer. The primary-competitor framing below names Musco explicitly and the Brite differentiator against Musco's commercial model.

**Musco Sports Lighting — primary competitor at US stadiums (stadium-native, not cross-vertical).** Musco (Cedar Rapids, IA) is the dominant US stadium lighting vendor. Two product tiers matter for Brite's positioning: (1) **sport-critical field-of-play lighting** (Class I / Class II telecast-spec, FCC-regulated, capex-heavy) is **out of Brite's zone and out of Brite's competitive overlap** — Brite does not compete for on-field sport lighting and should never pitch against it; (2) **Show-Light® entertainment** is Musco's color-changing, programmable, music-synchronized layer for team-entry routines, intermission programming, celebrations, and non-sport events ([Musco Entertainment](https://www.musco.com/entertainment/)). Show-Light® is **directly in Brite's zone** — it's the color-programmable audience-experience layer that non-sport events depend on. Confirmed deployments: Michigan Stadium (field + entertainment integration), Auburn Jordan-Hare Stadium (2025 announced entertainment upgrade — [Auburn Tigers](https://auburntigers.com/news/2025/08/22/jordan-hare-stadium-neville-arena-to-receive-cutting-edge-musco-entertainment-upgrades)), Phoenix Rising USL. **Brite's differentiator from Musco at stadium is commercial model, not capability:** Musco sells capital equipment + integration + event-programming service contracts (capex upfront, service fees ongoing); Brite finances the install against pipeline revenue (multi-stream sponsor + tourism + F&B co-marketing + ad inventory), shares ancillary revenue with the property, and repurposes the same infrastructure across off-season events + holiday programming + shoulder-season activations. **Resort capital commitment near-zero; Musco requires upfront capex.** The outbound frame must name the distinction explicitly or the prospect will default to "we already talked to Musco / we have a Musco contract." No cross-vertical inversion flag — Musco is stadium-native, not a repurposed zoos / hotels / ski vendor.

**Holiday specialty installers — regional fragmented vendor pool, single-season vendor-IP-locked framing.** Enchant Christmas runs the world's-largest-Christmas-light-maze format at PayPal Park (San Jose Earthquakes MLS — [San Jose Earthquakes](https://www.sjearthquakes.com/news/news-enchant-christmas-returns-to-san-jose%27s-paypal-park)) and Las Vegas Ballpark; Magic of Lights runs the drive-through / walk-through model at Gillette Stadium (Nov–Dec — [Gillette Stadium](https://www.gillettestadium.com/magic-of-lights-2024/)); Skylands Stadium (NJ) runs a Holiday Light Show + Christmas Village with 2M+ lights ([Skylands Stadium](https://skylandsstadium.com/your-holiday-tradition-at-skylands-stadiums-light-show-christmas-village)); Colorado Christmas Lights and Above All Holiday Lighting serve mountain-resort and regional commercial properties (Colorado-concentrated, named earlier in the ski-resorts playbook). **Archetype signature:** turnkey installation, vendor owns the IP, static or animated but non-programmable, 6–10 week seasonal footprint (Nov–Jan), high switching costs after one season (learned site access). **Brite's Offer A tactical wedge targets this archetype** — the incumbent holiday installer is the most-likely displacement path for an off-season programmable-media pilot at a property with existing holiday programming.

**Venue AV integrators — adjacent-not-competitive, suite-and-booth focused.** Diversified ([Diversified Stadiums & Arenas](https://onediversified.com/spaces/stadiums-arenas)) and AVI-SPL integrate in-stadium video walls, luxury-suite AV, telecast booth infrastructure, ballroom / premium-club AV. **Focus is on-field event delivery and in-venue TV-spec infrastructure — not off-season plaza / concourse / exterior programmable-media.** Wrong buyer (VP Facilities or VP IT), wrong P&L (capex infrastructure, not revenue programming). Adjacent in the building, not competitive for Brite's off-season wedge.

**Projection / immersive studios — adjacent-not-competitive at US mid-market stadiums (out-of-ICP at the flagship tier where they operate).** Moment Factory surfaced one stadium-scale installation: **Real Madrid Bernabéu (2025, corridor-level interactive games + projection — [Moment Factory](https://momentfactory.com/products/realmadrid-games))** — European pro-flagship tier, not Brite ICP. Moment Factory's stated sports track record ([Moment Factory sports](https://momentfactory.com/blogs/news/sports-experiences)) is projection-heavy installation at pro-flagship or ticketed-destination scale, not US mid-market plaza or concourse programming. Obscura, ESI Design, BRC Imagination Arts similarly surface at flagship / theme-park / museum scale, not regional stadium. **No cross-vertical inversion to Brite ICP tier.** Any stadium preset that frames Moment Factory as a direct US mid-market competitor should be sent back — the evidence isn't there.

**Lantern-festival producers — structurally absent at US stadiums.** Tianyu Arts & Culture's venue list ([Tianyu](https://tianyuculture.us/about-us/)) names zoos, fairgrounds, botanical gardens, urban greenfield lots — no US stadium. Illuminight / Illuminights / Lightscape run zoo and botanical-garden routes only. Zigong Lantern Festival and Lantern Festival Company have no US stadium deployment. **Structural reason:** lantern-pathway format requires flat parkable 6–8-week footprints with linear crowd-flow routing; stadium bowls, concourses, plazas are irregular multi-level geometries unsuited to the archetype. More decisively absent than at ski; treat as off-map for stadium composition.

**LED-retrofit vendors — adjacent, same framing as zoos / hotels / ski.** Goodlight, Orphek, EcoMedia serve Facilities buyers on energy-reduction cycles (concourse fluorescent-to-LED swaps, parking-lot pole replacements, back-of-house fixture upgrades). Wrong buyer, wrong P&L, wrong motion. Surfaces in "stadium lighting" searches and must be set aside.

**Moment Factory — adjacent at US mid-market stadiums; active-watch on any US regional expansion.** Unlike the ski-resorts playbook (where Moment Factory IS primary — Vallea Lumina / Alta Lumina / Tonga Lumina), at US mid-market stadiums Moment Factory is adjacent-not-competitive. **S4 Lights — supply-chain partner, not competitor.** Same framing as at zoos, hotels, and ski. ## Buyer personas

Four primary personas plus an optional fifth at larger MLS / Group of 5 FBS properties cover the decision surface at US regional mid-market stadiums. The persona set **departs from the BC-5923 issue body directive** ("VP Bookings / VP Non-Gameday Revenue / Director of Events") — R-9 research confirmed those VP titles are enterprise-flagship-only and do not exist at MiLB / Group of 5 FBS / MLS / regional multipurpose tier. At ICP tier the dominant top title is **General Manager**, with Director of Revenue and Director of Events (or Director of Event Sales / Director of Private Events) as the operating layer. Mis-naming the title in outbound reads as enterprise-VP copy-paste and is a tell. User gate-confirmed the research-backed set at pre-compose time. Documented here for downstream readers; full reasoning in `docs/precedents/BC-5923.md`. This is the 3rd instance of the pre-compose persona gate pattern (BC-5921 hotels VP Revenue → GM, BC-5922 ski VP Village Ops → GM + DOSM, BC-5923 stadium VP Bookings → GM + Director of Revenue); elevates the pattern to architecture-grade standing recipe.

**General Manager (GM) — primary decision-maker at regional mid-market stadiums.** Owns property-level P&L, signs vendor contracts, at smaller MiLB A-level and regional multipurpose properties wears revenue and operations hats simultaneously. Cares about event-day attendance, non-sport event fill rate, ancillary F&B / parking / merch attach, compression-week revenue, year-round calendar utilization. Signs **Offer E (Stadium Production Finance)** as primary decision authority and authorizes **Offer A (Plaza Pilot Zone)** directly. Fears signing: unrecoverable capex without a defensible per-event-revenue or attendance-lift story; owner-operator or conference-office pushback on vendor spend that doesn't move non-sport event revenue in a shoulder-season window.

**Director of Revenue / Director of Revenue Operations — co-primary on financial modeling of Offer E.** Present at mid-to-larger MiLB clubs (AAA Diamond Baseball Holdings portfolio), Group of 5 FBS athletic departments, MLS clubs with dedicated revenue organizations, regional multipurpose venues with bookings P&L. Owns booking fill rate, per-cap economics, F&B / parking / merch uplift modeling. GM routes Offer E financial modeling to Director of Revenue for ancillary-math review before signing. Pitch Director of Revenue as co-primary at properties with a named revenue-org lead; skip at sub-scale properties where GM owns revenue directly.

**Director of Events / Director of Event Sales / Director of Private Events — operational influencer and coalition-builder.** The most common event-operations title at stadium ICP tier — more common than VP Bookings or VP Non-Gameday Revenue by a wide margin. Owns event booking pipeline, day-of event operations, vendor coordination, F&B and tenant coordination for non-sport events. Rarely the decision-maker but critical for surfacing Offer E internally and authorizing Offer A at pilot scope. Pitching Director of Events as sole decision-maker is the enterprise-outbound mistake; position as coalition-builder who routes upward to GM + Director of Revenue.

**Owner-Operator / Principal Owner — final sign-off at family / local-investor-owned MiLB and regional properties.** Real at Diamond Baseball Holdings portfolio (Endeavor-backed, 150+ MiLB clubs — multi-property but individual GM authority preserved at club level); family-owned MiLB clubs outside DBH; regional multipurpose venues with local-investor ownership; college FBS Group of 5 with athletic-director-as-owner-proxy authority. Cares about multi-year P&L of non-sport programming, comp-set differentiation story, press-worthy programming upside, and (especially) legacy narrative for ownership / board / donor communications. When account research surfaces family ownership, DBH portfolio membership, or single-investor structure, pitch Owner-Operator as primary with GM as implementation partner.

**[Optional] Sr Director Non-Gameday Revenue / Director of Non-Sport Events — primary operational evaluator at larger MLS and Group of 5 FBS properties with dedicated non-sport event organizations.** Exists at properties with substantial event-revenue operations: newer MLS stadiums (PayPal Park scale), Group of 5 FBS programs with active non-sport calendars (UNC Kenan hosting international soccer friendlies at $67K revenue per match; Texas A&M Kyle Field $265K Mexico-vs-Brazil friendly — [Paciolan](https://www.paciolan.com/post/offseason-playbook-turning-non-gameday-events-into-revenue-with-digital-marketing)), newer regional multipurpose venues with event-revenue maturity. At enterprise flagship this is a VP title; at ICP tier it's Senior Director or Director. Not present at smaller MiLB A-level, sub-tier Group of 5, or less-established regional properties.

**Explicitly NOT a target — per BC-5923 issue directive and confirmed by R-9 research:** **VP Facilities / Director of Facilities / VP Operations** (wrong P&L — capex maintenance and compliance, not revenue programming; target for LED-retrofit vendors, not Brite). **Pro-team front-office** (GM of the sports business, team President, Senior VP of Football/Baseball/Basketball Operations — sport-critical procurement, not non-sport event programming; target for Musco field-lighting, not Brite). **Marketing Director / Director of Brand** (brand-activation persona, not revenue-signer; can be a coalition member but never the decision-maker at ICP tier). Outbound that targets Facilities or pro-team front-office reads as vendor fundamentally mis-understanding Brite's zone — it is the fastest way to eliminate trust at a stadium prospect.

## Recency signals stadiums leak publicly

Stadiums leak signals through trade press (Sports Business Journal, Stadia Magazine, Sportico, Paciolan blog), MiLB.com announcements, athletic department press (college FBS), MLS team press, regional newspapers, chamber-of-commerce releases, capital-project board filings, and ownership-transition press. Eight recency signals matter; decay windows cross-reference [`../shelf-life-patterns.md`](../shelf-life-patterns.md).

**Ownership transitions + capital-planning windows** (decay: 12–24 months, strongest in first 6). Diamond Baseball Holdings consolidation of 150+ MiLB clubs (Endeavor-backed, ongoing through 2026) is the highest-volume stadium-ownership transition signal in the US currently. Individual MiLB clubs acquired into DBH run capital-plan refresh cycles in months 3–12 post-acquisition. Local-investor acquisitions at independent-league clubs and regional multipurpose venues leak through regional business press.

**Capital-project approvals at target-band FBS venues** (decay: 12–18 months, tier-specific). USF announced a $348.5M on-campus stadium (2027 opening — [USF](https://www.usf.edu/news/2025/trustees-approve-updated-usf-stadium-plan)) — greenfield stadium design with non-sport-event programming intent from day one. Group of 5 FBS refurb cycles (below Power 4 enterprise tier but above small-college) are the strongest college-tier Brite-ICP signal. Power 4 FBS cycles (Auburn $323M, Penn State $700M) are ceiling references — confirm ICP band before pursuing.

**New GM / Director of Revenue / Director of Events appointments** (decay: 6–12 months, strongest in first 6). MiLB.com, athletic department press releases, LinkedIn role-change alerts, Sports Business Journal executive moves. New GMs and new Directors of Revenue run audit cycles in months 3–9 of tenure; outbound landing in that window fits the audit cadence.

**Non-sport event calendar announcements** (decay: 3–6 months for new programming; evergreen + annual refresh for recurring). MiLB off-season calendars (Thanksgiving through April programming — TinCaps 388 events, Fluor Field 300+), college FBS spring event calendars, MLS shoulder-season programming (yoga classes, farmer's markets, plaza activations per [HOK MLS](https://www.hok.com/ideas/publications/how-mls-stadiums-are-revolutionizing-revenue-streams/)), regional multipurpose concert announcements, holiday programming announcements (Enchant Christmas, Magic of Lights, Skylands Light Show).

**Musco / holiday-installer contract renewals** (decay: 3–6 months, annual renewal windows). Musco Show-Light® entertainment installations and multi-year service contracts surface in MiLB and FBS press releases; holiday-installer renewals surface as year-N-return announcements (Enchant Christmas "returns to San Jose" pattern). **Signal inversion:** renewal season is the Offer-A-pilot-zone window — the property is actively evaluating incumbent vendor value.

**International soccer friendlies + concert bookings** (decay: 3–6 months). College FBS international friendlies are a high-revenue non-sport event type — UNC Kenan generated $67K on Man City vs Celtic, Texas A&M Kyle Field generated $265K on Mexico vs Brazil ([Paciolan](https://www.paciolan.com/post/offseason-playbook-turning-non-gameday-events-into-revenue-with-digital-marketing)). MLS All-Star Week fan events, summer concert tour stops at regional multipurpose venues, and amphitheater concert calendars all surface through team press and regional event-booking announcements.

**MLS expansion + Group of 5 FBS conference realignment** (decay: 6–12 months). MLS adds new clubs on a multi-year expansion cadence (Charlotte FC 2022, future expansion announcements). Group of 5 FBS conference realignment creates new stadium budget conversations (program relocations, conference reconfigurations trigger facility evaluations). Watch MLS and college athletic press for expansion announcements.

**Amphitheater + regional multipurpose concert booking announcements** (decay: 3–4 months). Concert tour routing announcements in February–April publish summer-fall stadium-footprint concert bookings. Properties with strong concert calendars are strong Offer E candidates — multi-stream sponsor + promoter + venue revenue structures are already in operating muscle memory.

## Program economics

Anchor numbers for stadium outbound work. Every number below is either public-source (cited) or inferred from BC-5923 research grounding (marked).

**Non-sport event calendar density.** Fort Wayne TinCaps host **388 annual non-baseball events** ([The Sport Journal](https://thesportjournal.org/article/ensuring-the-business-sustainability-of-minor-league-baseball-after-the-covid-global-pandemic/)) — the high-end observed benchmark. Fluor Field (Colorado Springs) plans **300+ community events for 2026** ([MiLB](https://www.milb.com/news/baseball-is-the-best-job-in-the-world)) — representative mid-tier MiLB. Median MiLB venue: **200–250 annual non-baseball events** inferred from R-9 research. College FBS off-season event programming is emerging (international friendlies + corporate rentals + weddings); MLS clubs program year-round plaza activations; regional multipurpose venues run 150–300 concert / festival / community events annually.

**Off-season revenue concentration (MiLB tier).** 60–75% of annual non-baseball revenue concentrated Oct–April at MiLB clubs (inferred from TinCaps 388 events ÷ ~180 off-season days = 2.1 events/day cluster). Off-season is the dominant revenue surface outside the baseball regular season — the inversion of the ski playbook's compression-week framing (where the regular season IS the revenue surface).

**Per-event revenue ranges.**
- Holiday light programs (Enchant Christmas, Magic of Lights, Skylands Light Show): **$35–$60 adult admission**, 6–10 week multi-week run (Nov–Jan), estimated 1K–5K daily foot traffic at venue scale.
- Concerts (summer tour stops at MLS / regional multipurpose / amphitheater): **$35–$150+ ticket** depending on artist tier; stadium-footprint concerts generate six-to-seven-figure gross-gate revenue.
- International soccer friendlies (college FBS): **$67K–$265K per match** ([UNC](https://www.unc.edu) / [Texas A&M](https://www.tamu.edu/) examples cited via [Paciolan](https://www.paciolan.com/post/offseason-playbook-turning-non-gameday-events-into-revenue-with-digital-marketing)).
- Corporate / wedding rentals (club level): **$5K–$15K per event** (estimated from suite-rental comparable data; venue-specific).
- MLS All-Star fan events: free family entry + $35+ concert tickets + sponsor-activation revenue ([MLS](https://www.mlssoccer.com/allstar/2025/news/2025-mls-all-star-week-in-austin-kicks-off)).

**Ancillary F&B per-cap.** Stadium F&B per-cap **$20–$30** on non-sport events (inferred from general stadium F&B benchmarks); MiLB context from zoos-playbook comparables suggests **$15–$25 per head** on family events and holiday programs. Parking **$10–$15 per vehicle** standard across MiLB / MLS / FBS tiers.

**Capital-cycle anchors.** Auburn Jordan-Hare $323M renovation approved (trustees-board-driven, completion 2028–2029 — [ESPN](https://www.espn.com/college-football/story/_/id/48515876/auburn-moving-forward-323m-renovation-jordan-hare-stadium)); Penn State Beaver Stadium $700M 3-phase (approved May 2024, completion 2027); USF $348.5M on-campus stadium (2027 opening — [USF](https://www.usf.edu/news/2025/trustees-approve-updated-usf-stadium-plan)). **These are enterprise ceiling references, NOT Brite ICP** — 12–24 month committee-driven procurement with board-level approval. Target-band capital decisions (MiLB refurbs, Group of 5 FBS upgrades, MLS stadium refreshes, regional multipurpose capital plans) are 6–9 month GM-authority cycles.

**Installation-cost anchors (limited public data).** No US regional stadium off-season programmable-media installation cost publicly disclosed. Closest existing precedent is **Boyne Mountain SkyBridge "Lights in the Sky"** — 200K individually-controllable LED nodes, Nov–April multi-season operation ([Boyne SkyBridge](https://www.boynemountain.com/skybridge-michigan)) — a mid-market mountain-resort programmable-media scale proof-point, not stadium-specific but the single strongest US mid-market precedent for the scale of infrastructure Brite's Offer E implies at a stadium. Cost undisclosed; inferred high-six-figure to low-seven-figure install plus annual operating cost.

**Ceiling anchors (use sparingly, always paired with mid-market).** Michigan Stadium Musco integration, Auburn Jordan-Hare 2025 Musco entertainment announcement — ceiling references for Musco Show-Light® capability, not ICP anchors. Real Madrid Bernabéu Moment Factory 2025 corridor install ([Moment Factory](https://momentfactory.com/products/realmadrid-games)) — European pro-flagship ceiling for projection-studio stadium installations.

## Good-vs-bad program taxonomy

Six dimensions distinguish compounding stadium non-sport programs from burning-out ones. Derived from BC-5923 R-9 research grounding (Paciolan / HOK MLS / MiLB economics research).

**Programmable / dynamic vs static.** Good: programmable-media infrastructure that re-programs across off-season events + holiday programming + shoulder-season activations + sponsor campaigns (Boyne SkyBridge 200K-node precedent at non-stadium mid-market scale; Musco Show-Light® entertainment at stadium scale). Bad: static turnkey holiday installer packages that operate 6–10 weeks and pack up (Enchant, Magic of Lights, Skylands model). Strongest predictor of per-season cost efficiency and multi-program amortization.

**Non-sport-revenue-generative vs non-sport-revenue-neutral.** Good: programmable-media layer that generates measurable per-event revenue — ticketed off-season events, sponsor-activation inventory, ad-revenue on plaza pixel displays, F&B tenant co-marketing campaigns that drive attach rate. Bad: aesthetic-only installations that don't surface on the booking revenue P&L — fixtures that make the venue look good but don't move event-booking pipeline, sponsor pricing, or F&B attach.

**Off-season calendar density vs single-season.** Good: Thanksgiving-through-April programming + summer concert + shoulder-season corporate events + year-round plaza activations = 200–400 annual non-sport events on reusable infrastructure (TinCaps 388-event, Fluor 300+-event band). Bad: 6–8 week holiday-only activation (Enchant-model) that sits idle 10 months of the year on vendor-owned IP the venue can't repurpose.

**Plaza / concourse / exterior activation vs playing-surface or field-rig.** Good: plaza programmable lighting, concourse sponsor-activation zones, club-level event programming, exterior gateway / porte-cochère / parking-lot entry statements — all repurposable across off-season events, holiday programs, and year-round activations. Bad: on-field sport-critical infrastructure — Brite doesn't compete with Musco on field-of-play; any outbound that frames Brite as a field-lighting vendor is mis-positioned and signals wrong-zone to the buyer.

**Compression-week amplification vs competition.** Good: programming that layers on top of existing big-event anchors (championship weeks, rivalry games, MLS Cup matchweek, MiLB playoff runs) with sponsor-activated programmable-media treatment that amplifies the day-of experience and extends attendance patterns. Bad: programming that competes for calendar slots against the venue's own sport calendar, or that dilutes marquee-event messaging with generic aesthetic layering.

**Vendor tenure with multi-year accretion vs installer-of-the-year rotation.** Good: multi-year accretive partnerships that add scope annually — Musco Show-Light® at Michigan / Auburn as a multi-year service relationship; Brite Offer E as multi-year revenue-share partnership with annual program scope expansion. Bad: turnkey installer rotations with annual RFPs, no compounding narrative, no platform lock-in, every renewal starts from scratch (fragmented regional holiday-installer pool — Enchant, Magic of Lights, Skylands, Colorado Christmas Lights all operate on annual-contract models with low switching-cost moats once initial site knowledge is earned).

## Offer candidates

Four offer structures surfaced from BC-5923 research + zoos / hotels / ski pattern adaptation. Each evaluated through Hormozi value-equation and frontend / backend lens per [`../offer-design-frameworks.md`](../offer-design-frameworks.md). Statuses in § V1 offer picks below.

### Offer A — Plaza Pilot Zone (tactical complement)

**One-line statement.** Brite installs a single-zone programmable-media demonstration at a stadium plaza / concourse / exterior gateway with an incumbent holiday or AV installer, underwriting the install cost in exchange for case-study footage, metrics, and venue-name usage rights.

**Hormozi mapping.**
- Dream Outcome: measurable proof-of-concept pilot the venue can use to justify expanding scope or displacing the incumbent at the next renewal cycle.
- Perceived Likelihood: high on narrow scope (one plaza / concourse zone, one season) — failure is bounded, venue isn't betting the whole infrastructure.
- Time Delay: low (pilot install in 4–8 weeks, results measurable within one operating month).
- Effort + Sacrifice: very low (single-zone scope, Brite handles install + programming, venue contributes site access and measurement partnership).

**Frontend deliverable.** A rendered concept board (approximately 24"×36") for a specific pilot zone at the stadium, named by location (plaza, concourse, club-level terrace, exterior gateway) and scope. Tangible artifact, not a visualization-required promise — apply the BC-5879 zoos-ledger Offer A fix #1 discipline.

**Backend commitment.** Pilot-zone install with a **stadium-native guarantee metric** — non-sport event dwell time in the pilot zone (sensor / head-count), F&B per-cap lift in pilot-adjacent concessions, post-event foot traffic in programmed hours, or attach-rate improvement on an in-pilot sponsor activation. Not photo-share metrics — those are gimmicky and disconnected from stadium revenue success (per BC-5879 zoos-ledger Offer A fix #2).

**Commercial structure.** Brite underwrites install cost in exchange for case-study usage rights — footage, metrics, venue name. Phrased confidently: "We'll underwrite the install cost. In exchange, we use the results — footage, metrics, venue name — in our sales material." (Per BC-5879 zoos-ledger Offer A fix #3.)

**When it fits.** Venue has a long-tenured incumbent holiday installer (Enchant, Magic of Lights, Skylands pattern) or a pending Musco Show-Light® entertainment renewal AND a decision-maker (typically GM or Director of Events) open to a low-commitment test but won't move on a full-venue replacement from a cold-outbound touch.

### Offer B — Permanent Plaza Backbone, Seasonal Dress (pending Brite facilities sales-motion confirmation)

**One-line statement.** Year-round architectural programmable-media infrastructure on the stadium plaza, concourse, or primary pedestrian gateway that doubles as seasonal-program entry and shoulder-season re-programmable surface — subscription-backed, Sr Director Non-Gameday Revenue-owned or Owner-Operator-owned at properties with dedicated non-sport event infrastructure.

**Hormozi mapping.**
- Dream Outcome: year-round stadium programmable infrastructure that upgrades everyday venue experience AND amortizes into holiday / shoulder-season / concert programming without a second capital cycle.
- Perceived Likelihood: moderate — the dual-use framing requires cross-department coordination (Operations + Revenue + Events + Marketing).
- Time Delay: high (capital cycle 12–18 months, installation 8–12 weeks).
- Effort + Sacrifice: moderate-high (capital commitment, cross-department coordination, permanent-infrastructure operations hosting).

**Frontend deliverable.** A stadium-infrastructure site survey + year-round programmable-media design document with seasonal overlay and shoulder-season programming already mapped.

**Backend commitment.** Subscription-model architectural install (multi-year contract) with scheduled holiday, shoulder-season, and off-season-event programming deployments included. Brite retains infrastructure ownership; venue licenses year-round use.

**When it fits.** Property has a Sr Director Non-Gameday Revenue title or meaningful owner-operator authority over dedicated non-sport event real estate (newer MLS stadiums, Group of 5 FBS with active non-sport calendars, newer regional multipurpose venues), is in a capital-planning window (often triggered by ownership transition or DBH acquisition), and the plaza / concourse / exterior gateway is ready for permanent infrastructure. **Pending Brite Facilities-VP-equivalent sales-motion confirmation** (matches zoos / ski Offer B pending pattern).

### Offer D — Superseded by E

A production-orchestration variant without the multi-stream finance layer (Brite designs and runs programming for a flat fee; venue keeps all ancillary revenue) was evaluated and folded into Offer E. Without the finance layer, the offer loses its core differentiator — near-zero venue capital commitment — and collapses into fee-for-service against Musco's service-model tier (which Brite is not competitively positioned to win on installed-fixture scale). Retained here as a named entry so future readers don't re-propose it as a separate offer.

### Offer E — Stadium Production Finance (V1 primary)

**One-line statement.** Brite orchestrates multi-stream funding — regional tourism / CVB partnership + F&B tenant co-marketing contribution + regional / national sponsor (category-specific: beverage, finance, automotive, regional utility) + ad-revenue on DMX-pixel plaza displays + (where applicable) ticket revenue-share on off-season programmed events — designs the programmable-media composition, runs the programming, and takes revenue-share on incremental non-sport event revenue and ancillary lift (F&B + parking + merch + sponsor) vs prior-year baseline. Venue contributes near-zero capital.

**Hormozi mapping (Value Equation lever: Effort + Sacrifice dominant).**
- Dream Outcome: non-sport event programming that makes the venue money (not costs it); per-event revenue lift + F&B tenant retention + calendar-fill rate improvement + ad revenue all built in. Revenue-diversification lever orthogonal to regular-season sport calendar.
- Perceived Likelihood: proven by pipeline — sponsor pledges + tourism-bureau letter + F&B tenant co-marketing commitments surfaced BEFORE install. Finance itself is a credibility signal.
- Time Delay: 6–9 months venue-commit to program-live; year-2 shoulder-season re-programming and expanded-sponsor upside.
- Effort + Sacrifice: venue near-zero capital, near-zero execution risk, operations limited to hosting and site access.

**Frontend deliverable.** A stadium-production-finance deck with a named regional tourism / CVB partnership letter of intent, at least one anchor F&B tenant (or outfield / concourse tenant) co-marketing commitment, a regional or national category sponsor pledge, and a pro-forma revenue model for Year 1 (non-sport-event lift + F&B attach + sponsor + ad-revenue line items, with a downside case stress-tested at a conservative event-calendar assumption to show base-case durability). The deck itself is the proof that Brite has done the multi-party close work, not just the design work.

**Backend commitment.** Full stadium-programming installation + season operation. Brite finances against pipeline revenue. Venue contributes site access, permits, operations hosting, F&B tenant introductions. Revenue-share on incremental non-sport-event revenue over a defined baseline (prior-year same-calendar-window event count + ancillary attach).

**Tech stack anchor.** DMX-pixel light curtains + pixel trees + architectural façade washes (sourced from S4 Lights or equivalent component supplier). Pixel-mapped content including programmable ad inventory. Static holiday installers can't carry dynamic ad inventory; Moment Factory projection installations aren't repurposable as plaza ambient infrastructure at US mid-market scale; Musco Show-Light® is capex-heavy equipment service, not revenue-share programmable-media finance; LED retrofits don't do narrative. Brite's architectural-commercial-programmable-media stack is the only archetype on the vendor landscape that supports seasonal narrative AND year-round plaza ambient AND ad-monetized dynamic content on the same infrastructure.

**Why this beats A / B / D against Musco.** Capital from Brite near-zero. Venue gets net-positive-revenue partnership, not expense line. Musco's capex-equipment + service-contract model requires venue capital commitment AND separate event-programming service fees; Brite's revenue-share + multi-stream-finance model requires neither. Brite's edge isn't Show-Light®-scale fixture portfolio; it's production-finance orchestration + ad-monetized programmable-media infrastructure integrated into existing stadium plaza / concourse / F&B / sponsor operations.

**Open risks flagged (not dealbreakers, but real).**
- Sponsor + CVB close capability: Brite must demonstrate it can close regional sponsors and CVB partnerships, not just install lights.
- CVB / DMO funding cycles are political (6–18 months); Year 1 may not have this unlock.
- Ad revenue on stadium plaza pixel installations is unproven at scale at US mid-market stadiums (jumbotron + digital signage are the closest published analogs; plaza-ambient ad inventory is novel).
- First stadium venue is hardest — trust earned through relationships and a named credibility anchor, not cold outbound alone; the outbound email carries this complexity without overwhelming.
- No confirmed Brite stadium deployment exists at time of playbook authoring — credibility anchors lean on zoos precedent (Hogle Zoo transferable economics), S4 Lights supply-chain partnership (stadium portfolio pending BC-5939), and Boyne SkyBridge as mid-market programmable-media scale proof.

**Credibility anchors.** **Boyne Mountain SkyBridge "Lights in the Sky"** — 200K individually-controllable LED nodes on a 1,203-ft suspension bridge, Nov–April multi-season ([Boyne SkyBridge](https://www.boynemountain.com/skybridge-michigan)) — is the single strongest US mid-market precedent for permanent-programmable infrastructure at stadium-relevant scale; Brite's Offer E composes the plaza-ambient-layer equivalent of Boyne SkyBridge at stadium footprint. **Zoos precedent (Hogle Zoo BC-5879 anchor)** — transferable economics on programmable-media + sponsor-finance orchestration + ad-revenue stack; the stadium economics differ (calendar shape, personas, revenue structure) but the production-finance mechanics map cleanly. **S4 Lights supply-chain partnership** — hardware supply secured; stadium-specific venue-portfolio name-drop rights pending BC-5939 customer-list retrieval. Explicit honesty: "We've built this model at zoos; here's how stadium economics differ" is the Year 1 credibility frame, not stadium-specific proof.

## Voice rules

Six voice disciplines cumulative from BC-5923 issue body + R-9 research grounding + BC-5920 / BC-5921 / BC-5922 transferable operator confirmations. All six apply to outbound email composition, account-research note-taking, and creative-angle generation consuming this playbook.

1. **Don't pitch Facilities or pro-team front-office.** The Brite zone is the off-season programmable-media layer — plaza, concourse, club-level, exterior gateway. Facilities / VP Operations are wrong-P&L (capex maintenance, not revenue programming); pro-team front-office is wrong-zone (sport-critical procurement, not non-sport event programming). Target GM primarily, Director of Revenue and Director of Events as coalition, Owner-Operator at family / local-investor-owned or DBH-portfolio properties. (Source: BC-5923 issue directive + R-9 persona research.)

2. **Don't anchor US stadium outbound on enterprise flagship brands.** NFL / MLB / NBA / NHL properties, Power 4 FBS (Alabama, Ohio State, Texas, Michigan, Penn State), Madison Square Garden, Yankee Stadium, Dodger Stadium are ceiling references only — name them paired with a specific MiLB / Group of 5 FBS / MLS / regional multipurpose ICP anchor in the same sentence, never as standalone framing. Growth + pipeline density concentrate at mid-market; the ICP is regional independents with dedicated non-sport event calendars. (Source: BC-5923 issue directive + capital-cycle research.)

3. **Don't frame as sport-programming.** Stadium non-sport programming compounds over the full non-sport calendar (holiday activations, concerts, corporate / wedding rentals, graduations, community events, international friendlies at FBS, MLS plaza activations) — a sport-programming framing signals vendor who hasn't understood stadium non-sport economics and positions Brite in Musco's zone (wrong). Use "non-sport calendar," "year-round plaza programming," or "revenue-diversification layer." (Source: BC-5923 issue directive + research on stadium non-sport revenue surface area.)

4. **Describe the anti-slop discipline in terms of what TO say, not what NOT to say.** Voice rules that enumerate banned phrases (see § Anti-slop rules for the full list) should be phrased at the discipline level — "lead with per-event revenue economics, not warm-glow consumer-marketing language" — without quoting the banned phrases themselves. The anti-slop section is the one place banned phrases appear in-text (in "don't" bullets); elsewhere the voice discipline is modeled positively. (Source: BC-5920 task-3 literal-grep-trap precedent; BC-5922 extension to URL slugs / citations.)

5. **Use operator vocabulary, not consumer / sports-media vocabulary.** Operator terms (per-cap, ancillary attach, compression-week, non-sport calendar, event fill rate, sponsor activation, DMO / CVB partnership, booking pipeline, DBH portfolio, Group of 5, mid-market independent) signal Brite has done stadium homework. Consumer / sports-media terms ("hallowed ground," "iconic venue," "the roar of the crowd," "legendary," "storied franchise," artist name-drops) signal vendor trying too hard and erode credibility the email is building. (Source: BC-5923 issue directive + R-9 voice research.)

6. **Lead with economic outcome, not aesthetic outcome.** Per-event revenue lift, F&B tenant retention, non-sport calendar fill rate, post-event plaza dwell, sponsor-attach rate improvement, compression-week premium — not "beautiful lighting," "stunning visuals," "transforming the plaza." Economic outcome is the GM / Director of Revenue / Director of Events / Owner-Operator vocabulary; aesthetic outcome is vendor-speak that signals Brite hasn't read the P&L. (Source: BC-5920 voice rule #5 generalized; R-9 confirms at stadium.)

## Anti-slop rules

Five rules with stadium-specific grounding. Rule 1 is the one place the banned consumer-marketing phrases appear in-text — the "don't" bullet format is the only section allowed to quote them (per BC-5920 task-3 literal-grep-trap precedent; BC-5922 extension). Voice rules and prose elsewhere describe the discipline without quoting.

1. **No "iconic" / "legendary" / "hallowed ground" / "storied franchise" / "the roar of the crowd" / "where legends are made" / "magical atmosphere" / "iconic venue" adjectives.** Consumer-marketing warm-glow language that zeroes out credibility on a per-event-revenue / ancillary-attach / calendar-fill buyer. If the programming matters, the numbers describe it (non-sport event count lift, F&B per-cap attach improvement, post-event dwell extension). If the numbers aren't there, adjectives don't substitute. (Source: BC-5923 issue directive + Brite-voice discipline extension.)

2. **No sport-specific fandom framing.** "The roar of the crowd," "packed house," "stadium shakes," "fans on their feet," "sea of fans," "electric atmosphere," "nothing like a home game" are consumer fandom vocabulary. Operator trade-press (Sports Business Journal, Stadia Magazine, Paciolan) does not use them in business-of-stadium contexts. A vendor using this vocabulary signals trying-too-hard; the GM / Director of Revenue reads it as pretending to be an insider. (Source: BC-5923 R-9 voice research.)

3. **No broadcast-spec or game-day sport-lighting language.** Don't reference broadcast-spec fixtures, game-day field rigs, telecast-compliance lighting, or FCC sport-lighting regulations — that's Musco's zone, not Brite's. "Ring of steel," "FOH tower," "truss and rig," "mothership" are concert-production vocabulary that also positions Brite as the wrong vendor archetype. Stadium plazas and concourses aren't concert-rig venues and aren't sport-lighting installations; either framing collapses credibility. (Source: BC-5923 issue directive; BC-5922 anti-slop #3 concert-rig transfers; Musco primary-competitor zone-separation.)

4. **No generic "ROI uplift" without a named stadium-specific metric.** Stadium economics are specific: per-event revenue, non-sport calendar fill rate, F&B attach rate, parking attach, sponsor-activation pricing, post-event plaza dwell, event-day foot-traffic lift, DBH portfolio comp-set benchmarks. Generic "ROI" or "returns" phrasing signals Brite hasn't done stadium-vertical homework. Use the specific metric the GM or Director of Revenue tracks on the non-sport P&L. (Source: BC-5923 R-9 + BC-5920 anti-slop #4 generalization.)

5. **Lead with per-event revenue and calendar-fill economics; destination-branding / press-worthiness lives in paragraph 2+, not the hook.** Destination-branding, venue-differentiation narrative, and press-worthy legacy are real and owner-operator-relevant, but opening cold outbound with destination-branding narrative reads as agency-speak to a GM measured on non-sport event calendar fill on the Monday after Thanksgiving or MLS Cup. Open with the economic outcome (per-event revenue lift, F&B attach, sponsor-inventory expansion, calendar-fill rate) and introduce destination-branding as a supporting beat once the economic frame has landed. (Source: BC-5920 anti-slop #5 — transfers to stadium.)
